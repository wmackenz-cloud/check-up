What this lesson is for

Why this matters: when something goes wrong, you must explain what happened, why, and what to do next. Clear language helps your team find the real cause and agree on a solution quickly.

You will know

  • words for faults: crack, leak, corrosion, wear, fatigue, deflection, misalignment, settlement
  • cause and effect phrases: due to, caused by, led to, resulted in
  • phrases to recommend: I recommend + -ing, we should, it would be better to

You will understand

  • which phrases come before the cause and which come before the result
  • how must be, might be and can't be show how sure you are

You will be able to

  • report a problem: what happened, the cause, the effect and the solution
  • say how sure you are about the cause
  • recommend a solution in a meeting or an email

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I recommend to check the alignment. I recommend checking the alignment.
  • I suggest you to stop the line. I suggest (that) we stop the line.
  • The pump stopped because of the seal failed. The pump stopped because the seal failed. / ... because of a failed seal.
  • It mustn't be the motor. We replaced it last week. It can't be the motor. (mustn't means it is not allowed)
  • a crack on the weld a crack in the weld
  • damage of the motor damage to the motor

Warm-up

  • Think of a problem at work: a machine that stopped, a leak, a crack, a delay. What happened?
  • When something goes wrong, who do you report it to? Do you usually call, write an email or talk in a meeting?
  • Is it easy for you to say “I think we should…” to a manager in English?

Key vocabulary

WordMeaningExample
a fault / a defectsomething wrong with a machine, part or structureThe technician found a fault in the control panel.
a crack (to crack)a thin line where a material has broken, but not into separate piecesThere is a crack in the weld next to the bracket.
a leak (to leak)liquid or gas that escapes through a hole or a bad sealOil is leaking from the gearbox seal.
corrosion (to corrode, corroded)damage to metal by a chemical reaction, for example rust on steelRoad salt caused corrosion of the reinforcing steel.
wear (worn)slow loss of material when two surfaces rub togetherThe conveyor belt is badly worn.
fatiguecracking caused by a load that is repeated many times, even when each load is smallThe bracket failed due to fatigue after years of vibration.
deflection (to deflect)how far a beam or slab bends or moves under a loadThe deflection at mid-span was 18 mm.
vibration (to vibrate)a fast shaking movementVibration in the pump increased after the repair.
overheating (to overheat)becoming hotter than is normal or safeThe motor is overheating because the cooling fan is blocked.
misalignment (misaligned)when two parts that should be in a straight line are notMisalignment between the motor and the pump damaged the coupling.
settlement (to settle)when a foundation moves down because the ground under it compressesSettlement of the footing caused cracks in the wall.
a failure (to fail)when a part or structure stops doing its jobA bearing failure stopped the line for six hours.
the root causethe first, main reason for a problemA new bearing is not enough: we need to find the root cause.

Exercise 2: Cause or result?

Cause = the reason. Result (or effect) = what happens because of it.

  • RESULT + due to / because of / caused by + CAUSE: The line stopped due to a bearing failure.
  • CAUSE + led to / resulted in / caused + RESULT: Poor lubrication led to overheating.
  • because + subject + verb: The pump stopped because the seal failed. But because of + noun: because of a failed seal.

Exercise 3: Must be, might be, can't be

When we look for the cause of a problem, we use modal verbs to show how sure we are.

  • must be = I am almost sure it is true (strong evidence): The bearing is very hot and noisy. It must be damaged.
  • might be / could be / may be = it is possible: It might be the seal, or it could be the valve.
  • can't be = I am almost sure it is not true: We replaced the seal yesterday. It can't be the seal.
  • Watch out: mustn't means “it is not allowed”. It is not the opposite of must be here. Say can't be.

Read the evidence. How sure is the engineer? Tap must, might or can't.

  1. The motor is very hot, and the cooling fan isn't turning. The fan be the problem.
  2. There is oil under the gearbox, but we replaced the seal yesterday and it is completely dry. The leak be from the seal.
  3. There is a noise from the conveyor, but I'm not sure where. It be a worn roller, or a loose guard.
  4. The sensor shows 300 °C, but I can touch the bearing housing with my hand. The sensor be wrong.
  5. The conveyor stopped at 10:15, but the power cut started at 11:00. The power cut be the cause.
  6. The vibration started after the new motor was installed. It be misalignment, but we need to check it with a laser tool.
  7. The footing has moved down 25 mm since last year, and the cracks are right above it. The cracks be due to settlement.

Exercise 4: Reading

Read the email from a maintenance engineer to his manager.

From: Tomasz Nowak
To: Sandra Lee
Subject: Pump P-3: vibration problem and recommendation

Hi Sandra,

Here is a short update on Pump P-3 at the Etobicoke plant.

On Monday the operators reported high vibration and a loud noise from the pump. When we checked it, the vibration level was 9.2 mm/s, which is about three times the normal level. The bearing at the motor end was also overheating (85 °C), and there was a small oil leak from the bearing housing.

The impeller can't be the cause, because we inspected it last month and it was in good condition. We think the root cause must be misalignment between the motor and the pump. The motor was replaced two weeks ago, and the problem started soon after that. Misalignment puts extra load on the bearing, and this led to the overheating and the leak.

I recommend shutting the pump down on Thursday and realigning the shafts with a laser alignment tool. We should also replace the bearing and the seal, because they are probably damaged. It would be better to do this now than to wait for a complete bearing failure.

Best regards,
Tomasz Nowak
Maintenance Engineer

Exercise 5: Speaking

Reporting a problem in a meeting

  • What: I'd like to report a problem with… / We've noticed that… / There's a crack in…
  • Cause: It looks like it might be… / It must be due to… / It can't be…, because…
  • Effect: This has led to… / As a result, …
  • Solution: I recommend replacing… / We should… / It would be better to…

Think about each question. We will talk about them in our lesson.

  1. Describe a real problem you had with a machine, a structure or a project. What happened, and what was the cause?
  2. Role play: you are in a weekly team meeting. Report a problem to your manager in four steps: what, cause, effect, solution. (Ideas: a leaking valve, a noisy gearbox, cracks in a parking garage ramp, a beam that deflects too much.)
  3. Job interview: “Tell me about a technical problem you solved.” Say what the problem was, how you found the cause, what you did, and the result.
  4. Challenge: when a machine stops, the production manager wants it running again in one hour. The engineer wants to stop longer and find the root cause. Who is right? What would you say in the meeting?

Exercise 6: Recommending a solution

Watch out

  • I recommend + -ing: I recommend replacing the seal. Not I recommend to replace.
  • I recommend / suggest (that) we + verb: I recommend that we replace the seal. Not I suggest you to replace.
  • We should + verb (no to, no -ing): We should check the alignment.
  • It would be better to + verb: It would be better to stop the line now.

Exercise 7: Prepositions and word forms

Part A: Prepositions

Watch out

  • a crack in, a leak in / from, an increase in: a crack in the weld, oil leaking from the gearbox
