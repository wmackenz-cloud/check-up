What this lesson is for

Why this matters: choosing the right material is an everyday engineering decision. You need to explain properties and compare options clearly, so your team and your client can agree on the best choice.

You will know

  • materials: steel, concrete, timber, aluminium, cast iron, composites
  • properties: strong, stiff, ductile, brittle, durable, corrosion-resistant
  • word families: strength, strong, strengthen

You will understand

  • how a noun, an adjective and a verb in one word family are used
  • how to compare: lighter than, twice as strong as, not as stiff as

You will be able to

  • describe the strengths and weaknesses of a material
  • compare two or three materials for one job
  • recommend a material and give your reasons

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Timber is more cheap than steel. Timber is cheaper than steel.
  • Steel is stronger from timber. Steel is stronger than timber.
  • It is three times as strong than the old beam. It is three times as strong as the old beam.
  • We need to strong the frame. We need to strengthen the frame. (strong is the adjective; strengthen is the verb)
  • resistant at corrosion resistant to corrosion
  • the most light option the lightest option

Warm-up

  • Which materials do you work with most: steel, concrete, timber, aluminium, plastics, composites?
  • Think of a bicycle frame. Why are some frames made of aluminium or carbon fibre, not steel?
  • Toronto winters are cold and the roads are salted. Which materials have problems with that?

Key vocabulary

Materials

MaterialNotesExample
steelstrong and ductile; rusts if it is not protected (paint, galvanizing)The frame is made of structural steel.
concretestrong in compression (pushing), weak in tension (pulling)The footings are made of concrete.
reinforced concreteconcrete with steel bars (rebar) inside; the steel carries the tensionThe slabs are reinforced concrete.
timber (wood for building)light and renewable; must be kept dryThe roof trusses are made of timber.
aluminium (Canada and US: aluminum)about a third of the weight of steel; corrosion-resistant; not as stiff as steelThe ladder is made of aluminium.
cast ironeasy to cast into shapes; strong in compression, but brittleThe old pump housing is cast iron.
compositestwo materials together, for example glass or carbon fibre in a plastic resinCarbon-fibre composites are used to strengthen old bridges.

Comparing materials

  • -er than / more ... than: Aluminium is lighter than steel. Stainless steel is more corrosion-resistant than carbon steel.
  • much / a lot / slightly + -er: Steel is much stiffer than timber.
  • twice / three times as ... as: Steel is about three times as stiff as aluminium.
  • not as ... as: Timber is not as strong as steel.
  • the -est / the most: The composite deck is the lightest option.

Say it right: aluMINium (Canada and US: aLUminum), DUrable, duraBILity, STRENGTH (one syllable), comPOSite (Canada and US; UK: COMposite), DUCtile.

Priya

Exercise 7: Prepositions and mistakes

Part A: Prepositions

Watch out

  • resistant to / resistance to: resistant to corrosion, resistance to heat
  • strong in / weak in + compression, tension, bending
  • made of + material; protected from + water, fire, salt
  • suitable for; the difference between A and B
