Teacher Notes 4: Processes, Procedures and Safety

Open the student page

Purpose: the student describes a process step by step, uses the passive for processes and reports, gives clear instructions and safety rules with the right modals, and uses common inspection and maintenance verbs.

Level: B1 to B2. Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Before the lesson: ask the student to think of one job they know well (servicing a pump, pouring a slab, installing a bracket) and, if possible, to bring a short procedure, checklist or method statement from work.

Why this lesson matters

  • Engineers describe processes and write method statements, inspection reports and work instructions all the time. These texts use the passive, sequencing words and modals much more than everyday English does.
  • On site, a wrong modal is a safety problem. You don't have to and you must not mean completely different things, and many students mix them up because of their first language.
  • Job interviews often include “Walk me through how you would…” questions: a process answer with clear sequencing sounds organised and confident.

50-minute lesson plan

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The warm-up questions. The student tells you the steps of one job; note how they sequence it.
  2. Maintenance verbs (8 min). Key vocabulary, then Exercise 1. Ask: “Which of these did you do last month?” (past simple active, a bridge to the passive).
  3. The passive for processes (12 min). Exercise 2. Then the student retells the slab process with the worksheet closed. Mechanical students can then describe a process of their own (a part being machined, a pump being overhauled).
  4. Safety and modals (10 min). Exercise 3 (sorter), then the must / must not / don't have to box and Exercise 7 Part A. Ask for the safety rules on their own site.
  5. Procedure and reading (10 min). Exercise 4 (the belt procedure) and Exercise 5 (the site report). Ask: “What would you do next if you were Priya?”
  6. Wrap-up (5 min). One speaking question from Exercise 6, then set the homework, the writing task in Exercise 5 and Exercise 7 Parts B and C.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • procedure: pro-CE-dure /prəˈsiːdʒər/. maintenance: MAIN-te-nance /ˈmeɪntənəns/, three syllables, not “main-TAIN-ance”. Compare the verb main-TAIN.
  • lubricate: LU-bri-cate. inspect, install, adjust, replace, remove all have stress on the second syllable.
  • -ed endings in the passive: tightened /d/, poured /d/, installed /d/; inspected, tested, adjusted /ɪd/; checked, fixed, replaced /t/. Students often add an extra syllable (“check-ed”).
  • must not / mustn't: the first t in mustn't is silent: /ˈmʌsənt/. In safety instructions, the full must not is clearer and more common in writing.
  • isolate: I-so-late, first syllable /aɪ/.

False friends and common errors

  • must not vs don't have to. German muss nicht means don't have to, so German speakers often say you must not when they mean it's not necessary. Spanish and Italian speakers can mix them too (no tienes que / non devi). Drill with real site rules.
  • control (from controllare, contrôler, kontrollieren) is often used for check or inspect. In English, control usually means manage or operate: you check the oil level, you don't control it.
  • armature (Italian armatura, French armature, Russian armatura) means reinforcement or rebar in concrete. In English an armature is part of an electric motor.
  • montage / mount: for machines, English usually says installation / assembly and install / assemble.
  • concrete vs cement. Cement is one ingredient of concrete. You pour concrete, not cement.
  • formwork (also forms in North America, shuttering in the UK). Curing = keeping new concrete moist and at a suitable temperature so it gains strength; the required time depends on the specification.
  • must + to (must to wear) and after + base verb (after check) are the most common grammar errors here.

Accuracy notes if a student asks: in Ontario, a trench deeper than 1.2 m needs sloping, shoring or a trench box. Each worker on a lockout puts on his or her own lock. Belt tension is adjusted (by moving the motor) before the motor bolts are tightened. Torque is measured in newton metres (N·m).

Answers

  • Exercise 1, Maintenance verbs: lubricate the bearings with grease; tighten the anchor bolts to the correct torque; replace the worn V-belt with a new one; adjust the valve until the pressure is correct; inspect the welds for cracks; isolate the electrical supply before work starts; cure the new concrete for seven days; install a new handrail on the stairs.
  • Exercise 2, How a concrete slab is built: 1 is built, 2 is placed, 3 is inspected, 4 is poured, 5 is vibrated, 6 is kept, 7 was tested, 8 were tightened.
  • Exercise 3, Safety rules: Must (or should): wear a hard hat in the work area, lock out the power before you clean the conveyor, report a near miss to your supervisor, wear safety glasses when you grind metal, check the sling before every lift. Must not: stand under a suspended load, remove a machine guard while the machine is running, use a ladder with a broken step, enter a deep trench with no shoring or trench box, wear loose clothing near a rotating shaft.
  • Exercise 4, Replacing a conveyor belt: 1 First, 2 Once, 3 After, 4 Then, 5 before, 6 until, 7 Finally. The “Your turn” sentences are checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 5, Reading: 1 jackhammers, 2 corrosion, 3 14:00 (2 p.m.), 4 must, 5 seven. The instructions writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 7, Part A: 1 must not, 2 don't have to, 3 must, 4 should, 5 to lock out, 6 starting, 7 was installed, 8 are lubricated.
  • Exercise 7, Part B: 1 You must wear a hard hat on site. 2 The bolts were tightened yesterday. 3 The concrete is poured into the formwork. 4 No mistake. 5 The beam was tested in the lab last week. 6 After checking the oil level, close the cover. 7 No mistake. 8 You must not smoke near the fuel tank.
  • Exercise 7, Part C: 1 The concrete is poured and then vibrated. 2 You must not remove the guard while the machine is running. 3 Once the bolts are tight, refit the guard. 4 All the welds were inspected for cracks. 5 Before starting the pump, check the oil level. Also fine: Check the oil level before starting the pump.

Speaking prompts

  • Walk me through how you would replace a worn part on a machine you know. Start with safety.
  • Explain how something is made in your company, or how a building is built, from start to finish. Use the passive.
  • What PPE do you need on your site? Which rules do people break most often, and why?
  • Give me three rules for a visitor to your workplace: one with must, one with must not, one with don't have to.
  • Interview question: “Tell me about a time you stopped work because it was unsafe.”
  • B2: Who is responsible for safety on a site: the company, the supervisor or each worker? Explain your view.
