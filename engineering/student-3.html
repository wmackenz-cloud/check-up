What this lesson is for

Why this matters: engineers share numbers all day: sizes, loads, tolerances and test results. If you say them clearly and correctly, nobody is confused, and a small language mistake does not become a big technical one.

You will know

  • words for dimensions: length, width, height, depth, thickness, diameter, span
  • how to say numbers and units: 2.5, ¼, 10³, ± 0.5 mm, kN, MPa
  • words for approximate figures and trends: approximately, just under, rose sharply, levelled off, peaked at

You will understand

  • why English uses a point for decimals and a comma for thousands
  • the difference between a rise in temperature, a rise of 5 °C and rose by 5 °C

You will be able to

  • give the main dimensions of a part or structure
  • say numbers with units aloud, correctly
  • describe a test result or a graph in a short email

Common mistakes to watch for

  • two comma five metres two point five metres (2.5 m)
  • a 9-metres span a 9-metre span (no -s before a noun), but the span is 9 metres
  • The plate has 12 mm of thickness. The plate is 12 mm thick.
  • The pressure rose of 50 kPa. The pressure rose by 50 kPa.
  • The load raised after lunch. The load rose after lunch. (raise needs an object)
  • plus and minus 0.2 mm plus or minus 0.2 mm

Key vocabulary

Word or phraseMeaningExample
length / longthe distance from one end to the otherThe conveyor is 24 m long. = It has a length of 24 m.
width / widethe distance from one side to the otherThe belt is 800 mm wide.
height / highthe distance from the bottom to the topEach column has a height of 3.6 m.
depth / deepthe distance from the top downThe footing is 600 mm deep.
thickness / thickthe distance from one surface to the otherThe slab is 250 mm thick.
diameter / radiusthe distance across a circle through the centre / half the diameterThe shaft has a diameter of 50 mm, so its radius is 25 mm.
spanthe distance between two supports of a beam or bridgeThe beam spans 9 m between the columns.
loadthe force or weight that a structure or part carries (in kN)Each column carries a load of about 1,200 kN.
stressthe force on each unit of area inside a material (in MPa)The maximum stress in the bracket is 140 MPa.
tension / compressiona pulling force (the part gets longer) / a pushing or squeezing force (the part gets shorter)A cable is in tension. A column is in compression.
tolerancehow much a size can be different from the exact size and still be OKThe hole is 20 mm with a tolerance of ± 0.1 mm.
approximately / roughly / aboutclose to this number, but not exactThe pump weighs approximately 350 kg.
just under / just overa little less / a little more thanThe test result was just under 30 MPa.
rise, increase / fall, drop, decreasego up / go down (verbs and nouns)The temperature rose from 20 °C to 60 °C. There was a drop in pressure.
level off / peak atstop going up or down and stay the same / reach the highest pointVibration peaked at 3.1 mm/s and then fell. The temperature levelled off at 68 °C.
sharply / steadily / slightlyfast and by a lot / at the same speed / by a small amountStrength rose sharply in the first week.

Exercise 7: Prepositions and numbers

Watch out

  • a rise / an increase / a fall / a drop in + the thing: a rise in temperature.
  • a rise of + the amount: a rise of 5 °C.
  • rose / fell / increased by + the size of the change: fell by 10%.
  • from 20 to 60; peaked at 72 °C; levelled off at 68 °C.
  • rise – rose – risen has no object (the price rose). raise needs an object (they raised the price).
  • a 6-metre beam (before a noun: no -s) but the beam is 6 metres long.
