The rule

Many verbs, adjectives and nouns are always followed by the same preposition.

There is often no logical reason, so learn the word + preposition together: depend on, interested in, afraid of.

After a preposition, use a noun or the -ing form: I'm interested in learning Spanish.

Common combinations

Verb + preposition Adjective + preposition Noun + preposition
depend on interested in a reason for
listen to good at / bad at a solution to
wait for afraid of an increase in
pay for proud of a problem with
apply for worried about the cause of
succeed in responsible for an advantage of
insist on similar to a demand for
belong to different from an effect on
consist of married to a fall in
agree with (a person) famous for an example of

Watch out

  • It depends of the weather. It depends on the weather.
  • I'm married with a teacher. I'm married to a teacher.
  • She is good in maths. She is good at maths.
  • I'm interested to learn French. I'm interested in learning French.
  • We discussed about the problem. We discussed the problem. (No preposition after discuss.)
