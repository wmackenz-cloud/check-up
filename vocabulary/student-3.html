The rule

A phrasal verb is a verb + a small word (up, out, off, on...). Together they have a new meaning.

give = hand something to someone. give up = stop doing something.

Learn each phrasal verb as one new word, with an example sentence.

Some can be separated: turn the music down or turn down the music. With a pronoun, always separate: turn it down (not turn down it).

Fifteen useful phrasal verbs

Phrasal verb Meaning Example
look after take care of I look after my sister's children.
look for try to find I'm looking for my keys.
look forward to be happy about something in the future I'm looking forward to the holiday.
give up stop doing He gave up smoking.
find out discover information I found out the truth.
turn down refuse; make quieter She turned down the job offer.
put off postpone, do later We put off the meeting until Friday.
get on with have a good relationship with I get on well with my boss.
run out of have no more We've run out of milk.
take up start a new hobby I took up yoga last year.
set up start a business or organisation They set up a company in 2020.
come across find by chance I came across an old photo.
carry on continue Carry on, I'm listening.
bring up raise a child; mention a topic She was brought up in Hanoi.
call off cancel They called off the match because of the rain.

Watch out

  • look forward to is followed by a noun or -ing: I look forward to hearing from you. (not to hear)
  • Can you turn down it? Can you turn it down?
  • I gave up to smoke. I gave up smoking.
