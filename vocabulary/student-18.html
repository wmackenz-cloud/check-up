What this lesson is for

Why this matters: some phrases start with a preposition, and the whole phrase never changes: on purpose, in advance, out of order. The preposition is often different from your language, so you need to learn them as blocks.

You will know

  • 21 fixed phrases with by, on, in, at, out of, for and under
  • three pairs to keep apart: on time / in time, for sale / on sale, by mistake / by accident

You will understand

  • why you learn each phrase as a block, like one long word
  • why translating the preposition from your language often goes wrong

You will be able to

  • use these phrases to talk about work, shopping and everyday life
  • choose the right phrase in a story

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I did it by purpose. I did it on purpose.
  • I took your pen with mistake. I took your pen by mistake.
  • He's in the phone. He's on the phone.
  • She's in charge for the team. She's in charge of the team.
  • Book your tickets on advance. Book your tickets in advance.

Before you start

In Words and Prepositions, you learnt that many words choose their own preposition: good at, afraid of, interested in. This lesson is the next step.

Fixed phrases start with the preposition, and the whole phrase never changes: on purpose, in advance, out of order.

Learn each one as a block, like one long word. Don't translate the preposition from your language: it is often different. by mistake, not with mistake; on the phone, not in the phone.

In Prepositional Phrases 2 you will learn nouns and verbs with their own preposition: a rise in, rely on, deal with.

The fixed phrases

PhraseMeaningExample
by
by mistakebecause of an error: you did the wrong thingI took your umbrella by mistake. Sorry!
by accidentnot planned; it just happenedI deleted the photos by accident.
by chancewithout planning, often luckyWe met by chance at the airport.
by handwithout a machineThis sweater was made by hand.
on
on purposeyou planned it; you wanted to do it (the opposite of by mistake)He didn't break the cup on purpose.
on the phonetalking on the phoneSorry, she's on the phone right now.
on averageusually; as a typical numberOn average, I work 40 hours a week.
on saleat a lower price than usualWinter coats are on sale: 30% off!
on timeat the planned time, not lateThe train left on time.
in
in charge ofresponsible for; the boss ofWho's in charge of the budget?
in advancebefore the time; earlyBook your tickets in advance.
in personface to face, not online or by phoneI'd like to meet the landlord in person.
in touchin contactLet's keep in touch. I'll be in touch next week.
in generalusually; about most people or thingsIn general, people here are very friendly.
in timeearly enough, before it is too lateWe got to the station just in time.
at
at riskin danger200 jobs are at risk if the factory closes.
at leastnot less than; or: one good thing in a bad situationIt takes at least an hour. It rained, but at least it was warm.
out of, for, under
out of ordernot working (a machine, often in a public place)The lift is out of order again.
out of datetoo old; not valid or not modern any moreMy passport is out of date.
for salethe owner wants to sell itTheir house is for sale.
under pressurestressed because you must do a lot, or do it fastI work well under pressure.

Three pairs to keep apart

on time = not late: The meeting started on time, at 9:00.   in time = early enough: I got home just in time to see the match. They are tricky twins, like the ones in Tricky Twins: one small word changes the meaning.

for sale = someone wants to sell it: Our car is for sale.   on sale = cheaper than usual: Shoes are on sale this week. (On sale can also mean “in the shops”: Tickets go on sale on Monday.)

by mistake and by accident both mean not on purpose. Use by mistake when you did the wrong thing (the wrong bus, the wrong file). Use by accident when something just happened. On purpose is the opposite of both.

Speaking

  • Phrase challenge: tell your teacher or a friend about your last week at work or at home. Use at least four phrases from this lesson. Your partner counts them! On Monday I was under pressure, because the printer was out of order again. At least my boss was in a good mood…
  • Have you ever done something embarrassing by mistake? What happened?
  • What are you in charge of at work or at home?
  • Do you work well under pressure, or do you like to plan everything in advance?
  • Do you prefer to meet people in person or talk on the phone? Why?
  • Are you usually on time? What do you do if you're late?
  • Do you buy things when they're on sale? What was your best bargain?
