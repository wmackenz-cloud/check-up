Before you start

In Lesson 5, Words and Prepositions, you learnt that many words choose their own preposition: good at, afraid of, interested in. This lesson is the next step.

1. Fixed phrases start with the preposition, and the whole phrase never changes: on purpose, in advance, out of order.

2. More nouns and verbs with their own preposition, very common at work and in the news: a rise in prices, access to the files, rely on someone, deal with a problem.

Learn each one as a block, like one long word. Don't translate the preposition from your language: it is often different. by mistake, not with mistake; on the phone, not in the phone.

And remember from Lesson 5: after a preposition, use a noun or the -ing form: I apologised for being late.

Three pairs to keep apart

on time = not late: The meeting started on time, at 9:00.   in time = early enough: I got home just in time to see the match. They are tricky twins, like the ones in Lesson 15, Tricky Twins: one small word changes the meaning.

for sale = someone wants to sell it: Our car is for sale.   on sale = cheaper than usual: Shoes are on sale this week. (On sale can also mean “in the shops”: Tickets go on sale on Monday.)

by mistake and by accident both mean not on purpose. Use by mistake when you did the wrong thing (the wrong bus, the wrong file). Use by accident when something just happened. On purpose is the opposite of both.

Group 2: Noun + preposition

Noun + prepositionMeaningExample
a rise / an increase / a fall in (Lesson 5)the thing that goes up or downThere's been a rise in rents.
a reason for (Lesson 5)why something happensWhat's the reason for the delay?
the cause ofwhat made something happenThe cause of the fire is not known.
an effect ona change that something makesStress has an effect on your sleep.
damage toharm to a thingThe storm did a lot of damage to the roof.
a demand forpeople want or need itThere's a big demand for nurses.
a difference betweenhow two things are not the sameWhat's the difference between these two phones?
access tothe right or the way to use or enter somethingAll staff have access to the gym.

in or of? Use in for the thing that changes, and of for the amount: a rise in rents, a rise of 6%. Both together: a rise of 6% in rents.

Group 3: Verb + preposition

Verb + prepositionMeaningExample
rely onneed and trust someone or somethingI rely on my car to get to work.
depend on (Lesson 5)change because of something; or: needIt depends on the weather.
deal withtake action to solve or handle somethingI deal with customer complaints.
focus ongive all your attention toLet's focus on the main problem.
apologise (to someone) forsay sorryI apologised to my boss for being late.
approve ofthink something is good or rightMy parents didn't approve of my first boyfriend.
consist of (Lesson 5)be made of these partsThe course consists of ten lessons.

Person first, then the reason: apologise to someone for something.   approve with no preposition means “say yes officially”: My manager approved my holiday. approve of is your opinion: I don't approve of smoking.

Watch out: common mistakes

  • It depends of the price. It depends on the price.
  • We discussed about the plan. We discussed the plan. (No preposition after discuss.)
  • Can you explain me the rules? Can you explain the rules to me? / Can you explain to me how it works?
  • She apologised me. She apologised to me.
  • an increase of prices an increase in prices (but an increase of 10%)
  • I did it by purpose. I did it on purpose.
  • He's in the phone. He's on the phone.
  • She's in charge for the team. She's in charge of the team.
  • the difference of A and B the difference between A and B

Speaking

  • Phrase challenge: tell your teacher or a friend about your last week at work or at home. Use at least four phrases from this lesson. Your partner counts them! On Monday I was under pressure, because the printer was out of order again. At least my boss was in a good mood…
  • Have you ever done something embarrassing by mistake? What happened?
  • What are you in charge of at work or at home?
  • Do you work well under pressure, or do you like to plan everything in advance?
  • Who do you rely on when you have a problem? Who relies on you?
  • Do you prefer to meet people in person or talk on the phone? Why?
  • What's the biggest difference between life in your country and life here?
  • Has there been a rise in prices where you live? What has gone up the most?
