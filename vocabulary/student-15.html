Before you start

Some adverbs have a twin with -ly. They look almost the same, but the meaning is different!

She works hard. (with a lot of effort)   →   She hardly works. (almost not!)

Remember from Vocabulary 12 and 13: some adverbs never change. fast, hard, late, early, straight, far are adjectives and adverbs: He drives fast. Go straight on. We don't live far away. fastly, straightly

Today we look at the twins: the word without -ly keeps its normal meaning, but the -ly word means something new.

The rule: ten tricky twins

Without -lyWith -ly: a new meaning
hard with a lot of effortShe works hard.hardly almost notI can hardly hear you.
late not on timeThe bus came late.lately recentlyI've been tired lately.
near close, not farChristmas is getting near.nearly almostIt's nearly midnight.
high a long way upThe birds flew high.highly very, very muchThis book is highly recommended.
most more than anything elseI like summer most.mostly mainlyThe guests were mostly students.
close nearCome and sit close to me.closely carefully, in detailWatch closely.
free at no costChildren travel free.freely without limits, openlyYou can speak freely here.
short before the endOur holiday was cut short.shortly soonThe doctor will see you shortly.
deep a long way down or inWe walked deep into the forest.deeply very much (feelings)I'm deeply sorry.
pretty quite (before an adjective)It's pretty cold today.prettily in a pretty wayThe table was prettily decorated.

  • nearly = almost
  • highly = very, very much
  • mostly = mainly
  • closely = carefully, in detail

Exercise 7: Speaking: Twin questions

Ask and answer with your teacher or a friend. Use the same twin in your answer, and say a little more.

  • Have you been busy lately? What have you been doing?
  • Do you work hard? What do you work hardest at?
  • Is there anything you can hardly do, but would like to do well?
  • Do you ever arrive late? What happens when you do?
  • What have you nearly forgotten to do this week?
  • Which film, book or restaurant can you highly recommend?
  • Do you live near the shops? What can you walk to?
  • Is there anything that is still free in your town? Where can you speak freely?
  • Twin challenge: say one sentence with both twins: I've been working hard, so I've hardly seen my family lately.
