Before you start

Some words always come in pairs, joined by and (sometimes or). They are called binomials, or fixed pairs.

After a busy week, I just want some peace and quiet.   We got home safe and sound.

The order never changes. peace and quiet, not quiet and peace. ups and downs, not downs and ups. sooner or later, not later or sooner. The other order sounds wrong to a fluent speaker, even when the meaning is clear.

Why do people use them? They sound natural, friendly and a little colourful. Many pairs put two words with almost the same meaning together, just for emphasis: peace and quiet, sick and tired, first and foremost. It's the same trick as in our lesson Say It Twice (synonyms for emphasis): say it twice, and it sounds stronger. Other pairs join opposites: ups and downs, pros and cons, give or take.

Fixed pairMeaningExample
Two words, one idea (for emphasis)
peace and quietcalm, with no noise or stressI go to the park for some peace and quiet.
safe and soundsafe and not hurtThe children got home safe and sound.
sick and tired (of)very annoyed and bored with somethingI'm sick and tired of this rain.
first and foremostmost importantly, before anything elseFirst and foremost, thank you all for coming.
rules and regulationsall the official rulesThere are so many rules and regulations at the airport.
bits and piecessmall things of different kindsI keep bits and pieces in this drawer.
tried and testedused many times and known to work wellThis is a tried and tested recipe.
pick and choosechoose only the things you like bestYou can't pick and choose which rules to follow.
Opposites together
ups and downsgood times and bad timesEvery marriage has its ups and downs.
pros and consadvantages and disadvantagesLet's think about the pros and cons first.
back and forthfrom one place to another and back again, many timesHe walked back and forth, waiting for the call.
more or lessalmost; aboutI've more or less finished the book.
sooner or laterone day in the future; it will certainly happenSooner or later, you'll have to tell her.
give or takeabout, approximately (after a number)It takes an hour, give or take ten minutes.
More useful pairs
short and sweetshort and pleasant, not long or boringLet's keep this meeting short and sweet.
loud and clearvery clearly, easy to hear or understandI hear you loud and clear.
odds and endssmall things of different kinds, often not important (like bits and pieces)The box was full of odds and ends.
by and largein general, mostlyBy and large, the trip was a success.
(every) now and thensometimes, but not oftenI eat cake every now and then.
few and far betweenrare; there are not manyGood jobs are few and far between here.
wait and seewait to find out what happens before you decideWill it rain tomorrow? We'll have to wait and see.

  • by and large = in general, mostly
  • now and then = sometimes, but not often
  • give or take = about, approximately
  • odds and ends = small things of different kinds
  • sooner or later = one day in the future, certainly
  • few and far between = rare, not many
  • wait and see = find out what happens before you decide

Exercise 9: Your sentences

Write one sentence about your own life with each pair. Press Check after each one.

  1. sick and tired of: something that annoys you (I'm sick and tired of my neighbour's dog barking at night.)
  2. peace and quiet: where or when you find it
  3. ups and downs: your job, your town, or learning English
  4. pros and cons: something you are trying to decide
  5. now and then: something you do sometimes, but not often
  6. sooner or later: something that will happen one day
