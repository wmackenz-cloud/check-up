What this lesson is for

Why this matters: very, so and too all make a word stronger, but they don't mean the same. If you say too when you mean very, people may think there is a problem.

You will know

  • the meaning and pattern of very, so ... that and too ... to
  • when to use such a, too much, too many and enough

You will understand

  • that too always means more than is OK, so there is a problem
  • why enough goes after an adjective but before a noun

You will be able to

  • choose very, so or too in a sentence
  • join two sentences with too ... to and so ... that
  • answer questions quickly using these words

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Your English is too good! Your English is very good! (Too means a problem. Use very or so for praise.)
  • It was so a long day. It was such a long day. (Before a noun, use such a.)
  • I'm very tired to work. I'm too tired to work. (Too + adjective + to.)
  • There is too many sugar. There is too much sugar. (Much for things you can't count, many for things you can.)
  • I'm not enough old. I'm not old enough. (Enough goes after an adjective, but before a noun: enough money.)
  • I like tea, also. I like tea, too. (At the end of a sentence, too means also. That's a different meaning.)

The rule

Very makes a word stronger. So makes it stronger with feeling, often with a result. Too means more than is OK, so there is a problem.

The soup is very hot. (I can still eat it.) The soup is so hot that I burned my tongue. The soup is too hot to eat. (I can't eat it.)

The three words

WordMeaningPatternExample
verystrong, neutralvery + adjectiveIt's very cold today.
sostrong, emotionalso + adjective (+ that…)It's so cold (that) the lake froze.
toomore than OK, a problemtoo + adjective (+ to + verb)It's too cold to swim.

Too or enough?

Enough means as much as you need. It goes after an adjective or adverb, but before a noun.

He's old enough to drive. She didn't run fast enough.   We have enough money. There isn't enough time.

Too and not … enough often say the same thing from opposite sides: He's too young to drive. = He isn't old enough to drive.
