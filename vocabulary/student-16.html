What this lesson is for

Why this matters: saying the same word again and again sounds flat. A second, stronger word makes your point clearer and helps people remember it, but only if you choose it well.

You will know

  • four ways to use synonyms: double it, climb the scale, restate it, choose the precise word
  • useful phrases: in fact, or rather, not just ..., it was ..., In other words

You will understand

  • that synonyms are not always the same: a vast desert, a huge mistake, heavy rain
  • why one extra word is enough

You will be able to

  • make a plain sentence stronger
  • say the same idea again in different words
  • avoid saying the same thing twice by accident

Common mistakes to watch for

  • It was big, huge, enormous, vast and gigantic. It was big, really huge. (One extra word is enough. Use one synonym, then move on.)
  • The room was nice, really lovely. The food was good, really tasty. The pool was big, really huge. The food was good, really delicious. (Not in every sentence. Save it for the point you really want people to remember.)
  • a free gift a gift · return back return · repeat again repeat · past history history (Some words already contain the meaning, so the extra word adds nothing.)
  • It was good, in fact okay. It was good, in fact excellent. (Climb up, not down. After in fact or not just, the second word must be stronger.)
  • very exhausted, very freezing, very tiny absolutely exhausted, really freezing, tiny (Strong words don't take very.)

Before you start

Good speakers don't say the same word again and again. They use synonyms (words with a similar meaning) to make their point stronger and clearer.

Plain: The exam was difficult.

Richer: The exam was difficult, really challenging. The second word says the same thing again, so the listener feels it more.

Four ways to use synonyms

  1. 1Double it: put two words with the same meaning side by side. It was difficult, really challenging.
    It was a calm, peaceful morning.
    The children were noisy, really loud.
  2. 2Climb the scale: say a word, then a stronger one with in fact, or rather, or not just …, it was … warm→hot→boiling The film was good, in fact excellent.
    I was tired, or rather exhausted.
    The flat isn't just small, it's tiny.
  3. 3Restate it: say the same idea again in different words. The hotel was miles from the beach. In other words, we needed a car.
    What I mean is, …
    To put it another way, …
  4. 4Choose the precise word: synonyms are not always the same. Each one has its own meaning and its own word partners.
    WordBest forExample
    bigeveryday, general sizea big house, a big dog
    hugevery big; also for ideas and feelingsa huge mistake, a huge success
    vastvery large areas or amountsa vast desert, the vast majority
    enormousvery big in size or amountan enormous meal, an enormous amount of work
    Word partners: heavy rain (strong rain), a strong coffee, a broad smile, a wide road, a tall man, high prices.

Exercise 8: Speaking

  • Say it twice: one person says a plain sentence: The party was good. The partner says it again, stronger, with in fact, or rather or In other words: The party was good, in fact fantastic. In other words, we didn't want to go home! Then swap. Try: The weather was cold. The queue was long. My boss was angry. The book was interesting. The room was dirty.
  • Up the scale: say a word and your partner says a stronger one: warm → hot → boiling. Who runs out of words first?
  • My best holiday: describe your best holiday. Use at least three doubled descriptions: The beach was quiet, really peaceful. The food was good, in fact delicious. The hotel was enormous; in other words, we got lost every day! Your partner counts them.
  • Don't overdo it: listen to your partner. Did they use one synonym or too many? Did they say anything twice by accident, like a free gift?
