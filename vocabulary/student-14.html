Before you start

A plain sentence tells us what happened. A rich sentence lets us see it, hear it and feel it.

Plain: Maya walked to the car.

Rich: Maya walked briskly through the cold, grey rain to her old car.

Today you help tell the story of Maya's long day, from a rainy morning in Toronto to a cosy night by the fire. Tap any photo to see it bigger.

Three ways to make a sentence richer

  • Add an adjective before a noun: the rain → the icy rain
  • Add an adverb after a verb: she walked → she walked briskly
  • Swap a boring word for a precise one: nice → breathtaking, bad → awful, very big → enormous

Exercise 7: Speaking

  • Add a word: one person says a plain sentence: The cat sat. The next adds an adjective: The fat cat sat. The next adds an adverb: The fat cat sat lazily. Keep going! How long can you make it?
  • Fortunately, unfortunately: tell a story together. Each person adds one sentence starting with Fortunately or Unfortunately, with at least one adjective or adverb. Unfortunately, it was raining heavily. Fortunately, Maya had a huge umbrella…
