Before you start

A good photo makes us feel something. In this lesson you look at eight photos and learn the words to describe them.

Tap any photo to see it bigger. The photo bar at the bottom of the screen lets you look at any photo again, from anywhere in the lesson.

In many speaking exams (Aptis, Cambridge, IELTS) you describe and compare photos. This lesson gives you the language to do it well.

The rule: looks, looks like, looks as if

Three ways to say what a photo seems like. The word after them is the key.

looks + adjective: It looks peaceful.

looks like + noun: It looks like a painting.

looks as if + a sentence: It looks as if it's going to rain. (looks like + a sentence is also common in speaking.)

It looks like peaceful. It looks a painting.

The rule: guessing with must, might and can't

When you describe a photo, you often guess. Show how sure you are.

must = I'm sure it's true: They must be cold. There's snow everywhere.

might = maybe: He might be going to work.

can't = I'm sure it's not true: It can't be summer. The trees have no leaves.

They must to be cold. They must be cold.

Exercise 5: Match the strong adjectives

Tap an ordinary adjective, then tap its strong partner. Remember: very beautiful, but absolutely stunning.

  • beautiful = stunning
  • crowded = packed
  • cold = freezing
  • scary = terrifying
  • big = enormous
  • happy = delighted

Exercise 8: Speaking

  • Guess the photo: describe a photo to your teacher or a friend without saying its letter. Can they guess which one it is?
  • Compare: choose two photos and compare them. Photo C looks lonely, whereas Photo B looks absolutely joyful.
  • Imagine: what can you hear, smell and feel in your favourite photo?
  • Your photo: show a photo from your phone and describe how it makes you feel.
