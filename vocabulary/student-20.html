What this lesson is for

Why this matters: of and for are two of the most common words in English, and many languages use one word (like French de) where English needs one or the other. Getting them right makes you sound natural straight away.

You will know

  • that of shows a part, an amount or a material
  • that for shows a purpose, who gets something, or how long

You will understand

  • the difference between a cup of coffee and a cup for coffee
  • why wait, pay and look need for in English

You will be able to

  • choose of or for in everyday sentences
  • use for + a length of time: for two years
  • fix common of / for mistakes

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Thank you of your help. Thank you for your help.
  • I'm waiting the bus. I'm waiting for the bus.
  • I live here since three years. I've lived here for three years.
  • She's responsible of the team. She's responsible for the team.
  • a class of French a French class

The rule

Main ideaExamples
ofa part, an amount, belonging or a materialthe end of the film · a glass of water · the name of the street · made of wood
fora purpose, who gets it, or how longa knife for cutting · a present for you · for three years

Picture a mug. A cup of coffee is the coffee inside it. A cup for coffee (or a coffee cup) is the empty mug: that's what it is for.

Quick test: is it a gift or a help for someone? Then use for: I made tea for you. Walking is good for your health.

Careful: some words just take of, even before -ing: tired of waiting, afraid of flying, proud of you. Learn them as one block.

Tip for French speakers: attendre, payer and chercher have no preposition in French, but English needs for: wait for, pay for, look for. (But pay the bill: when you name the bill or the person, no for.)

Merci de → thank you for. Responsable de → responsible for. Depuis deux ans → for two years. Un cours de français → a French class.
