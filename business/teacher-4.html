Teacher Notes 4: Numbers, Sales and Trends

Open the student page

Purpose: give the student the vocabulary to talk about money and results (revenue, costs, profit, budget, forecast) and to describe changes in numbers accurately: trend verbs, adverbs of degree and speed, and the prepositions by, to, from ... to, in.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student which numbers matter in their job. If they can share a simple chart from work (with private figures removed or changed), use it for the speaking task. Otherwise, any public chart works: the price of gas in Ontario, a company’s share price.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Ask the student to say two or three big numbers from their work and note any problems.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Money words first, then the trend verbs (drill the past forms: rise, rose, risen; fall, fell, fallen), then the adverbs and prepositions. Draw quick lines in the air or on the screen and ask the student to describe them.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the trends. In Exercise 2, focus on by (the change) vs to (the new number), and rise vs raise.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one in class and set the other for homework.
  5. Reading (8 min). Lena’s Q3 email. Ask the student to underline every trend verb and preposition first, then answer the questions. Follow-up: “Draw the revenue and profit as a simple chart.”
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 2 is the core: the student draws and describes a graph. Then you describe one and the student draws it.
  7. Trend words and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A quickly; Part B and the homework at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • revenue /ˈrevənjuː/ (UK) or /ˈrevənuː/ (North America): stress on REV.
  • increase, decrease: the noun has stress on the first syllable (an IN-crease), the verb on the second (to in-CREASE). The same with record, export, import.
  • quarter /ˈkwɔːtə/: in North America the t often sounds like a quick d.
  • Big numbers: 1,250 = one thousand two hundred (and) fifty or twelve fifty for prices. $1.2m = one point two million dollars. No -s: two million, not two millions.
  • Decimals and fractions: 0.5 = zero point five or oh point five; 3/4 = three quarters; 2.5% = two point five percent.
  • -teen or -ty: 15 vs 50, 13 vs 30. Stress the end in fifTEEN, the start in FIFty. Very important on the phone.

False friends and common mistakes

  • raise vs rise: raise needs an object (we raised prices); rise doesn’t (prices rose). Also rised → rose.
  • benefit: Spanish beneficio, French bénéfice, Italian beneficio often mean profit. In English, a benefit is an advantage, or an extra from your employer (health benefits). Say profit.
  • turnover: in the UK it means revenue; in North America it usually means staff leaving a company (high staff turnover). For Canadian or US contexts, say revenue or sales.
  • billion: in English 1 billion = 1,000,000,000 (a thousand million). In many European languages a billion (Billion, billion, bilion) is a million million. Check the student knows this.
  • The comma and the point: English writes 1,250.50; many languages write 1.250,50. Check written numbers carefully.
  • two millions / hundreds dollars: no -s on million, thousand, hundred after a number: two million dollars.

Accuracy with numbers matters more in business than almost anywhere else. A mistake like fifteen for fifty, or billion for million, can cost real money. Spend a few minutes on listening: say ten numbers and ask the student to write them down.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Important numbers. Follow up: “Is that number going up or down this year?” “What is your target?”
  • Draw a graph. The student draws and describes; push for an adverb with every verb (rose steadily, fell sharply) and at least one by and one to.
  • A good year and a bad year. Good for reasons: because of, due to, as a result of. Link to the past simple.
  • Say the numbers. Then reverse it: you say ten numbers and the student writes them. Include 15/50, 13/30 and a price with cents.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: revenue = all the money from sales, before costs; costs = the money a company spends; profit = what is left after you take away the costs; loss = when you spend more than you earn; budget = a plan for how much you can spend; forecast = what you expect to happen next; invoice = a document that asks a customer to pay; quarter = three months of the year.
  • Exercise 2: 1 rose, 2 from, 3 by, 4 to, 5 rise, 6 slightly, 7 steadily, 8 reached.
  • Exercise 3: 1 loss, 2 budget, 3 invoice, 4 quarter, 5 revenue, 6 profit, 7 forecast, 8 decrease.
  • Exercise 4: 1 Sales rose by ten percent last year. 2 Costs fell from six to five million. 3 There was a sharp increase in online orders. 4 Our profits remained stable in the third quarter. 5 What was the revenue in the first quarter?
  • Exercise 5: 1 8, 2 online, 3 stable, 4 tax, 5 rise. The writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: up: rose, grew, improved, went up. down: fell, dropped, declined. no change: remained stable, levelled off, stayed the same. Part B: 1 Our sales rose by 10% last year. 2 Costs increased by 4% in the second quarter. 3 There was a big increase in sales in December. 4 No mistake. 5 The company made a big profit last year.
