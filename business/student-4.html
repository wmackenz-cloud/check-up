What this lesson is for

Why this matters: at work you often need to report results: are sales up or down, and by how much? The right verbs and prepositions help you explain numbers and graphs clearly.

You will know

  • money words: revenue, costs, profit, loss, budget, forecast, invoice, quarter
  • trend verbs: rise, fall, increase, drop, remain stable, level off, reach a peak
  • words for how much and how fast: sharply, steadily, slightly

You will understand

  • the difference between rose by 5% (the change) and rose to $2 million (the new number)
  • the difference between rise (no object) and raise (needs an object)

You will be able to

  • describe how numbers changed, and why
  • say big numbers, decimals and percentages correctly
  • write a short summary of results

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Our sales raised in May. Our sales rose in May. (raise needs an object: we raised our prices)
  • Costs went up in 4%. Costs went up by 4%.
  • a rise of sales a rise in sales (but a rise of 10%)
  • Our benefits were very high this year. Our profits were very high this year. (benefits are good things or extras, not money you earn)
  • two comma five million two point five million ($2.5m)
  • three millions dollars three million dollars (no -s after a number)

Warm-up

  • Which numbers are important in your job: sales, customers, costs, orders, hours?
  • Is your company (or your industry) doing better or worse than last year? Why?
  • In English, what is harder for you: saying big numbers, or describing a graph?

Key vocabulary

Money words

WordMeaning
revenue / salesall the money a company gets from selling, before costs (UK also: turnover)
costs / expensesthe money a company spends
profitrevenue minus costs, when the result is positive
losswhen the costs are bigger than the revenue
budgeta plan for how much money you can spend
forecastwhat you expect to happen in the future
invoicea document that asks a customer to pay
quarter (Q1, Q2 ...)three months of the year: Q1 = January to March
figuresnumbers, especially official results: the sales figures

How much, how fast, and the prepositions

WordMeaningExample
sharply / dramaticallya lot, and quicklySales rose sharply in December.
significantlya lotCosts fell significantly.
steadily / graduallyat a regular speed / slowly over timeOur customer numbers grew steadily.
slightlya littlePrices increased slightly.
by + the changehow much it changedSales rose by 5%.
to + the new numberwhere it finishedSales rose to $2 million.
from ... to ...the start and the endCosts fell from $50,000 to $40,000.
a rise in + noun
a rise of + number		the noun formsa rise in sales; a rise of 10%

Say it right: REVenue, INvoice, QUARter. Noun or verb: an INcrease, to inCREASE; a DEcrease, to deCREASE. $2.5m = two point five million dollars.

Exercise 5: Reading

From: Lena Fischer, Sales Director
To: Management team
Subject: Q3 sales results

Hi all,

Here is a short summary of our results for the third quarter (July to September).

Total revenue rose by 8%, from $2.5 million to $2.7 million. This increase came mainly from online sales, which grew sharply after the new website launched in July. Sales in our stores fell slightly, by about 2%.

Costs remained stable at $2.1 million, so our profit increased to $600,000. That is the highest figure we have ever had in one quarter.

The only bad news is in the export market. Sales to the US dropped by 15% because of the new import tax. We are looking into ways to reduce our prices there.

For Q4, our forecast is a further rise of 5%, as November and December are always our busiest months.

Best,
Lena
