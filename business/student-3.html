What this lesson is for

Why this matters: a lot of your work happens by email and phone. Clear, polite standard phrases save you time, and people understand you the first time.

You will know

  • standard email phrases: I'm writing to, Thank you for, Please find attached, I look forward to
  • phrasal verbs for calls and follow-ups: get back to, call back, put through, hold on, follow up, look into
  • email words: attachment, subject line, cc, forward, reply all

You will understand

  • when to write formally and when you can be informal
  • why I look forward to is followed by -ing

You will be able to

  • write a clear, polite work email from start to finish
  • answer the phone and promise to call back
  • follow up on a problem or a request

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I look forward to see you. I look forward to seeing you. (to here is a preposition, so use -ing)
  • I write you to ask about your prices. I'm writing to ask about your prices.
  • I send you in attachment the invoice. Please find attached the invoice. / I've attached the invoice.
  • I'll come back to you on Monday. I'll get back to you on Monday.
  • Can you recall me later? Can you call me back later? (recall means remember)
  • Dear Mr. John, Dear Mr. Smith, (Mr. / Ms. + the family name)

Warm-up

  • How many work emails do you write in English in a week? Who do you write to?
  • Do you prefer to phone someone or to send an email? Why?
  • What is harder for you in English: starting an email, or ending it? Answering the phone, or making a call?

Key vocabulary

Email phrases

PhraseUse it forExample
I'm writing to + verbthe reason for your emailI'm writing to confirm our meeting on Friday.
Thank you for + noun / -ingthanking at the startThank you for your quick reply.
Please find attached ... / I've attached ...a file with the emailPlease find attached the invoice for May.
Could you please + verb?a polite requestCould you please send me the contract?
I'm afraid (that) ...bad news, politelyI'm afraid the delivery will be late.
Please let me know if ...inviting questionsPlease let me know if you have any questions.
I look forward to + -ing / nouna polite endingI look forward to hearing from you.
Best regards / Kind regardsclosing the emailKind regards, Ana

Phrasal verbs for emails and calls

Phrasal verbMeaningExample
get back to someonereply laterI'll get back to you tomorrow.
call someone backreturn a phone callCan I call you back in ten minutes?
put someone throughconnect a phone callI'll put you through to Sales.
hold onwait a moment (on the phone)Hold on a moment, please.
follow up (on)check later what happenedI'm following up on my email from Monday.
look intotry to find out about a problemWe're looking into the problem.
set uparrange a call or meetingLet's set up a call for Thursday.
send outsend to many peopleWe sent out the invitations yesterday.

Exercise 7: Style and mistakes

Part A: Formal or informal?

Watch out

  • Formal: to clients, senior managers and people you don't know. Dear Ms. Clark, I'm writing to ..., Kind regards
  • Informal: to colleagues you know well. Hi Tom, Just a quick note ..., Cheers
  • Not sure? Hi + first name ... Best regards is safe for most work emails in Canada and the US.
