Teacher Notes 2: Meetings

Open the student page

Purpose: help the student take an active part in meetings in English: understand the vocabulary of agendas and minutes, use the right verbs with meeting, and give an opinion, agree, disagree and interrupt politely.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student what meetings they have at work (team meetings, client calls, project updates) and how often they speak in them. If they can share a real agenda (with private details removed), use it in the speaking task.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Listen for how the student describes meetings now, and note any missing words (agenda, minutes, chair).
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Meeting words, then the verbs, then the phrases. Drill the phrases with intonation: polite disagreement needs a soft, rising tone on I see your point ...
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then choosing the phrase. In Exercise 2, ask the student to say each finished sentence out loud, as in a real meeting.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one in class and set the other for homework.
  5. Reading (8 min). The minutes. Ask the student to find each meeting verb first (raised, postpone, agreed), then answer the questions. Follow-up: “What would you add to the action points?”
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 4 is the core: the student chairs a short meeting. Play a difficult attendee: talk too long so they must interrupt, and disagree so they must respond politely.
  7. Phrases and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A quickly; Part B and the homework at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • agenda /əˈdʒendə/: stress on GEN. The g is soft, like j.
  • minutes /ˈmɪnɪts/: the same as minutes on a clock. Not /maɪˈnjuːt/ (that is the adjective minute, very small).
  • postpone /pəʊstˈpəʊn/: stress on PONE. The t is often almost silent.
  • issue /ˈɪʃuː/ (North America) or /ˈɪsjuː/ (UK): not /ˈaɪsju/.
  • schedule: /ˈskedʒuːl/ in North America, /ˈʃedjuːl/ in the UK. Both are fine.
  • Intonation: Sorry to interrupt, but ... with a fall on interrupt and a short pause before but sounds confident and polite.

False friends and common mistakes

  • assist a meeting: Spanish asistir a, French assister à, Italian assistere a. In English, assist means help. Say attend a meeting (no to).
  • I am agree: very common from Spanish, Italian and Portuguese. Agree is a verb: I agree. Also I'm not agree → I don't agree.
  • discuss about: discuss takes a direct object: discuss the budget. With about, use talk about.
  • resume: French résumer, Spanish resumir mean summarize. In English resume means start again after a break (and a résumé is a CV). Say to sum up or summarize.
  • actual / actually: in many languages actual means current. In English it means real. The current situation, not the actual situation.
  • explain me: say explain it to me or Could you clarify that?

Some students come from work cultures where you don't interrupt or disagree with a manager in a meeting. Talk about this openly: in many North American and UK workplaces, polite disagreement is expected, and silence can look like agreement.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Useful and useless meetings. Follow up: “How would you make that meeting shorter?” “Who should not be there?”
  • A good and a bad meeting. Good for the past simple and for the verbs: The chair didn't follow the agenda. We didn't reach a decision.
  • Disagree with your manager. Write the two sentences together, then practise the intonation until it sounds polite but firm.
  • Chair the role play. Agenda: date, place, budget, food. Interrupt them twice and disagree once. At the end, ask them to sum up and give two action points.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: agenda = the list of topics for the meeting; minutes = written notes of what was said and decided; action points = tasks people must do after the meeting; chair = the person who runs the meeting; deadline = the date when a task must be finished; AOB = the last item: topics not on the list; postpone = move the meeting to a later time; attendees = the people at the meeting.
  • Exercise 2: 1 started, 2 interrupt, 3 but, 4 on, 5 sum, 6 agree, 7 clarify, 8 reach.
  • Exercise 3: 1 agenda, 2 attend, 3 postpone, 4 cancel, 5 chair, 6 minutes, 7 raise, 8 deadline.
  • Exercise 4: 1 Shall we get started? 2 Let's move on to the next item. 3 Could I just add something here? 4 I'm not sure I agree with that. 5 Who is going to write the minutes?
  • Exercise 5: 1 Kim, 2 budget, 3 1, 4 fair, 5 printer. The writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: agree: That's a good point., Exactly., I couldn't agree more., I totally agree with Omar.. disagree: I'm not so sure about that., I see your point, but ..., I'm afraid I disagree.. interrupt: Sorry to interrupt, but ..., Could I just add something?, Can I come in here?. Part B: 1 I agree with your idea. 2 We will discuss the budget at the next meeting. 3 Twelve people attended the meeting. 4 No mistake. 5 Can you send me the minutes of the meeting?
