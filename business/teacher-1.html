Teacher Notes 1: Your Company and Your Job

Open the student page

Purpose: give adult professionals the core language to introduce their company and their own role clearly: what the company does, how it is organized, which department they work in and what they are responsible for (work for / in / on, be responsible for, be in charge of, report to, deal with, involve + -ing).

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student for their job title and one sentence about their company. If they are happy to share it, look at their LinkedIn profile or company website. It gives you real material for the speaking task and the homework.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Listen for job titles translated word for word, and note them for later.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). The job phrases first, then the company words and departments. Ask the student to place their own job in a department, and draw a quick chart of their company together on the screen.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the job phrases. In Exercise 2, push for the preposition and the form after it: responsible for + -ing, involves + -ing.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one in class and set the other for homework, depending on the student’s speed.
  5. Reading (8 min). Priya’s introduction email. Ask the student to underline every job phrase first, then answer the questions. Follow-up: “Draw Priya’s place in the company as a chart.”
  6. Speaking (10 min). Questions 2 and 4 are the core. The student explains their department chart; then you play the interviewer for question 4.
  7. Prepositions and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A quickly; Part B and the homework at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • colleague /ˈkɒliːɡ/: stress on the first syllable. Many students say col-LEEG.
  • responsible /rɪˈspɒnsəbl/: four syllables, stress on SPON. Students often add a vowel at the end.
  • subsidiary /səbˈsɪdiəri/: stress on SID.
  • headquarters /ˌhedˈkwɔːtəz/: stress on QUAR. In head office the stress is on OF.
  • purchasing /ˈpɜːtʃəsɪŋ/: -chase becomes /tʃəs/, not chays.
  • staff /stɑːf/ and stuff /stʌf/: a useful minimal pair. Our staff are people; our stuff is things.

False friends and common mistakes

  • I work in Google: for the employer, say work for (or at). Use in for a department or an industry: work in finance, work in IT.
  • responsible of: say responsible for. Also Spanish el responsable, French le responsable: in English say the person in charge or the manager, not the responsible.
  • staffs: staff is a group noun: 300 staff, a member of staff. Say employees if the student needs a plural with -s.
  • chef: German and French Chef / chef means boss. In English a chef cooks food. Say boss, manager or head of.
  • direction: French, Spanish and Italian use this word for the company’s management. In English say management or the board.
  • I am working here since 2019: say I have worked or I’ve been working here since 2019. This links to Grammar Trap 1 (present perfect).

Job titles do not translate directly. A Spanish licenciado, a Polish kierownik or a Vietnamese trưởng phòng needs a description, not a single word: I manage a team of eight in the accounts department. Teach the description first, then the title.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Describe your company. Follow up: “Who are your main competitors?” “Who are your biggest clients?”
  • Draw your department. Follow up: “Who do you go to when there’s a problem?” “How often do you meet your manager?”
  • A normal working day. Contrast routines (present simple) with current projects (present continuous): “Usually I deal with invoices, but this month I’m working on the audit.”
  • Interview question. Coach the structure: the role and company in one sentence, two or three main responsibilities, one recent result. Keep it to about one minute.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: HR = hires new staff and organizes training; Finance = pays invoices and prepares the budget; Sales = finds new customers and sells the products; Marketing = plans advertising and promotes the brand; Customer Service = answers questions and complaints from customers; IT = fixes computers and sets up software; Logistics = moves the goods and stores them in the warehouse; Purchasing = buys materials from suppliers.
  • Exercise 2: 1 for, 2 in, 3 on, 4 for, 5 talking, 6 of, 7 to, 8 deal with.
  • Exercise 3: 1 headquarters, 2 branch, 3 colleague, 4 supplier, 5 staff, 6 competitor, 7 client, 8 subsidiary.
  • Exercise 4: 1 I work for a small software company. 2 She is responsible for training new staff. 3 Who do you report to? 4 My job involves meeting clients every week. 5 He is in charge of the Calgary branch.
  • Exercise 5: 1 Vancouver, 2 Purchasing, 3 Bell, 4 chain, 5 warehouse. The writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: for: work for a bank, is responsible for sales, apply for a job. of: is in charge of the team, is head of marketing, is part of a big group. to: report to the manager, talk to a client, introduce her to the team, send it to HR. Part B: 1 I am responsible for the budget. 2 My job involves visiting clients. 3 I report to the sales director. 4 No mistake. 5 Our company has 300 staff in Canada.
