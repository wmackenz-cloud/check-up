Teacher Notes 5: Negotiating and Making Decisions

Open the student page

Purpose: give the student the language to negotiate a simple deal politely and clearly: make and respond to offers, ask for better terms, say no, suggest a compromise and confirm the agreement. The key grammar is the first conditional for offers (If you ..., we can ...) and polite questions (Would you be willing to ...?).

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student what they buy or sell at work, or what they might negotiate (a salary, a contract, a rent). Prepare a simple role-play card for question 3 with your own limits (lowest price, best delivery date) and don’t show it to the student.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Talk about negotiation in the student’s culture; it often changes how direct they are in English.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Negotiation words, then the phrases (drill them with polite intonation), then the verb + noun pairs.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the offers. In Exercise 2, focus on If + present simple (not will) and willing to + verb.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one in class and set the other for homework.
  5. Reading (8 min). Hannah and Carlos. Read it aloud together (you take one part). Ask the student to find the offer, the condition, the compromise and the confirmation. Follow-up: “Who won this negotiation?”
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 3 is the core: the laptop role play. Use your hidden limits. At the end, the student must confirm the deal with So, just to confirm ...
  7. Collocations and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A quickly; Part B and the homework at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • negotiate /nɪˈɡəʊʃieɪt/: stress on GO, and the t sounds like sh. Also negotiation /nɪˌɡəʊʃiˈeɪʃn/.
  • compromise /ˈkɒmprəmaɪz/: stress on the first syllable; the end rhymes with size.
  • contract: the noun is CONtract; the verb (to get smaller, or to catch an illness) is conTRACT.
  • discount /ˈdɪskaʊnt/: stress on DIS.
  • I'm afraid ...: say it softly with a fall-rise. Said flat and fast, it can sound rude.
  • Prices: $11 each = eleven dollars each; $10.50 = ten fifty or ten dollars and fifty cents.

False friends and common mistakes

  • If you will order: after if for the future, use the present simple: If you order ... This links to Grammar Trap 6 (conditionals).
  • firm the contract: Spanish firmar, Italian firmare, Portuguese assinar → sign. In English, a firm is a company.
  • do a decision / do an offer: make a decision, make an offer. British English also says take a decision; both are fine.
  • eventually: Spanish eventualmente, French éventuellement, German eventuell mean possibly. In English, eventually means in the end, after a long time. Say possibly or perhaps.
  • sympathetic: in many languages this means nice or friendly. In English it means showing that you understand someone’s problem. Say friendly or pleasant.
  • I want a discount: grammatically correct but very direct. Teach softer forms: Could you ...? Would you be willing to ...? We were hoping for ...

Directness varies a lot between cultures. Students from very direct cultures can sound rude in English (No. Too expensive.); students from very indirect ones can be too unclear (Maybe it is a little difficult ...). The phrases in this lesson are the middle path for Canadian and US business: polite, but clear.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Negotiation in your culture. Follow up: “Would you negotiate your salary? How?” “What happens if you don’t negotiate at a market?”
  • A past negotiation. Good for the past simple and for reported offers: They offered ..., so I asked if they could ...
  • The laptop role play. Your hidden limits: list price $900, lowest $780 for 50 or more, free delivery only if they pay in 15 days. Make them work for it.
  • Tips for negotiators. Good for advice language: You should ..., It's a good idea to ..., Never ...

Answers

  • Exercise 1: discount = a lower price than usual; terms = the conditions of a deal: price, payment, delivery; compromise = an agreement where both sides give something; contract = a written legal agreement; deal = an agreement between two businesses; proposal = a plan or offer that you present; condition = something that must happen first; negotiate = discuss something to reach an agreement.
  • Exercise 2: 1 order, 2 can, 3 on, 4 to sign, 5 afraid, 6 about, 7 make, 8 sign.
  • Exercise 3: 1 condition, 2 compromise, 3 contract, 4 terms, 5 discount, 6 agreement, 7 offer, 8 deal.
  • Exercise 4: 1 Could you do a little better on the price? 2 If you order today, we can deliver next week. 3 Would you be willing to pay in advance? 4 I'm afraid we can't accept that offer. 5 How about if we meet in the middle?
  • Exercise 5: 1 12, 2 2,000, 3 delivery, 4 20, 5 contract. The writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: make: make a decision, make an offer, make a profit, make a mistake, make a phone call. do: do business, do research, do your best, do someone a favour, do the paperwork. Part B: 1 If you order today, we can deliver on Monday. 2 Can you make us a better offer? 3 Would you be willing to pay in advance? 4 No mistake. 5 Let's sign the contract tomorrow morning.
