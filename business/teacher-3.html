Teacher Notes 3: Emails and Phone Calls

Open the student page

Purpose: give the student reliable, ready-made phrases for work emails and simple phone calls, so they can write and call with confidence: opening and closing an email, asking politely, giving bad news, and the phrasal verbs used for calls and follow-ups.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student to bring one real work email they wrote or received in English (with private details removed). It is ideal for the speaking discussion and shows you their real level of formality.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Ask how they start and end emails now; note any translated openings (I write you because ...).
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Email phrases first, then the phrasal verbs, then the email words. For phrasal verbs, act out a short call: Hold on ... I'll put you through.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the email phrases. Focus on the form after each phrase: writing to + verb, thank you for + -ing, look forward to + -ing.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one in class and set the other for homework.
  5. Reading (8 min). Karen’s reply to a complaint. Ask the student to find the reason for writing, the bad news, the solution and the ending. Follow-up: “Was this a good reply? Why?”
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 3 is the core: a phone role play about a late order. Then swap roles: they are the supplier and must give bad news politely.
  7. Style and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A quickly; Part B and the homework at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • attachment /əˈtætʃmənt/: stress on TACH.
  • appreciate /əˈpriːʃieɪt/: the c is /ʃ/, like sh.
  • signature /ˈsɪɡnətʃə/: the g is pronounced, unlike in sign.
  • email addresses: @ = at, . = dot, - = dash or hyphen, _ = underscore. Practise saying the student’s own address.
  • phone numbers: say them in groups, with 0 as oh or zero: 416 / 555 / 0182 = four one six, five five five, oh one eight two.
  • Spelling: practise the NATO alphabet (Alpha, Bravo, Charlie ...) for names on the phone.

False friends and common mistakes

  • I write you because ...: say I'm writing to ... (+ verb) or I'm writing about ... (+ noun).
  • I look forward to hear: to is a preposition here, so use -ing: look forward to hearing / to meeting / to your reply.
  • I come back to you: French je reviens vers vous, Spanish vuelvo a contactarle. Say I'll get back to you.
  • Please find attach / in attachment: say Please find attached ... or I've attached ...
  • informations / advices: both are uncountable: some information, some advice. This links to Grammar Trap 4.
  • Dear Mr. John: Mr. / Ms. + family name (Dear Mr. Novak), or first name only (Hi John). Never title + first name.

Many students write emails that are too long and too formal. A good rule for Canadian and US workplaces: the reason in the first line, one idea per paragraph, and a clear request at the end. Read one of the student’s real emails together and shorten it.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Hard emails. Follow up: “What do you usually write in the first line?” “How do you end an email to your manager?”
  • Answering the phone. Practise: Good morning, Bright Office Supplies, Karen speaking. How can I help? and I’m afraid she’s not available. Can I take a message?
  • The late order call. As the supplier, be a little vague so the student has to ask follow-up questions: When exactly? Can you put that in an email?
  • Phone or email. Good for comparing and giving reasons: For bad news, I prefer to call, because ...

Answers

  • Exercise 1: attachment = a file you send with an email; subject line = the short title of an email; cc = sends a copy to another person; forward = send an email you received to someone else; reply all = answer everyone who got the email; signature = your name and contact details at the end; inbox = where new emails arrive; out-of-office reply = an automatic answer when you are away.
  • Exercise 2: 1 to ask, 2 replying, 3 attached, 4 hearing, 5 afraid, 6 let, 7 back, 8 on.
  • Exercise 3: 1 call, 2 put, 3 hold, 4 follow, 5 look, 6 set, 7 forward, 8 attachment.
  • Exercise 4: 1 I'm writing to confirm our meeting. 2 Please find attached the invoice for May. 3 Could you call me back this afternoon? 4 I look forward to hearing from you. 5 I'll put you through to Mr. Diaz.
  • Exercise 5: 1 chairs, 2 warehouse, 3 Wednesday, 4 discount, 5 note. The writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: formal: Dear Ms. Clark,, I'm writing to inform you that ..., Please find attached the report., I look forward to hearing from you., Kind regards,. informal: Hi Tom,, Just a quick note to say ..., Here's the file you wanted., Talk soon!, Cheers,. Part B: 1 I look forward to hearing from you. 2 Please find attached the report for June. 3 I'm writing to ask about your prices. 4 No mistake. 5 I will get back to you tomorrow with the numbers.
