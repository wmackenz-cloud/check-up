Trial Lesson: Say More with A.R.E.

Open the student worksheet

Purpose: a relaxed, chatty trial that gives the student one quick, visible win. A.R.E. turns short answers into longer ones and gives you natural follow-up questions, so the conversation keeps going without feeling like a test.

Length: works in 25 or 50 minutes. Timings below are for 25; for 50, double the two chat stages.

Before the trial

  • Read their Preply message and trial notes. Pick two or three easy questions about their life: work, family, travel, hobbies.
  • Have the student page ready to screen-share. Don't show it at the start.
  • Optional: send a Check-Up link before the lesson so you know their level going in.

The plan (25 minutes)

TimeStageWhat you do
8 to 10 minChatEasy questions about them. Keep it warm and light. Quietly note one or two short answers, for example "I like my job."
5 minThe trickShare the page. "Can I show you a quick trick? Earlier you said …" Rebuild their own answer together with A, R and E, out loud. Have them say the better version twice.
8 to 10 minChat again, with A.R.E.Back to conversation on their topics. Prompt with "Why?" and "Can you give me an example?" Use the topic list only if the chat runs dry. Don't correct grammar now; note two or three phrases for later.
3 to 5 minWrap-upOne thing they did well, one thing you'd work on together, then the invitation (below).

Follow-up questions that keep it going

  • For R: Why do you think that? What's the main reason? Has it always been that way?
  • For E: Can you give me an example? Has that happened to you? What happened next?
  • To extend: Do other people agree with you? Was it different when you were younger? What would change your mind?

The wrap-up

Say something like: "You were really good at … When you added a reason and an example, your answers got twice as long, and you sounded confident. If we work together, we'd start with …, because that's where you'll see progress fastest."

Fill in their strength, fastest win and first two lesson topics on the Trial tab straight after. Your follow-up messages use them.

If the student asks...

  • "Can we just talk?" Yes. That's most of this lesson. A.R.E. is a five-minute tool inside the chat, not a lecture.
  • "Will you correct my mistakes?" "In this lesson I'll note a few and send them to you afterwards, so we don't interrupt the conversation." Then send them in the 24 to 48 hour message.
  • "I'm preparing for an exam." A.R.E. is exactly how to answer opinion questions in IELTS, Cambridge and Aptis speaking. Mention the matching Check-Up or exam lesson.
