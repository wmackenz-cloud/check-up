What this lesson is for

Why this matters: in a new school, you need to know who does what and who to ask for help. American and British schools use different job titles, so this lesson helps you understand both and explain your own role clearly.

You will know

  • US and UK job titles: principal / head teacher, substitute / supply teacher, teacher's aide / teaching assistant
  • phrases for roles: be responsible for, be in charge of, report to, work as, cover a class

You will understand

  • why faculty and professor are false friends
  • when to use a, the or no article with a job: a deputy head, the deputy head at Hillside, head of science

You will be able to

  • explain who works at your school and what they do
  • describe your own role and who you report to
  • ask who to talk to about a problem

Common mistakes to watch for

  • All the faculties came to the meeting. All the teachers came to the meeting. (In a US school, the faculty means all the teachers, as a group. In the UK, a faculty is usually a group of departments at a university: the Faculty of Law.)
  • I'm a professor at a primary school. I'm a teacher at a primary school. (A professor teaches at a university; in the UK, only the most senior university teachers.)
  • I work like a teaching assistant. I work as a teaching assistant. (work like = work in a similar way)
  • She is the responsible of the library. She is in charge of the library. / She is responsible for the library.
  • Who should I talk about this problem? Who should I talk to about this problem?
  • Can you substitute my class tomorrow? Can you cover my class tomorrow?

Warm-up

  • How many people work at your school? Who are they? Think about more than the teachers.
  • Who is your manager at work? What is their job title in your language?
  • If you have a problem with a student, who do you talk to first?

Key vocabulary

Say it right: PRINcipal (sounds the same as principle), DEPuty, SUBstitute, aide (sounds like aid), SENco, cusTOdian, CAREtaker, FACulty, COUNsellor.

Language point: roles and responsibilities

PatternExample
be responsible for + noun / -ingThe deputy head is responsible for timetabling.
be in charge of + people or an areaShe's in charge of the English department.
report to + personTeaching assistants report to the SENCo.
work as + job (not like)I work as a supply teacher. (work like = work in a similar way)
cover a class / cover for a colleagueCan you cover my Year 8 class? I'm covering for Ana this week.
talk to someone about somethingWho should I talk to about a student's reading? The preposition goes at the end of the question.
articles with jobsShe's a deputy head. (one of many) She's the deputy head at Hillside. (the only one) He's head of science. (a title: no article needed)
