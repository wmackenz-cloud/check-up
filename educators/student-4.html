What this lesson is for

Why this matters: teachers talk about what they teach and how they plan it in interviews, meetings and training. This lesson gives you the US and UK words and the natural phrases, so you can explain your work like an expert.

You will know

  • US and UK words for subjects: math / maths, language arts / English, social studies / humanities
  • planning words: unit plan / scheme of work, state standards / National Curriculum, learning objective, PD / CPD
  • word partners: plan a lesson, cover a topic, set objectives, set / assign homework

You will understand

  • the difference between teach (what you do) and learn (what the student does)
  • why course and curriculum are false friends

You will be able to

  • explain what you teach and how you plan a unit
  • describe one lesson: its objective, its activities and how it ends
  • talk about your training and professional development

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I sent my curriculum to the school. I sent my CV (UK) / résumé (US) to the school. (In English, the curriculum is what students learn.)
  • In Year 10 I teach a biology course. In Year 10 I teach biology. (UK) (In the US, a course is usually one subject. In the UK, a course is often a whole programme of study, like a three-year degree course, and a training course is a short course for adults.)
  • I learn my students English. I teach my students English.
  • Please explain them the rules. Please explain the rules to them.
  • I followed a training on dyslexia. I did a training course on dyslexia.
  • The lesson focuses in speaking. The lesson focuses on speaking.

Warm-up

  • What subjects do you teach, or did you teach? Which do you enjoy most?
  • How much freedom do you have to choose what and how you teach?
  • What was the last training course you did for your job? Was it useful?

Language point: teaching collocations

PatternExample
teach + person + subject / teach + person + to + verbI teach adults English. I teach them to write clear emails. (teach someone how to is also fine)
learn is what the student doesMy students learn quickly. Not: I learn my students English.
explain something to someoneCould you explain the task to the class? Not: explain me
plan / teach a lesson; cover a topic; follow the curriculumThis week we're covering fractions.
set objectives; set (UK) / assign (US) homeworkI set homework on Mondays. I assign two readings a week.
meet the needs of; adapt a task for a groupI adapt the reading for students who need more support.
a lesson on / about; focus on; based on; link something toa lesson on the water cycle; the unit is based on the standards
do a training course (not a training)I did a training course on dyslexia last year.

Exercise 8: Prepositions: on, to or for?

Part A: on, to or for?

Watch out

  • on: the topic or focus: a lesson on fractions, focus on speaking, based on the standards, work on a project
  • to: the person or the thing it connects with: explain the task to the class, link it to the curriculum
  • for: the people or purpose: a course for new teachers, materials for Year 6, prepare students for the exam
