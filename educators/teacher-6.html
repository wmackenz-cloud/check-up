Teacher Notes 6: Parents, Colleagues and Job Interviews

Open the student page

Purpose: help teachers communicate professionally with parents, colleagues and interview panels: the words for school life and careers in American and British English (parent-teacher conference / parents' evening, résumé / CV, teaching license / QTS), soft and positive phrases for concerns, and accurate tenses for talking about experience.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student if they have a real interview or parents' meeting coming up. If they do, build the speaking around it. If they are happy to share it, look at their CV or LinkedIn profile.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three questions. Note the tense the student uses for their experience (I am teaching since is very common).
  2. Key vocabulary (8 min). Both tables. Spend a moment on holiday / vacation and CV / résumé / curriculum.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). US or UK, then phrases for situations. Ask the student to read each phrase aloud with a warm voice.
  4. Language point and Exercise 3 (7 min). Present perfect vs past simple for experience; softening (doesn't always).
  5. Reading (7 min). The advert and the invitation. Ask: “What would you prepare for this interview?”
  6. Speaking (12 min). Question 1 (you play the parent), then questions 3 and 4 as a mock interview.
  7. Prepositions and wrap-up (3 min). Exercise 8 Part A together. Exercises 4, 5 and 8 Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • résumé /ˈrezəmeɪ/ (US), /ˈrezjumeɪ/ (UK use): three syllables, stress on the first. The verb resume /rɪˈzjuːm/ means start again.
  • conference /ˈkɒnfərəns/: stress on CON; often two syllables in fast speech: /ˈkɒnfrəns/.
  • tenure /ˈtenjər/: stress on TEN.
  • qualified /ˈkwɒlɪfaɪd/: three syllables; students often add one: qua-li-fi-ed.
  • referee /ˌrefəˈriː/: stress on REE; reference /ˈrefrəns/: stress on REF.
  • colleague /ˈkɒliːɡ/: stress on COL.

Getting the US and UK pairs right

  • pass out / hand out: Americans really do say pass out the papers. In both varieties, pass out also means faint, so British listeners may smile. Hand out is safe everywhere.
  • call in sick is common in both varieties; phone in sick and ring in sick are British.
  • tenure / permanent contract is a rough match. In England, many teachers get a permanent contract from the start; US K-12 tenure comes after a probation period and gives extra protection.
  • QTS is for England (and Wales). Scotland and Northern Ireland register teachers differently. In Ontario, teachers are certified by the Ontario College of Teachers (OCT).
  • references / referees: Americans say references available on request and list their references (the people). British forms ask for referees.

False friends and common mistakes

  • curriculum (Spanish currículum, Portuguese currículo, Italian curriculum): for the career document say CV (UK) or résumé (US).
  • I am working here since (Spanish trabajo aquí desde, Italian lavoro qui da, French je travaille ici depuis, Portuguese trabalho aqui desde, Polish pracuję tu od): say I have worked / have been working here since 2015.
  • conference (Spanish conferencia, Italian conferenza, French conférence): these often mean a talk or lecture. In US schools, a parent-teacher conference is a short meeting with parents.
  • actually (Spanish actualmente, Italian attualmente, French actuellement, Portuguese atualmente, Polish aktualnie): in an interview say currently or at the moment. Actually means in fact.
  • sympathetic (Spanish simpático, Italian simpatico, French sympathique, Polish sympatyczny): to describe a friendly parent or colleague, say nice, friendly, pleasant. Sympathetic means understanding someone's problems.
  • pretend (Spanish pretender, Portuguese pretender, French prétendre): to talk about plans say intend or hope to. I pretend to stay for many years sounds very strange in an interview.
  • Vietnamese: họp phụ huynh = parents' evening / parent-teacher conference; sơ yếu lý lịch = CV / résumé. Watch tense marking for experience (I teach here since 2015) and the final /d/ and /t/ in worked, taught, managed. Very direct translations of concerns can sound harsh; practise the softening phrases.

Interview answers are long turns, which can be stressful. Let the student prepare notes first, and say it is fine to pause and think. Nothing in this lesson needs to be fast.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Parents' evening. Coach the order: welcome, a strength, one area to work on, what the school is doing, how parents can help, next step. You play a friendly parent first, then a worried one.
  • Unhappy parent. Useful: I understand why you're worried. Let me show you how the work was marked. What we can do next is ...
  • A lesson that went well. Use STAR if the student knows it: situation, task, action, result. Push past simple for the story.
  • Managing behaviour. Coach a clear structure: clear routines, positive relationships, consistent consequences, one short example.

Notes on the exercises

  • Exercise 1: staff meeting is used in both countries, so it is not in the sort. CV exists in the US for academic jobs; the explanation says so.
  • Exercise 2: after matching, ask the student to say each phrase as if to a real parent or panel.
  • Exercise 3: item 4 shows softening. Ask: “What's the difference between she never and she doesn't always?”
  • Exercise 6: the advert and email are British. Ask for the US words: vacancy → opening, CV → résumé, referees → references, QTS → teaching license.
  • Exercise 8: Part B item 3 is correct American English, a nice discussion point.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: US English: parent-teacher conference, teachers' lounge, résumé, vacation, teaching license, student teaching. UK English: parents' evening, staff room, CV, the summer holidays, QTS, teaching placement.
  • Exercise 2: Start with a strength = Sam is making real progress in his writing.; Raise a concern gently = One area we're working on is homework.; Ask for help at home = I'd love your support with reading at home.; Suggest a meeting = Could we arrange a time to talk next week?; Describe a past job = In my last school, I was responsible for Year 6.; Sell yourself in an interview = A strength I'd bring to your team is my work with EAL students.; Ask for a moment to think = That's a good question. Let me think for a moment.; End a meeting politely = Thank you for coming in. Let's talk again after half-term..
  • Exercise 3: 1 have been, 2 was, 3 arrange, 4 doesn't, 5 for, 6 in, 7 hand out, 8 in.
  • Exercise 4: 1 résumé, 2 staff room, 3 parents' evening, 4 vacancy, 5 referees, 6 placement, 7 tenure, 8 conference.
  • Exercise 5: 1 Your son is making good progress in maths. 2 Could we arrange a time to talk next week? 3 I have been teaching English for ten years. 4 One area we are working on is spelling. 5 Why do you want to work at our school?
  • Exercise 6: 1 permanent, 2 SENCo, 3 three, 4 referees, 5 EAL.
  • Exercise 8: Part A: for: apply for the post, thank you for coming, a vacancy for a music teacher. in: experience in teaching adults, interested in the job, a degree in education, progress in reading. about: worried about his marks, talk about her progress, a question about homework. Part B: 1 I have been teaching here since 2015. 2 I'm applying for the job of EAL teacher. 3 No mistake. 4 I have experience in teaching teenagers. 5 Please find attached my CV and a cover letter.
