What this lesson is for

Why this matters: colleagues, parents and visitors often ask about your school day. You need the right words for times, places and routines, and you need to know which words are American and which are British.

You will know

  • US and UK words for the school day: schedule / timetable, recess / break, take attendance / take the register
  • words for the classroom and building: hallway / corridor, eraser / rubber, bulletin board / noticeboard
  • time prepositions: at 8:30, on Tuesdays, in the morning, during the lesson

You will understand

  • why we use the present simple for routines and timetables
  • why some words, like rubber, can cause embarrassment

You will be able to

  • describe your school day from start to finish
  • describe your classroom and your school building
  • talk about your duties and your timetable

Common mistakes to watch for

  • To an American colleague: Can I borrow your rubber? Can I borrow your eraser? (In the UK a rubber is an eraser, but in American English it is also slang for a condom. If you are not sure, say eraser.)
  • Half-term is a short test in the middle of the term. Half-term is a week of holiday in the middle of a term (UK), not a test.
  • Who makes the attendance today? Who takes attendance today? (US) / Who takes the register? (UK)
  • Lunch is in 12:15. Lunch is at 12:15.
  • Please don't talk while the test. Please don't talk during the test. (while + subject + verb: while you are writing)
  • Lessons are starting at 8:50 every day. Lessons start at 8:50 every day. (present simple for timetables)
