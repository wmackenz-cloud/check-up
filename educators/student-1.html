What this lesson is for

Why this matters: when you work abroad or with teachers from other countries, you need to explain your school system clearly. American and British English use different words for the same stages, so knowing both avoids confusion.

You will know

  • US and UK names for school stages: elementary / primary school, high school / secondary school, sixth form
  • how grades and years compare: US ninth grade = UK Year 10
  • types of school: public / state school, private / independent school

You will understand

  • why public school means something very different in the US and in England
  • when to say go to school (no article) and when to say the school (one building)

You will be able to

  • describe the school system in your country
  • describe the school where you teach
  • talk about schools naturally: teach at, be in Year 9, a class of eleven-year-olds

Common mistakes to watch for

  • My children go to a public school in London, so we pay no fees. My children go to a state school in London. (In England, a few very old, expensive private schools, such as Eton, are called public schools. In the US, a public school is free.)
  • My son is on Year 9. My son is in Year 9. / He is in ninth grade.
  • She goes to the school by bus. She goes to school by bus. (no the for school as an activity)
  • a class of twelve-years-old students a class of twelve-year-old students / students aged twelve
  • She graduated from secondary school in Leeds. She left school in Leeds. (In the UK, graduate is mainly for university.)
  • My daughter is in second class. My daughter is in second grade (US) / Year 3 (UK). (In England the Year number is one higher than the US grade.)

Warm-up

  • What kind of school do you teach at, or did you last teach at? How old are the students?
  • At what age do children start school in your country? When can they leave?
  • Do you know any American or British school words from films, series or books? Which ones?

Easy rule: in England the Year number is one higher than the US grade. A US first grader is in Year 2. In the US, the four years of high school are freshman, sophomore, junior and senior year.

In Canada (where Bill lives), most words are American: elementary school, high school, grade 9. But Canadians say school board for the district, and many say marks for grades.

Say it right: KINdergarten (with a t: kinder-gar-ten), SECondary, PRImary, indePENdent, comPULsory, auTHORity, DIStrict. University starts with a /j/ sound, so say a university, not an university.

Language point: talking about schools

PatternExample
teach at / work at + a schoolI teach at a state primary school in Lyon. (teach in a school is also fine)
teach + a year groupI teach Year 6. (UK) I teach sixth grade. / I teach sixth graders. (US)
be in + a year or gradeMy son is in Year 9. (UK) My son is in ninth grade / in grade 9. (US)
no article for school as an activitygo to school, start school, leave school, at school; UK go to university, at university; US go to college, in college
the for one particular school or buildingThe school is next to the park. I went to the school to meet the head teacher.
ages: aged 11 to 16, an eleven-year-olda school for students aged 11 to 16; a class of eleven-year-olds; an eleven-year-old student (no -s in year before a noun)

Subject: Welcome to Westbrook: how our school works

Dear Daniel,

We are so pleased that you are joining us on the teacher exchange in January. You asked how our school compares with your high school in Austin, so here is a short guide.

Westbrook is a state secondary school for students aged 11 to 18. It is an academy, so it is paid for by the government, but it is not run by the local authority. We have about 1,200 students, from Year 7 to Year 13.

Most students take their GCSE exams at the end of Year 11, when they are 16. About 300 then stay on in our sixth form for two more years to study for A levels. Others leave and go to a college of further education.

Remember that our Year numbers are one higher than your grades. Your ninth graders would be in Year 10 here, and your seniors would be in Year 13, which we also call the upper sixth.

You will teach two Year 9 history classes and one Year 12 group. The younger students call their teachers “Sir” or “Miss”, so don't be surprised!

Best wishes,
Helen

Exercise 8: Prepositions: at, in or to?

Part A: at, in or to?

Watch out

  • at: a place or a point in time: teach at a school, at university (UK), at the age of six, at the end of Year 11
  • in: inside a year group or stage: in Year 9, in ninth grade, in the sixth form, in college (US)
  • to: movement towards: go to school, move up to secondary school, send their children to a private school
