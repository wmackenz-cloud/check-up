Teacher Notes 5: Assessment, Grading and Reports

Open the student page

Purpose: give teachers the language of assessment, marking and reports in American and British English (grade / mark, grade papers / mark work, rubric / mark scheme, proctor / invigilator, report card / school report), the exam verbs that cause most mistakes (take / sit vs pass), and phrases for clear, kind report comments.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student how marks work in their system (scale, pass mark, main exams), and if possible for an anonymous example of a report or report comment they wrote.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three questions. Listen for pass an exam (meaning take) and notes (meaning marks).
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Draw the student's own grading scale next to US letter grades (A to F) and English GCSE grades (9 to 1). Then go through the three tables.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (7 min). US or UK, then partners.
  4. Language point and Exercise 3 (7 min). Take / sit / pass / fail, feedback, make progress.
  5. Reading (8 min). Nadia's report. Ask the student to find the positive phrases and the phrases for areas to improve.
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 2 as a role-play (you are the unhappy student), then question 4.
  7. Prepositions and wrap-up (3 min). Exercise 8 Part A together. Exercises 4, 5 and 8 Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • assessment /əˈsesmənt/: stress on SESS.
  • rubric /ˈruːbrɪk/: stress on RU.
  • invigilator /ɪnˈvɪdʒɪleɪtə/: stress on VIG; the g is /dʒ/.
  • proctor /ˈprɑːktər/ (US): stress on PROC.
  • resit: verb /ˌriːˈsɪt/ (stress on SIT), noun /ˈriːsɪt/ (stress on RE). The same pattern as record, present.
  • grade /ɡreɪd/ and graded /ˈɡreɪdɪd/: say the final /d/ and the extra syllable in graded.

Getting the US and UK pairs right

  • grade / mark: British teachers use grade for final exam results (an A level grade, a GCSE grade 7) and mark for scores on work (a mark out of 20). American teachers use grade for both, and score for test points.
  • SAT / A levels and GPA are not true equivalents. Present them as “the closest thing” and explain the difference.
  • transcript: British schools do not usually issue transcripts; students show their exam certificates. Universities in both countries issue transcripts.
  • revise: British revise for an exam = study again. American revise = change and improve (revise an essay). Americans say review or study.
  • half-term (UK) is a holiday, not an exam. US midterm is an exam in the middle of a semester.

False friends and common mistakes

  • pass an exam (French passer un examen = take it; Polish zdawać = take, zdać = pass): in English pass only means succeed.
  • note (Spanish and Portuguese nota, French note; Italian voto, Polish ocena): say mark (UK) or grade (US). Italian voto is not a vote.
  • approve / suspend (Spanish aprobar = pass, suspender = fail; Portuguese aprovar = pass, reprovar = fail): say pass and fail.
  • control (French contrôle, Spanish control) and proof (Portuguese prova, Italian prova): say test. French surveiller an exam = invigilate (UK) / proctor (US).
  • assist / correct: Spanish asistir, Italian assistere = attend. Assist means help. Teachers can correct mistakes, but for scoring work say mark or grade.
  • feedbacks: feedback is used as a loanword in many languages and often gets a plural. In English it is uncountable.
  • Vietnamese: điểm = mark / grade / score (usually 0 to 10), bài kiểm tra = test, thi = exam, học bạ = school record. Watch final consonants: mark, test, grade, and plurals: marks, exams.

Report comments are a real task for most teachers. If the student writes reports in English (for example, in an international school), talk through one of their real comments together in the lesson.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • How students are assessed. Follow up: “What percentage is the final exam worth?” “Can students resit?”
  • Unhappy student. Coach the stages: listen, show the mark scheme / rubric, point to one strength, explain what was missing, agree a next step.
  • Are exams the best way? Push for a reason and an example. Useful: On the one hand ..., on the other hand ...
  • Interview question. Coach a concrete answer: a quiz or exit ticket, what it showed, what the teacher changed next lesson.

Notes on the exercises

  • Exercise 1: quiz and retake are used in both varieties, so they are not in the sort.
  • Exercise 2: take a test is used in the UK too; sit is the extra British option.
  • Exercise 3: items 1 and 2 test sit / take against pass. Ask the student to explain the difference in their own words.
  • Exercise 6: follow-up: “Rewrite one sentence for an American parent.” (mark scheme → rubric, revise → review)
  • Exercise 8: Part A shows the US on the quiz and the UK in the test. Both are fine in their own variety.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: US English: grade papers, rubric, proctor, report card, passing grade, review for a test. UK English: mark work, mark scheme, invigilator, school report, pass mark, revise for an exam.
  • Exercise 2: grade = mark; take a test = sit a test; retake = resit; final exam = end-of-year exam; practice test = mock exam; standardized test = national test; quiz = short test; flunk = fail.
  • Exercise 3: 1 sit, 2 passed, 3 out of, 4 feedback, 5 revise, 6 made, 7 invigilate, 8 grading.
  • Exercise 4: 1 rubric, 2 report card, 3 invigilator, 4 mock, 5 resit, 6 transcript, 7 quiz, 8 standardized.
  • Exercise 5: 1 She got a high mark in her history exam. 2 When do your students sit their final exams? 3 I give feedback on every piece of homework. 4 You need half the marks to pass this test. 5 He has made good progress this term.
  • Exercise 6: 1 6, 2 80, 3 points, 4 scheme, 5 often.
  • Exercise 8: Part A: on: feedback on the essay, a B on the quiz (US), comment on her work. in: progress in reading, an A in history, a mistake in question 3. for: revise for the exam, lose marks for spelling, prepare for a test, study for a quiz. Part B: 1 Tomorrow my students will take the exam at 9:00. 2 She made good progress in writing this year. 3 Thanks for all your feedback on my lesson plan. 4 No mistake. 5 My students got very good marks in the test.
