Teacher Notes 4: Curriculum, Planning and Teaching

Open the student page

Purpose: give teachers the language to explain what they teach and how they plan it, in American and British English (math / maths, unit plan / scheme of work, state standards / National Curriculum, PD / CPD, PPA time), and the collocations that make teaching talk sound natural (teach someone to, explain something to someone, cover a topic, set homework).

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student to bring one real lesson plan or unit plan, in any language. Translating its headings into English is a strong extra task.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three questions. Listen for learn / teach, explain me and a training.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Subjects first (the student's subjects in both varieties), then the planning table. Ask: “What do you call this in your school?”
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (7 min). US or UK, then meanings.
  4. Language point and Exercise 3 (7 min). Teach / learn, explain to, and the planning verbs.
  5. Reading (8 min). Joanna's department email. Ask the student to underline all the planning words, then answer.
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 1, using the student's own plan, then question 4 as an interview.
  7. Prepositions and wrap-up (3 min). Exercise 8 Part A together. Exercises 4, 5, 8 Part B and the homework at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • curriculum /kəˈrɪkjələm/: stress on RIC. Plural curricula /kəˈrɪkjələ/ or curriculums.
  • differentiation /ˌdɪfərenʃiˈeɪʃn/: stress on A; many students stop halfway. Practise it in chunks: diffe-ren-ti-A-tion.
  • scheme /skiːm/: the ch is /k/, as in school.
  • maths /mæθs/: the cluster /θs/ is hard. Math /mæθ/ (US) is easier.
  • objective /əbˈdʒektɪv/: stress on JEC.
  • INSET /ˈɪnset/: said as a word, stress on IN.

Getting the US and UK pairs right

  • social studies / humanities is a rough match. In UK secondary schools history, geography and RE are usually separate subjects; humanities is the group name. Humanities in the US usually means university subjects such as literature and philosophy.
  • state standards / National Curriculum: the National Curriculum is for England (Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own). US standards differ by state; many states use the Common Core for math and English language arts.
  • set / assign homework: both are understood everywhere, but British teachers usually set and American teachers usually assign.
  • classroom management / behaviour management: both terms are used in both countries; behaviour management is more common in UK policy documents.
  • exit ticket / plenary: a plenary is the review stage at the end of a British lesson; an exit ticket is one kind of plenary.

False friends and common mistakes

  • learn / teach (Polish uczyć = teach but uczyć się = learn; French apprendre can mean both): I teach my students, never I learn my students.
  • explain me (Spanish explícame, Italian spiegami, French explique-moi, Portuguese explica-me): say explain it to me.
  • a training (Spanish un entrenamiento / una formación, French une formation, Italian una formazione, Polish szkolenie): training is uncountable. Say a training course or some training. French formation is not English formation.
  • programme / programa (Spanish programa, Polish program nauczania, Italian programma): for the content of a course say syllabus or curriculum.
  • argument (Italian argomento, Portuguese assunto): say topic. Argument in English is a disagreement or a line of reasoning.
  • materia / matière / materia (Spanish, French, Italian): say subject, not matter or material.
  • Vietnamese: giáo án = lesson plan, chương trình = curriculum or syllabus, môn học = subject. Watch the cluster in maths, objectives, texts, and the past tense in describing a lesson (yesterday I teach).

Many teachers already know the planning terms in their own language from teacher training. Let them explain a concept first, then give the English term; it builds confidence.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • A recent lesson. Follow up: “What would you change next time?” Push past simple for the lesson and would for changes.
  • Who decides? Useful: the ministry of education sets ..., schools can choose ..., teachers have (no) freedom to ...
  • Curriculum freedom. If the student knows sp-4 (A.R.E.), ask for an answer, a reason and an example.
  • Interview question. Coach a concrete answer: one class, one task, three levels of support, how they checked it worked.

Notes on the exercises

  • Exercise 1: elective (US) has its UK partner option in the vocabulary table; INSET day (UK) has in-service day. Ask for both.
  • Exercise 3: items 1 and 2 are the two biggest errors in this lesson. Spend time there.
  • Exercise 4: item 8 accepts planning or prep.
  • Exercise 6: follow-up: “What would you add to Joanna's email for your own department?”
  • Exercise 8: Part B item 5 changes a training into a training course; also accept some training orally.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: US English: math, unit plan, PD, planning period, state standards, elective. UK English: maths, scheme of work, CPD, PPA time, National Curriculum, INSET day.
  • Exercise 2: scheme of work = a plan for a series of lessons on one topic; learning objective = what students should learn by the end of the lesson; differentiation = adapting a lesson for students with different needs; INSET day = a training day for teachers with no students in school; PPA time = time in the timetable to plan and mark; elective = a subject students choose; exit ticket = a short task at the end of a lesson to check learning; National Curriculum = the official list of what students in England must learn.
  • Exercise 3: 1 teach, 2 to, 3 covering, 4 objective, 5 set, 6 on, 7 for, 8 needs.
  • Exercise 4: 1 scheme, 2 standards, 3 elective, 4 maths, 5 differentiation, 6 CPD, 7 curriculum, 8 planning.
  • Exercise 5: 1 I teach teenagers how to write formal emails. 2 This unit covers the causes of the First World War. 3 What are the learning objectives for this lesson? 4 We have an INSET day on the first Monday of term. 5 The lesson plan links clearly to the curriculum.
  • Exercise 6: 1 9, 2 five, 3 ticket, 4 extension, 5 marking. The writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 8: Part A: on: a lesson on fractions, focus on speaking, based on the standards, work on a project. to: link it to the curriculum, explain the task to the class, introduce the topic to Year 7. for: a course for new teachers, materials for Year 6, prepare students for the exam. Part B: 1 Last year I taught Year 10 biology. 2 Can you explain to me the new scheme of work? 3 Today's lesson focused on the past simple. 4 No mistake. 5 Next week I'm doing a training course about AI in the classroom.
