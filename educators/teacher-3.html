Teacher Notes 3: The Classroom and the School Day

Open the student page

Purpose: help teachers describe their school day, their routines and their classroom in American and British English (schedule / timetable, recess / break, take attendance / take the register, hallway / corridor), with accurate time prepositions and the present simple for routines.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student to bring their real timetable (a photo or a screenshot is fine) and, if they are happy to, a photo of their classroom. They give you the material for the speaking tasks.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three questions. Listen for time prepositions and the present simple -s.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Go through the school day table in time order, from homeroom / registration to dismissal / home time. Then the classroom words: ask the student to point at things in their own room or picture.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (7 min). US or UK, then partners. Mention the rubber / eraser warning briefly and move on.
  4. Language point and Exercise 3 (7 min). at / on / in / during, and routines in the present simple.
  5. Reading (8 min). The American newsletter. Ask the student to underline every US word and give the UK word.
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 1 using the student's own timetable, then question 2 with their photo.
  7. Prepositions and wrap-up (3 min). Exercise 8 Part A together. Exercises 4, 5 and 8 Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • schedule /ˈskedʒuːl/ (US), /ˈʃedjuːl/ (UK, though many younger British speakers also say /ˈskedʒuːl/).
  • recess /ˈriːses/: stress on RE in American school use.
  • cafeteria /ˌkæfəˈtɪəriə/: stress on TER.
  • register /ˈredʒɪstə/: stress on REG; the g is /dʒ/.
  • attendance /əˈtendəns/: stress on TEN.
  • corridor /ˈkɒrɪdɔː/: stress on COR.
  • canteen /kænˈtiːn/: stress on TEEN.

Getting the US and UK pairs right

  • period / lesson: both varieties use period for a slot in the timetable. British teachers usually say lesson for the teaching itself: I've got Year 9 in period 3; it's a double lesson.
  • semester / term: not an exact match. Many US schools have two semesters (some have quarters or trimesters). UK schools have three terms (autumn, spring, summer), each with a half-term holiday.
  • restroom: in US schools, bathroom is the most common word. Canada: washroom.
  • playtime is for primary schools; secondary schools say break. In the UK, lunch in primary schools is sometimes dinner time, and the staff who supervise it are dinner ladies or lunchtime supervisors.
  • rubber: in American English it is slang for a condom. Mention it once, plainly, and recommend eraser.

False friends and common mistakes

  • while / during (Spanish durante / mientras, Italian durante / mentre, French pendant, Portuguese durante / enquanto): learners often say while the lesson. During + noun; while + subject and verb.
  • make / pass the register (Spanish pasar lista, Italian fare l'appello, French faire l'appel, Portuguese fazer a chamada): say take the register (UK) or take attendance (US).
  • lesson / lecture (Italian lezione, French leçon; Spanish lectura and Italian lettura = reading): a school class is a lesson. A lecture is a long talk, usually at university.
  • classroom / class (Spanish clase, Polish klasa): klasa and clase can mean the room, the group or the year. In English: classroom = room, class = group or lesson, Year 8 / eighth grade = year.
  • recreation (Spanish recreo, Italian ricreazione, French récréation, Portuguese recreio): say break (UK) or recess (US).
  • Polish: przerwa = break; dyżur = duty (I'm on duty, not I have a duty). Dziennik is the register.
  • Vietnamese: giờ ra chơi = break / recess; điểm danh = take the register / attendance; tiết = period. Watch third person -s in routines (the lesson start) and final consonants: break, lunch, desk.

If the student teaches online, ask them to describe their working day and their home teaching space instead: desk, webcam, headset, second screen, whiteboard app. The time language is the same.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • A typical day. Follow up: “What do you usually do at break?” “Are you ever on duty?” Push present simple and frequency adverbs.
  • Your classroom. Follow up: “What's on your walls?” “How do you arrange the desks, and why?”
  • Easiest and hardest. Useful: The hardest part of the day is ..., because ...
  • Interview question. Coach a clear routine answer: greeting at the door, a starter task on the board, taking the register while students work.

Notes on the exercises

  • Exercise 1: schedule is also British for plans in general (a busy schedule), but a school's lesson plan is a timetable in the UK.
  • Exercise 2: semester and term are the closest partners, not an exact match. Say so.
  • Exercise 3: item 4 (during, not while) is a very common mistake.
  • Exercise 4: the (US) and (UK) labels tell the student which word to use.
  • Exercise 6: the newsletter is American; the first two answers accept 8:05 and 8.05 (the British style with a dot).
  • Exercise 8: on duty is not a time, but it often appears in the same sentences, so it sits with on.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: US English: schedule, recess, hallway, eraser, restroom, field trip. UK English: timetable, playtime, corridor, rubber, the toilets, school trip.
  • Exercise 2: take attendance = take the register; homeroom = registration; cafeteria = canteen; backpack = rucksack; bulletin board = noticeboard; trash can = bin; lost and found = lost property; semester = term.
  • Exercise 3: 1 at, 2 on, 3 in, 4 during, 5 take, 6 at, 7 on, 8 starts.
  • Exercise 4: 1 timetable, 2 register, 3 corridor, 4 period, 5 recess, 6 cafeteria, 7 field trip, 8 noticeboard.
  • Exercise 5: 1 I take the register at the start of every lesson. 2 We have a twenty-minute break at half past ten. 3 Which period do you teach on Wednesday? 4 My classroom is at the end of the corridor. 5 I am on lunch duty twice a week.
  • Exercise 6: 1 8:05, 2 3:10, 3 cafeteria, 4 slip, 5 charity.
  • Exercise 8: Part A: at: at 8:40, at lunchtime, at the end of the day. on: on Monday mornings, on 6 October, be on duty. in: in the afternoon, in the summer term, in September, in the first lesson. Part B: 1 I take the register every morning at 8:45. 2 We have a break at 10:30. 3 Students can't use the toilets during the lesson. 4 No mistake. 5 The first lesson starts at 9:00 every day.
