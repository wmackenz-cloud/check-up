What this lesson is for

Why this matters: tests, marks and reports are a big part of every teacher's work. You need to talk about them clearly with colleagues and parents, and American and British schools often use different words.

You will know

  • US and UK words for marking: grade / mark, grade papers / mark work, rubric / mark scheme
  • words for exams: quiz, mock exam, proctor / invigilator, retake / resit
  • words for records: report card / school report, transcript

You will understand

  • the difference between take an exam (do it) and pass an exam (succeed)
  • why feedback has no plural

You will be able to

  • explain how students are assessed in your school
  • talk about results: 18 out of 20, an A in history
  • write a positive, useful report comment

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Her ten-year-old is studying for the SAT to get into university. In England, SATs are national tests for 10 to 11-year-olds at the end of primary school. In the US, the SAT is a test for university entry.
  • She got good notes in the test. She got good marks (UK, Canada) / grades (US). (In American English, grade is also a school year: She got a good grade in fifth grade.)
  • My students will pass the exam tomorrow at 9:00. My students will take (UK also: sit) the exam tomorrow. (pass means succeed)
  • Thank you for your feedbacks. Thank you for your feedback. (no plural)
  • He did a lot of progress. He made a lot of progress.
  • To American students: Revise for the test tonight! Study (or review) for the test tonight! (In American English, revise means change and improve a text.)

Warm-up

  • How are students assessed in your school: tests, projects, exams, or all of these?
  • How many hours a week do you spend marking or grading?
  • What scale do you use for marks in your country: 1 to 10, A to F, percentages, or something else?

Say it right: asSESSment, RUbric, inVIGilator, PROCtor, TRANscript, STANdardized, FEEDback. The verb is reSIT; the noun is a REsit.

Language point: exam verbs and feedback

PatternExample
take (US, UK) / sit (UK) an exam = do itMy students sit their exams in June.
pass an exam = succeed; fail = not succeedShe took the exam on Monday, and she passed!
retake / resit (UK) an examHe can resit the maths exam in November.
a score out ofShe got 18 out of 20. The test is marked out of 50.
a grade on a test (US) / a mark in a test (UK); an A in a subjectHe got a B on the quiz. (US) He got a good mark in the test. (UK) She got an A in history.
feedback is uncountable: give feedback onI give feedback on every essay. (not feedbacks, not a feedback; say a piece of feedback)
make progress in (not do progress)He has made great progress in reading this term.

Exercise 8: Prepositions: on, in or for?

Part A: on, in or for?

Watch out

  • on: feedback and comments on work; US a grade on a test
  • in: progress or results in a subject or skill: progress in reading, an A in history
  • for: the reason or the purpose: revise for the exam, lose marks for spelling, prepare for a test
