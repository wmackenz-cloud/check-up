Teacher Notes 2: People and Roles in a School

Open the student page

Purpose: give teachers the job titles for the people in a school in American and British English, and the verbs and prepositions to explain who does what (be responsible for, be in charge of, report to, work as, cover, talk to someone about).

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus homework.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: ask the student for their own job title and their manager's, in their language. If possible, get a staff list or the “Our team” page of their school website. Real names and roles make the speaking much better.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three questions. Note the job titles the student translates word for word (the director, the responsible).
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Build a quick chart of a school together: leadership at the top, teachers, then support staff. Write the US and UK title for each box.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (7 min). US or UK, then who does what.
  4. Language point and Exercise 3 (7 min). Focus on responsible for, in charge of, report to and work as.
  5. Reading (8 min). Ruth's welcome email. Ask the student to change each UK title to the US one, then answer the questions.
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 2 is the core: you play the experienced colleague. Then question 4 as an interview.
  7. Prepositions and wrap-up (3 min). Exercise 8 Part A together. Exercises 4, 5, 8 Part B and the homework at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • principal /ˈprɪnsəpl/: the same sound as principle. Stress on PRIN.
  • deputy /ˈdepjuti/: stress on DEP; the u is /ju/.
  • substitute /ˈsʌbstɪtjuːt/ (UK), /ˈsʌbstɪtuːt/ (US): stress on SUB.
  • aide /eɪd/: one syllable, the same as aid.
  • custodian /kʌˈstəʊdiən/: stress on TO.
  • faculty /ˈfæklti/: three syllables, stress on FAC.
  • SENCo /ˈsenkəʊ/: said as a word, not as letters.

Getting the US and UK pairs right

  • school board / board of governors: a US school board is elected and runs a whole district; a UK board of governors oversees one school. In Canada, the school board is like a US district.
  • vice principal and assistant principal both exist in the US; deputy head and assistant head both exist in the UK. The deputy is usually more senior than the assistant.
  • SENCo is a legal role in English schools (now often SENDCo, with D for disabilities). US schools have special education teachers and case managers; the title special education coordinator is used in some districts.
  • pupil sounds old-fashioned or very British to Americans. Student is safe everywhere.
  • counselor / counsellor: the American spelling has one l; British and Canadian spelling has two.

False friends and common mistakes

  • the responsible (Spanish el responsable, French le responsable, Italian il responsabile, Portuguese o responsável): in English say the person in charge or the job title. Responsible is not a noun.
  • director (Spanish director, Portuguese diretor, Polish dyrektor, French directeur): in a school say principal (US) or head teacher (UK). Director of studies is used in language schools. Italian preside and French proviseur / principal are also the head.
  • professor (Spanish profesor, Portuguese professor, Italian professore, French professeur): in English a school teacher is a teacher. Professor is for universities.
  • work like a teacher (Spanish trabajar como, Italian lavorare come, Portuguese trabalhar como, Polish pracować jako): say work as a teacher.
  • tutor: Spanish tutor, Italian tutor and Polish wychowawca are close to UK form tutor or US homeroom teacher. In English, a tutor alone often means a private teacher.
  • pedagogue (Polish pedagog szkolny): say school counsellor. English pedagogue is rare and sounds old-fashioned or negative.
  • Vietnamese: hiệu trưởng = principal / head teacher, hiệu phó = vice principal / deputy head, giáo viên chủ nhiệm = homeroom teacher / form tutor (a very close match). Watch the final consonants in aide, head, staff and the plural -s in teachers, assistants.

Ask the student to use the right article with titles. She's a teacher, She's the head teacher, She's head of science are all correct. Many learners leave out the article (She is teacher), especially Polish and Vietnamese speakers.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • School chart. Follow up: “Who do you go to when there's a problem?” “How often do you meet your line manager?”
  • Who do I talk to? Push the full question with the preposition at the end: Who do I talk to about ...? Then the answer: You need to speak to ..., because she's in charge of ...
  • Best colleague. Follow up: “What makes them good at their job?” Useful: She's great with ..., he's very organized.
  • Interview question. Coach a short answer: two people or teams, what you do together, one example.

Notes on the exercises

  • Exercise 1: all twelve are common job titles. After sorting, ask for the partner of each title.
  • Exercise 2: titles are British, with the US title in the explanation. Ask the student for the US title each time.
  • Exercise 3: item 6 (work as) is the most common mistake for Romance language speakers.
  • Exercise 4: item 5 accepts both spellings, counselor and counsellor. Point out which is which.
  • Exercise 6: follow-up: “Draw Marco's chart. Who does he go to for what?”
  • Exercise 8: meet with is American; in British English you just meet someone. Item 5 in Part B replaces three words: the responsible of becomes in charge of.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: US English: principal, vice principal, substitute teacher, teacher's aide, homeroom teacher, custodian. UK English: head teacher, deputy head, supply teacher, teaching assistant, form tutor, caretaker.
  • Exercise 2: principal / head teacher = is in charge of the whole school; deputy head = helps lead the school and often deals with behaviour; supply teacher = takes classes when a teacher is off sick; teaching assistant = works with small groups in the classroom; form tutor = checks on one class every morning; SENCo = organizes support for students with special needs; head of department = leads one subject team and plans its budget; caretaker = looks after the building and fixes small problems.
  • Exercise 3: 1 for, 2 to, 3 of, 4 cover, 5 to, 6 as, 7 with, 8 custodian.
  • Exercise 4: 1 supply, 2 principal, 3 deputy, 4 aide, 5 counselor, 6 governors, 7 faculty, 8 pupils.
  • Exercise 5: 1 Who do I talk to about a student's reading? 2 She is responsible for special educational needs. 3 I report to the head of department. 4 Could you cover my class on Thursday? 5 Our teaching assistant works with small groups.
  • Exercise 6: 1 Walsh, 2 morning, 3 7:30, 4 plans, 5 caretaker. The writing task is checked by Claude.
  • Exercise 8: Part A: to: report to the head teacher, talk to the SENCo, send the letter to parents. for: responsible for attendance, cover for a colleague, apply for a head of year job. with: work with the teaching assistant, deal with a complaint, help with the reading groups, meet with the parents (US). Part B: 1 She works as a supply teacher in London. 2 The deputy head is responsible for attendance. 3 No mistake. 4 The faculty are meeting at 4:00 to discuss the timetable. 5 Who is in charge of Year 9?
