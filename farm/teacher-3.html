Teacher Notes 3: Farm Machines and Tools

Open the student page

Purpose: give general learners the names of common farm machines and hand tools (tractor, plough, combine harvester, baler; spade, rake, hoe, pitchfork, wheelbarrow) and the language of purpose: is used to + verb, is used for + -ing, to, in order to and so that. The reading is a farm open-day leaflet.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: find photos of three or four farm machines (a tractor, a combine, a baler) and a few garden tools; a garden centre website or a toy catalogue works well. Ask the student to have one tool from home ready to show on camera.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. The third (describe a kitchen tool without its name) shows you how the student already talks about purpose.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Machines first, in the order of the farming year: plough, harrow, seed drill, sprayer, combine, baler. Then the hand tools, ideally with real objects or photos. Then the purpose phrases.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the purpose phrases. In Exercise 2, ask the student to say each sentence a second way: used to remove → used for removing.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one together and set the other for home.
  5. Reading (8 min). The open-day leaflet. First ask: “Would you go? What would you most like to see?” Then the questions. Point out so that it can drive and horses were used to pull it.
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 1 (the guessing game) is the core and good fun: take turns. Then Question 2 or 4.
  7. Purpose and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A together; Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • plough /plaʊ/: rhymes with cow. The -ough spelling confuses everyone (though, through, tough); the US/Canada spelling plow shows the sound.
  • combine: the machine is /ˈkɒmbaɪn/ (stress first), the verb to combine is /kəmˈbaɪn/ (stress second).
  • harrow /ˈhærəʊ/: like arrow with an h. hoe /həʊ/: like go.
  • shovel /ˈʃʌvəl/: the o sounds like love, not hot.
  • shears /ʃɪəz/: rhymes with ears. wheelbarrow /ˈwiːlbærəʊ/: stress on wheel.
  • baler /ˈbeɪlə/, bales /beɪlz/: like sail, not ball.

False friends and common mistakes

  • for + verb: Spanish para, French pour, Italian per, Portuguese para + infinitive lead to I came for learn or for to learn. In English, purpose is to + verb; for takes a noun or -ing (for digging, for hay).
  • is used to + verb (a tool's job) vs be used to + -ing (accustomed): A hoe is used to remove weeds vs I'm used to getting up early. Mention it only if the student mixes them.
  • so that needs a subject and verb: so that it stays dry, so that we can finish. In order to and to need just a verb.
  • spade vs shovel: a spade digs (flat, sharp edge); a shovel moves loose things (wide, curved). In everyday North American English, people often say shovel for both.
  • machinery, equipment are uncountable: farm machinery, new equipment. Not machineries or an equipment. Say a machine or a piece of equipment.
  • shears (and scissors) are always plural: these shears are sharp, a pair of shears.

Adapting the lesson

ElementB1B2
VocabularyFocus on tractor, trailer, plough, combine, and spade, shovel, rake, wheelbarrow, bucket. Phrases: used to + verb, used for + -ing, to + verb.All words; add cultivator, mower, irrigation system, drone, auger, and so as to, with the aim of, which allows farmers to.
SpeakingThe guessing game with everyday tools; say what three tools at home are used for.Explain a modern machine (a milking robot, a drone) to someone who has never seen one, or discuss whether technology is good for small farms.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Guess the tool. Take turns. Follow up with questions: “Is it made of metal?” “Do you use it outside?” “What else is it used for?” Listen for used for + verb.
  • Machines then and now. Follow up: “What did farmers use before tractors?” (horses, oxen) “What jobs did people do by hand?” Good for were used to pull.
  • Drive a tractor or a combine? Follow up: “What do you think is the hardest part?” “Why do farmers sometimes work all night at harvest time?” (to finish before the rain)
  • Self-driving tractors and drones. Follow up: “What are drones used for on a farm?” (checking crops, spraying, finding lost sheep) “Who wins and who loses?”

Answers

  • Exercise 1: tractor = pulls machines and trailers; plough = turns over the soil; harrow = breaks the soil into small pieces; seed drill = puts seeds into the ground in rows; sprayer = sprays crops with water or chemicals; combine harvester = cuts grain and separates it from the straw; baler = makes hay into bales; wheelbarrow = carries soil or manure by hand.
  • Exercise 2: 1 to remove, 2 collecting, 3 to, 4 so that, 5 in order to, 6 for, 7 protect, 8 to cut.
  • Exercise 3: 1 spade, 2 pitchfork, 3 bucket, 4 tractor, 5 trailer, 6 baler, 7 shovel, 8 plough.
  • Exercise 4: 1 A harrow is used to break up the soil. 2 What is this machine used for? 3 She wears gloves so that she doesn't cut her hands. 4 The combine harvester cuts the wheat in August. 5 We need a wheelbarrow to move all this soil.
  • Exercise 5: 1 tractor, 2 lines, 3 barley, 4 horses, 5 muddy.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: is used to ...: dig holes, carry soil, collect leaves, lift hay, remove weeds. is used for ...: digging holes, carrying soil, collecting leaves, lifting hay, removing weeds. Part B: 1 A spade is used for digging holes. 2 He bought a trailer to carry the bales. 3 No mistake. 4 The sprayer is used to protect the crops. 5 Farmers use a combine to harvest wheat.
