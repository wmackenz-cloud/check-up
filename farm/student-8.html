What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • understand and use farm idioms in everyday conversation
  • compare life in the country and life in the city
  • give your opinion about where you would prefer to live, with reasons

You will know

  • make hay while the sun shines, hold your horses
  • rural, urban, countryside, commute
  • quieter than, not as busy as, the most peaceful

You will understand

  • cheaper, more expensive (not more cheaper)
  • fixed idioms: till the cows come home

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Houses in the country are more cheaper. Houses in the country are cheaper. (no more with -er)
  • The village is not as busy than the city. The village is not as busy as the city.
  • Life here is more good. Life here is better.
  • Don't count your chicken before they hatch. Don't count your chickens before they hatch.
  • We talked until the cows go home. We talked till the cows came home.
  • I want to live in a country. I want to live in the country. (the country = outside towns; a country = a nation)

Warm-up

  • Do you live in a city, a town or a village? Do you like it?
  • Do you know any English idioms with animals in them? What do they mean?
  • If you could live anywhere for one year, would you choose a farm or a big city?

Comparing places

PatternRuleExample
-er thanshort adjectives: quiet, cheap, safeThe village is quieter than the city.
-ier thanadjectives ending in -y: busy, noisy, friendlyThe city is busier and noisier.
more ... thanlong adjectives: expensive, peaceful, crowdedToronto is more expensive than our village.
better, worse, furtherirregular: good, bad, farThe roads are worse, but the air is better.
not as ... asless than, said politelyShopping is not as easy as in the city.
much / a lot / a bit + comparativehow big the difference isIt's much cheaper and a bit colder.
the most ... / the -estthe top of the groupIt's the most peaceful place I know.

Exercise 5: Reading

From Toronto to the farm: one year later

A year ago, my husband and I left our apartment in downtown Toronto and bought a small farm near Owen Sound. Our friends said we were mad. “Hold your horses,” my mother said. “You don't know anything about farming!”

She was right. In the first month we put the cart before the horse: we bought twelve hens before we built a henhouse. Then we spent a whole day on a wild goose chase, driving from town to town to find chicken wire.

But life here is much better than I expected. It's quieter than the city, of course, and the air is cleaner. The nearest supermarket is thirty kilometres away, so shopping is not as easy as it was in Toronto. My husband still works online, so he brings home the bacon, and I look after the animals.

The best thing is the community. Our neighbours helped us bring in the hay in July. “Make hay while the sun shines,” they laughed. In the city, I didn't even know my neighbours' names.

Exercise 7: Comparatives and mistakes

Watch out

  • -er / -ier: short adjectives: quiet → quieter, busy → busier

  • irregular: good → better, bad → worse, far → further
