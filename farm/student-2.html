What this lesson is for

Why this matters: when you visit the countryside, book a farm stay or follow a walking map, you need the names of farm buildings and land, and small words like across, through and along to explain where things are and how to get there.

You will know

  • farm buildings: farmhouse, farmyard, barn, stable, pigsty, henhouse, silo, shed
  • farm land: field, meadow, pasture, orchard, hedge, fence, gate, stile, pond, lane
  • prepositions of place and movement: behind, opposite, between, across, through, along, over, past

You will understand

  • the difference between across (from one side to the other), through (into and out of) and along (following a line)
  • why we say behind the barn, not behind of the barn, but in front of the barn

You will be able to

  • describe where the buildings on a farm are
  • give and follow simple directions in the countryside
  • describe a walk: We went through the gate, across the field and along the river.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • The barn is behind of the house. The barn is behind the house.
  • The pond is in front the farmhouse. The pond is in front of the farmhouse.
  • The shop is opposite of the church. The shop is opposite the church. (US/Canada also: across from the church)
  • We walked along the field to the other side. We walked across the field to the other side.
  • The cows are in the pastor. The cows are in the pasture. (a pastor works in a church)
  • My uncle lives in a farm. My uncle lives on a farm.

Warm-up

  • Think of a farm you have seen, in real life, on TV or in a children's book. What buildings were there?
  • Have you ever stayed on a farm or in a cottage in the countryside? Would you like to?
  • Describe the way from your home to the nearest park or shop. Which words do you use: past, along, across, through?

Key vocabulary

Farm buildings

WordWhat it isExample
farmhousethe house where the farmer's family livesThe farmhouse is two hundred years old.
farmyardthe open area between the farm buildings (US/Canada also barnyard)The hens walk around the farmyard all day.
barna big building for hay, straw, machines or animalsThe hay is stored in the barn.
stablea building where horses are keptThe horses sleep in the stable.
cowshed / milking shedwhere cows are kept or milked (a modern one: a milking parlour)The cows walk to the milking shed twice a day.
pigsty (US/Canada pigpen)a small building with a yard for pigsYour room is as messy as a pigsty!
henhouse / chicken coopa small building where hens sleep and lay eggsClose the chicken coop at night because of the foxes.
siloa tall, round tower for storing grain or animal foodYou can see the silo from the main road.
sheda small, simple building, often for toolsThe spades are in the shed.

Farm land

WordWhat it is
fieldan area of land with a fence or hedge round it, for crops or animals
meadowa field of grass and wild flowers, often cut for hay
pastureland with grass where animals eat (graze)
orcharda field of fruit trees: apples, pears, cherries
hedgea line of bushes growing between fields or along a road
fence / gatea fence is a barrier of wood or wire; a gate is the part that opens
stilesteps that help people climb over a fence or wall
ponda small area of still water
lane / tracka lane is a narrow country road; a track is a rough path for tractors and walkers

Where is it? How do you get there?

PrepositionMeaningExample
behind / in front ofat the back of / at the front ofThe stable is behind the barn. The pond is in front of the farmhouse.
next to / betweenbeside / with one thing on each sideThe shed is next to the silo. The pond is between the orchard and the meadow.
opposite (US/Canada also across from)on the other side of a road or yard, facingPark opposite the farmhouse.
acrossfrom one side of a flat area to the otherWalk across the field.
throughinto one side and out of the otherGo through the gate. The path goes through the orchard.
alongfollowing a line: a road, a river, a hedgeDrive along the lane for two kilometres.
overfrom one side of something high to the otherClimb over the stile.
pastgo by something without stoppingGo past the church. The farm is on the right.

Say it right: BARN has a long ah; MEAdow sounds like meddo; in ORchard the ch is like chair; SIlo sounds like sigh-low; through sounds exactly like threw; stile sounds like style.

Exercise 7: Prepositions and mistakes

Part A: Where it is or how you move?

Watch out

  • where it is: The stable is behind the barn. The pond is between the trees.
  • how you move: Walk across the field, through the gate and along the river.
  • No of after behind, opposite, between. But: in front of, next to.
