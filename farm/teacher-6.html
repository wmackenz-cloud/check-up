Teacher Notes 6: From Farm to Table

Open the student page

Purpose: help general learners talk about food from the farm: the meat words that differ from the animal (beef, pork, bacon, ham, lamb, mutton), shopping and label words (dairy, produce, free-range, organic, grass-fed, local, seasonal), and the grammar of amounts: countable and uncountable nouns with containers (a carton of, a loaf of, a dozen) and much / many, a few / a little.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: bring (or show on screen) some real food packaging: an egg box, a milk carton, a bread bag, a honey jar. Labels like free-range or organic are a bonus. Or ask the student to photograph three things from their kitchen.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Listen for breads, milks, fruits and for animal names used as meat (We ate pig). Note them for later.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Start with the animal and meat table: test by asking “What do we call meat from a pig?” Then the shopping words with your packaging. Then the amounts table: hold up each item and ask “What is it? A carton of ...?”
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the amounts. In Exercise 2, ask “Can you count it?” before each answer.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one together and set the other for home.
  5. Reading (8 min). The market guide. First ask: “Which stall would you visit first, and why?” Then the questions. Follow-up: make a shopping list for the market with amounts (a dozen eggs, a loaf of sourdough).
  6. Speaking (10 min). Questions 2 and 3 are the core. For question 3, ask for a short recipe with amounts: “How much flour? How many eggs?”
  7. Countable and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A together; Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • butcher /ˈbʊtʃə/: the vowel of put and push, not but.
  • dozen /ˈdʌzn/: the o sounds like u in cup.
  • loaf /ləʊf/, loaves /ləʊvz/: f changes to v, like calf, calves in Lesson 1.
  • produce: noun /ˈprɒdjuːs/ (stress first: local PROduce), verb /prəˈdjuːs/ (farms proDUCE milk).
  • yogurt: UK /ˈjɒɡət/, North America /ˈjoʊɡərt/. Both are fine.
  • mutton /ˈmʌtn/, veal /viːl/, venison /ˈvenɪsn/: three syllables, stress on the first.

False friends and common mistakes

  • Animal vs meat. A nice story for students: after 1066, French-speaking nobles ate the meat (bœuf, porc, mouton) and English-speaking farmers looked after the animals (cow, pig, sheep). So the meat words come from French.
  • lamb, chicken, turkey, duck are both the animal and the meat. fish too. Only the big farm animals have separate meat words.
  • bread, milk, cheese, butter, fruit, meat, food are normally uncountable: some bread, a piece of fruit. Cheeses and fruits exist, but mean different kinds (B2).
  • a dozen eggs, not a dozen of eggs. But dozens of eggs (= a lot). The same with a hundred people / hundreds of people.
  • organic: Spanish ecológico / biológico, German and French Bio. In English say organic, not ecological or biological.
  • free-range is about how animals live, not price: free does not mean no cost. Some students guess this.
  • produce (noun) is uncountable: local produce, not produces.

Adapting the lesson

ElementB1B2
VocabularyFocus on beef, pork, bacon, ham, lamb; dairy, free-range, organic, local; a carton of, a dozen, a loaf of, a bag of; much/many.Add mutton, venison, veal, grass-fed, seasonal, produce, a punnet of, a sprig of, a pinch of, and kinds: French cheeses, tropical fruits.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • A market or shop you like. Follow up: “What do you usually buy there?” “How much does a dozen eggs cost?” Listen for breads and a dozen of.
  • Local, organic or free-range? Follow up: “Would you pay twice as much for free-range eggs?” “How do you know the label is true?”
  • A typical dish. Ask for a mini recipe: “How much rice? How many onions?” This drives much / many, a little / a few naturally.
  • Picking your own fruit. Follow up: “How many kilos did you pick?” “Did you eat any while you were picking?” A light, happy topic.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: beef = meat from cattle; veal = meat from a calf; pork = meat from a pig; bacon = salty pig meat, often fried for breakfast; ham = pig meat from the leg, often in sandwiches; lamb = meat from a young sheep; mutton = meat from an older sheep; venison = meat from a deer.
  • Exercise 2: 1 carton, 2 dozen, 3 much, 4 many, 5 a little, 6 loaves, 7 free-range, 8 in season.
  • Exercise 3: 1 dairy, 2 butcher, 3 organic, 4 produce, 5 jar, 6 bunch, 7 grass-fed, 8 pick-your-own.
  • Exercise 4: 1 How much milk do we need for the pancakes? 2 Could I have a loaf of brown bread? 3 We buy our eggs from a local farm. 4 There are only a few potatoes left. 5 My children love bacon and eggs for breakfast.
  • Exercise 5: 1 saturday, 2 100, 3 hill, 4 early, 5 loaves.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: countable: many, a few: egg, potato, sausage, carrot, apple. uncountable: much, a little: milk, butter, bread, honey, flour. Part B: 1 Can you buy two loaves of bread at the bakery? 2 How much milk do we need? 3 We had roast pork for Sunday lunch. 4 No mistake. 5 There are a few apples in the bowl.
