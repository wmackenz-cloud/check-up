Teacher Notes 2: Around the Farm: Buildings and Land

Open the student page

Purpose: give general learners the words for farm buildings and farm land (barn, stable, silo, orchard, pasture, hedge, stile) and the prepositions to describe where things are and how to get there (behind, opposite, between; across, through, along, over, past). The reading is a farm-stay welcome email with directions.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: find a picture or simple map of a farm (a children's picture book page works well), or sketch one with a barn, a farmhouse, a pond, an orchard and a lane. Ask the student to think of a walk they know well.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Note which buildings the student can already name and which prepositions they use for directions.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Buildings first, then land, using your picture: “Where do the horses sleep?” “What's the tall tower?” Then the prepositions: draw a field and move a pen across, along the edge and through a gate.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the prepositions. In Exercise 2, ask the student to show each movement with a finger on the table.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one together and set the other for home.
  5. Reading (8 min). The farm-stay email. First ask the student to draw a quick map while you read it aloud, then check it against the text, then do the questions.
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 1 (describe the map) and Question 3 (directions on a real walk) are the core. Follow up with “And then?” to get a chain of prepositions.
  7. Prepositions and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A together; Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • barn /bɑːn/ (North American /bɑrn/): a long vowel; not bun or burn.
  • meadow /ˈmedəʊ/: ea as in bread, not meet. pasture /ˈpɑːstʃə/ (North American /ˈpæstʃər/).
  • orchard /ˈɔːtʃəd/: ch as in chair, not k. Stress on the first syllable.
  • silo /ˈsaɪləʊ/: sigh-low. Spanish and Italian speakers often say see-lo.
  • through /θruː/: same as threw. Practise through vs true vs threw; many learners lose the th.
  • across /əˈkrɒs/: one c, one syllable stress on -cross. Learners often write and say accross. stile /staɪl/ sounds like style.

False friends and common mistakes

  • behind of, opposite of: no of. But in front of and next to need their small word. Spanish detrás de, enfrente de and French derrière, en face de cause these errors.
  • on a farm, in a field: you live or work on a farm, but the cows are in a field, in the barn, in the farmyard.
  • across / through / along: across = flat surface, side to side (a field, a road, a river by bridge); through = something with sides or a gap (a gate, a wood, long grass); along = following a line (a river, a lane, a hedge).
  • pasture / pastor: similar sound, very different meaning. A pastor is a church minister. stile / style: same sound.
  • meadow vs pasture: both are grass. A meadow usually has wild flowers and is cut for hay; a pasture is where animals graze. In everyday speech, field is fine for both.
  • barn, stable, shed: learners often use barn for everything. Horses live in a stable; a shed is small; a barn is big.

Adapting the lesson

ElementB1B2
VocabularyFocus on farmhouse, barn, stable, field, orchard, fence, gate, pond. Prepositions: behind, next to, opposite, across, through, along.All words; add outbuilding, paddock (a small field for horses), cattle grid (US/Canada cattle guard), footpath, right of way, and beyond, alongside, towards.
SpeakingDescribe a simple farm picture or map; give directions on a short walk.Guide the teacher around an imaginary farm with eyes closed (“Now you're walking along the hedge ...”), or compare countryside walking in their country and the UK or Canada.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Draw and describe the farm in the email. Follow up: “Where's the stable?” “What's opposite the farmhouse?” Listen for behind of and opposite of.
  • A farm, park or garden you know. Follow up: “What's in the middle?” “What's next to the water?” “How do you get in?”
  • Directions on a walk. Ask them to give you the walk step by step, then you repeat it back with one mistake (“So I go across the river ...”) and they correct you.
  • A farm-stay weekend. Follow up: “What would you do first?” “Would you help with the animals?” “What could be difficult for a city person?”

Answers

  • Exercise 1: barn = a big building for hay and machines; stable = where horses are kept; pigsty = a small building and yard for pigs; henhouse = where hens sleep and lay eggs; silo = a tall tower for storing grain; orchard = a field of fruit trees; pasture = grass where animals eat; stile = steps to climb over a fence or wall.
  • Exercise 2: 1 across, 2 through, 3 along, 4 over, 5 past, 6 behind, 7 opposite, 8 between.
  • Exercise 3: 1 barn, 2 silo, 3 coop, 4 shed, 5 hedge, 6 fence, 7 lane, 8 meadow.
  • Exercise 4: 1 The stables are behind the farmhouse. 2 Walk along the lane until you reach the pond. 3 Please close the gate after you go through. 4 Is the orchard next to the barn? 5 The cows are grazing in the pasture by the river.
  • Exercise 5: 1 church, 2 sheep, 3 farmyard, 4 barn, 5 stile.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: where it is: behind, next to, opposite, between, in front of. how you move: across, through, along, past, into. Part B: 1 We walked across the field to the other side. 2 The barn is opposite the farmhouse. 3 The stable is next to the barn. 4 No mistake. 5 The pigs live in a pigsty behind the barn.
