Teacher Notes 7: Weather, Soil and Farm Problems

Open the student page

Purpose: give general learners the language of weather problems and their effects on farms and food (drought, flood, frost, hail, heatwave, pest, erosion, yield, a poor harvest), clear cause-and-effect phrases (because of, due to, so, as a result, lead to, cause), and the first and second conditionals for real and imagined situations. It prepares students to follow and discuss news stories about weather and food.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: find a short, recent news headline or photo about extreme weather and farming in the student's country or yours (a drought, a flood, a frost on fruit trees). It makes the warm-up and speaking real.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions, or show your headline first. Note how the student links ideas: do they use because and so, or only and?
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Weather problems first (draw quick pictures: sun and cracked ground, rain and water, ice). Then soil and crops. Then the cause and effect table: make one chain together: No rain → dry soil → poor harvest → higher prices.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the connectors and conditionals. In Exercise 2, ask “Is a noun or a sentence after the gap?”
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one together and set the other for home.
  5. Reading (8 min). The news article. First ask: “Who had the biggest problem: the dairy farmer, the vegetable growers or the fruit farmer?” Then the questions. Ask the student to find one so, one as a result, one due to and both conditionals.
  6. Speaking (10 min). Questions 2 and 3 are the core. Push for chains of causes: “And what happened because of that?”
  7. Cause and effect and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A together; Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • drought /draʊt/: rhymes with out. Students often say /drɒt/ or /drɔːt/ because of bought, thought.
  • flood /flʌd/: rhymes with mud and blood, not food.
  • soil /sɔɪl/: one syllable with the oi of boy. hail /heɪl/ sounds the same as hale.
  • fertile: UK /ˈfɜːtaɪl/, North America /ˈfɜːrtl/. Both are correct.
  • yield /jiːld/: starts with a y sound, like yes.
  • erosion /ɪˈrəʊʒn/ and irrigation /ˌɪrɪˈɡeɪʃn/: the -sion in erosion is the sound in television.

False friends and common mistakes

  • because vs because of: the most common error in this lesson. Quick test: if there is a verb after it, use because.
  • due to works like because of: due to the rain. Not due to it rained.
  • cause: cause + noun (caused damage) or cause + someone/something + to + verb (caused the river to rise). Not caused that (a direct translation from Spanish causó que, French a fait que).
  • lead / led: the past is led, pronounced like the metal lead /led/. Students often write lead for the past.
  • No will after if in the first conditional: If it rains, not If it will rain. In the second conditional, no would after if: If we had, not If we would have.
  • weather is uncountable: bad weather, not a bad weather. hail and flooding are uncountable; a flood and a storm are countable.
  • plague vs pest: Spanish plaga, French parasite / nuisible. In English a plague is a big disaster (a plague of locusts); a normal insect problem is a pest.

Adapting the lesson

ElementB1B2
VocabularyFocus on drought, flood, frost, storm, heatwave, soil, pest, weed, harvest; because, because of, so; first conditional.Add erosion, yield, crop failure, fertility, as a result of, consequently, resulting in, and the second conditional for policy ideas (If the government paid farmers to plant trees, ...).
SpeakingDescribe one weather event and its effects with because and so.Discuss a news story: causes, effects on prices and people, and what you would do if you were a farmer or a minister.

A quick check: real or imagined?

If the student mixes the two conditionals, ask “Is it possible, or are you imagining?”

  • If it rains tomorrow, ...: possible; it might really happen → will.
  • If I had a farm, ...: imagined; I don't have one → would.

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • The worst weather you remember. Follow up: “What happened because of it?” “What was the result for people near you?” Listen for because of + sentence.
  • Weather problems in your country. Follow up: “What happens to food prices after a drought?” “Which crops suffer most?” Good for lead to and as a result.
  • No rain for three months. Second conditional practice. Follow up: “What would happen to the parks? To water bills? Would you change your habits?”
  • Your own farm or garden. Follow up: “What pests would you have?” “What would you do if a frost came in May?” Mix first and second conditionals.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: drought = a long time with little or no rain; flood = water covering land that is usually dry; frost = thin ice on the ground after a cold night; hail = small balls of ice that fall like rain; heatwave = a period of very hot weather; pest = an insect or animal that damages crops; weed = a wild plant growing where you don't want it; irrigation = bringing water to fields with pipes.
  • Exercise 2: 1 because of, 2 so, 3 due to, 4 As a result, 5 cause, 6 to, 7 doesn't rain, 8 would grow.
  • Exercise 3: 1 soil, 2 fertile, 3 erosion, 4 hail, 5 pests, 6 yield, 7 harvest, 8 storm.
  • Exercise 4: 1 If it rains tomorrow, the farmers will be happy. 2 The crops failed because of the long drought. 3 If I had a farm, I would keep goats. 4 The flood caused a lot of damage. 5 Dry weather can lead to forest fires.
  • Exercise 5: 1 12, 2 hay, 3 irrigation, 4 night, 5 pond.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: + a noun: the drought: because of ___, due to ___, as a result of ___, led to ___, caused ___. + a sentence: it didn't rain: because ___, so ___, if ___, As a result, ___, since ___. Part B: 1 If it is sunny tomorrow, we'll cut the hay. 2 The crops died because it was too hot. 3 No mistake. 4 Heavy rain can lead to flooding. 5 The soil here is very fertile.
