What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • describe a weather problem and its effects on farms and food
  • explain causes and results in simple, clear sentences
  • talk about what will happen, and what you would do, in different situations

You will know

  • drought, flood, frost, hail, heatwave
  • fertile, erosion, irrigation, pest, yield
  • because of, due to, as a result, lead to

You will understand

  • because of the drought, because it didn't rain
  • If it doesn't rain ... will vs If we had ... would

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Because of it didn't rain, the crops died. Because it didn't rain, the crops died. / Because of the drought, ...
  • If it will rain tomorrow, the farmers will be happy. If it rains tomorrow, the farmers will be happy.
  • If we would have more water, we would grow more. If we had more water, we would grow more.
  • The drought caused that the crops died. The drought caused the crops to die. / The drought killed the crops.
  • The storm lead to flooding last week. The storm led to flooding last week.
  • The soil here is very fertility. The soil here is very fertile.

Key vocabulary

Weather problems

WordMeaningExample
droughta long time with little or no rainThe drought lasted three months.
flood / floodinga lot of water covering land that is usually dryThe fields were under water after the flood.
frosta thin white layer of ice on the ground on a cold nightA late frost damaged the apple blossom.
hailsmall balls of ice that fall like rainThe hail broke the greenhouse windows.
heatwavea period of very hot weatherDuring the heatwave it was 38°C.
stormvery strong wind, often with heavy rainThe storm blew the roof off the barn.

Soil and crops

WordMeaningExample
soilthe top layer of earth where plants growPotatoes like light, sandy soil.
fertile (noun: fertility)good for growing plantsThe valley has very fertile soil.
erosionwhen wind or water slowly carries soil awayTrees and hedges help to stop erosion.
irrigation (verb: irrigate)bringing water to fields with pipes or channelsWithout irrigation, nothing grows here in summer.
pestan insect or animal that damages cropsSlugs are a common garden pest.
weeda wild plant growing where you don't want itWe pull up the weeds every week.
diseasean illness of plants or animalsA disease killed half the potato plants.
yieldhow much a field or crop producesThe wheat yield was high this year.
a good / poor harvest; crop failurea lot / not much food was collected; the crop didn't growAfter the drought there was a poor harvest.

Saying what causes what

PhraseUse it forExample
because of / due to + nounthe cause (a thing)The harvest was late because of the rain. The road is closed due to flooding.
because + subject + verbthe cause (a sentence)The harvest was late because it rained a lot.
so / As a result,the resultIt was very dry, so the grass turned brown. As a result, hay prices went up.
cause + noun / lead to + nouna cause and its result in one sentenceFrost can cause damage. Heavy rain can lead to floods.
If + present, ... will + verba real, possible future (first conditional)If it doesn't rain soon, the crops will fail.
If + past, ... would + verban imagined or unlikely situation (second conditional)If we had more water, we would grow more.

Say it right: drought rhymes with out (the gh is silent); flood rhymes with mud, not food; soil rhymes with oil; yield sounds like yeeld; eROsion, irriGAtion.

Dry summer hits local farmers

Farmers in the Fraser Valley say this has been the driest summer for thirty years. Only 12 millimetres of rain fell in July and August, compared with 90 millimetres in a normal year.

“The soil is like dust,” says dairy farmer Ken Dhaliwal. “The grass stopped growing in July, so we're already feeding the cows the hay we saved for winter.” As a result, many farmers will have to buy extra hay, and prices have doubled.

Vegetable growers have done better because most of them use irrigation. But water is now a problem too. Due to low river levels, the town has asked farms to water their fields only at night.

Fruit farmer Maria Santos lost part of her blueberry crop in a heatwave in early August. “If it doesn't rain soon, the plants will suffer next year too,” she says. “If we had a bigger pond, we would store more water in the winter. That's our plan now.”
