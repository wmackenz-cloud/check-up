Teacher Notes 4: Crops and the Farming Year

Open the student page

Purpose: give general learners the words for the main crops and the farming year (plough, sow, spray, harvest, store, rotate), the difference between hay and straw, and the language for describing a process: sequencing words and the present simple passive (The seeds are sown in April).

Lesson time: 50 minutes, plus a few exercises to finish at home.

Level: B1 to B2. Before the lesson: find a photo of a field of wheat, a field of yellow canola, and bales of hay or straw. Optional: a short video of a combine harvester at work (search “combine harvester wheat harvest”); students who grew up in cities have often never seen one.

Lesson plan (50 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. The breakfast question works well: toast = wheat, cereal = corn or oats, oil = canola or sunflower.
  2. Key vocabulary (10 min). Crops first, with your photos. Then the verbs: act them out in order (plough, sow, spray, harvest, store). Then the process box: show how the passive keeps the crop at the front of the sentence.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Matching, then the process sentences. In Exercise 2, ask for the past participle every time: sown, ploughed, harvested, stored.
  4. Exercise 3 or 4 (5 min). Do one together and set the other for home.
  5. Reading (8 min). The Prairie grain farm. Before the questions, ask the student to put the year in order: spring, summer, harvest, winter. Then underline every passive verb (there are eight).
  6. Speaking (10 min). Question 2 is the core: from field to plate, step by step. Insist on a sequencing word for every step, and at least two passives.
  7. Steps and wrap-up (4 min). Exercise 7 Part A together; Part B at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • plough /plaʊ/: rhymes with cow. The -ough spelling confuses everyone (though, through, rough). North American spelling plow shows the sound better.
  • sow /səʊ/, sown /səʊn/: rhyme with go and grown. A female pig, sow /saʊ/, rhymes with cow (Lesson 1).
  • harvest /ˈhɑːvɪst/: stress on HAR. harvested has an extra syllable: /ˈhɑːvɪstɪd/.
  • wheat /wiːt/ (long, like sheet); oats /əʊts/; barley /ˈbɑːli/.
  • grain /ɡreɪn/ rhymes with rain. silo /ˈsaɪləʊ/: SIGH-low.
  • Past participles with -ed: ploughed /plaʊd/ and stored /stɔːd/ are one syllable; harvested, rotated, planted add a syllable after t.

False friends and common mistakes

  • hay vs straw: hay is food (dried grass, green and smells sweet); straw is bedding (dry yellow stems, empty inside). Many languages use one word for both.
  • corn: in the UK corn can mean any grain (a cornfield may be wheat). In North America corn is always maize. Spanish maíz, French maïs: say corn in Canada and the US.
  • sow / sew / so: all /səʊ/. Sow seeds, sew clothes. Students often write sewn for sown.
  • At first vs First: At first means “in the beginning (but it changed later)”. For steps, use First or Firstly. In the end vs Finally: in the end suggests a long wait or problems.
  • The passive: learners drop the be (The wheat harvested in August) or use the base form (is harvest). Make them say the full form aloud: is harvested.
  • grain is uncountable for the crop (a lot of grain); a grain of rice is one seed. crop is countable: two crops a year.
  • harvest is a verb and a noun: We harvest in August. The harvest was good this year. In the UK harvest also means autumn festivals (harvest festival).

Speaking prompts and follow-ups

  • Main crops in your country. Follow up: “Where are they grown?” “Are they exported?” Good chances for the passive: Rice is grown in the south.
  • From field to plate. Follow up after each step: “And then what happens?” “Who does that?” Correct missing be in the passive gently by repeating the sentence correctly.
  • Seasons in the countryside. Follow up: “What colours are the fields then?” “Is there a harvest festival or holiday?”
  • Growing your own food. Follow up: “When would you plant it?” “What could go wrong?” This leads into Lesson 7 (weather and problems).

Answers

  • Exercise 1: wheat = flour for bread and pasta; barley = malt for beer, and animal feed; oats = porridge and granola; corn = cornflakes and corn on the cob; canola = cooking oil from yellow flowers; potatoes = grow under the ground; hay = dried grass for animals to eat in winter; straw = dry stems that animals sleep on.
  • Exercise 2: 1 are ploughed, 2 are sown, 3 First, 4 then, 5 is stored, 6 rotate, 7 is harvested, 8 Finally.
  • Exercise 3: 1 hay, 2 straw, 3 grain, 4 plough, 5 harvest, 6 spray, 7 silo, 8 rotation.
  • Exercise 4: 1 The potatoes are harvested in the autumn. 2 First the field is ploughed, then the seeds are sown. 3 What do farmers grow in your country? 4 The hay is stored in the barn for winter. 5 Canola is grown for its oil.
  • Exercise 5: 1 saskatchewan, 2 may, 3 water, 4 hail, 5 canola.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: beginning: First,, To begin with,, Firstly,. middle: Then, Next,, After that,, Once that's done,. end: Finally,, Lastly,, In the end,. Part B: 1 The seeds are sown in April. 2 The wheat is harvested in August. 3 No mistake. 4 Farmers grow potatoes in this valley. 5 First, the field is ploughed, and then the seeds are sown.
