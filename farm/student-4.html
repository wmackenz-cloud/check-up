What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • name the crops that grow in your country and say what they are used for
  • describe the farming year, season by season
  • explain a simple process step by step

You will know

  • wheat, barley, oats, corn, canola
  • plough, sow, spray, harvest, rotate
  • hay vs straw

You will understand

  • passive for a process: The seeds are sown in April.
  • first, then, after that, finally

Common mistakes to watch for

  • The seeds are sow in April. The seeds are sown in April. (sow, sowed, sown)
  • The wheat is harvest in August. The wheat is harvested in August.
  • At first, the field is ploughed. Then the seeds are sown. First, the field is ploughed. Then the seeds are sown.
  • Farmers grows potatoes here. Farmers grow potatoes here.
  • The cows sleep on hay. The cows sleep on straw. (hay is food; straw is bedding)
  • We eat a corn on the cob. We eat corn on the cob. / We eat an ear of corn.

Farming verbs

VerbMeaningExample
plough (US/Canada also plow)turn over the soil before plantingThe fields are ploughed in early spring.
sow (sowed, sown)put seeds in the groundThe wheat is sown in April.
plantput seeds or young plants in the groundWe plant potatoes in May.
grow (grew, grown)1 get bigger; 2 farmers grow cropsThey grow barley and oats.
spraycover a crop with a liquid to kill weeds or insectsThe field was sprayed last week.
harvestcut and collect a crop when it is ready; also a noun: the harvestThe grain is harvested in August.
storekeep something until laterThe grain is stored in a silo.
rotategrow a different crop in a field each yearFarmers rotate their crops to keep the soil healthy.

Describing a process

For a process, we often use the present simple passive: is / are + past participle. It puts the crop first, because the crop is what matters.

PhraseUse it forExample
First, / To begin with,the first stepFirst, the soil is ploughed.
Then, / Next,the next stepThen the seeds are sown.
After that, / Once that's done,a later stepAfter that, the crop is sprayed.
When ... / Once ...one step starts when another endsWhen the grain is dry, it is harvested.
Finally,the last stepFinally, the grain is stored in a silo.

Say it right: plough rhymes with cow; sow (seeds) rhymes with go, and so does sown; HARvest; wheat has a long ee; grain rhymes with rain.

Exercise 5: Reading

A year on a Prairie grain farm

My family has farmed near Swift Current, Saskatchewan, for four generations. We grow wheat, canola and lentils on about two thousand acres.

Our year really begins in April, when the snow melts. First, the machines are checked and repaired. Then, in early May, the seeds are sown. We don't plough any more: the seeds go straight into the stubble (the short stems left from last year's crop). This keeps water in the soil, and on the Prairies water is precious.

In June and July we watch the sky. With too little rain, the crop is small. Hail can destroy a whole field in ten minutes. The fields are sprayed once or twice to control the weeds.

Harvest starts in late August. The wheat is cut with a combine, and the grain is taken by truck to our storage bins. Finally, in late September, the canola is harvested. After that, the grain is sold, and we can relax a little.

In winter we plan next year's crops. We rotate them, so a field of wheat this year will be canola or lentils next year.

Exercise 7: Steps and mistakes

Part A: Beginning, middle or end?

Watch out

  • First (or Firstly) is for the first step. At first means “at the beginning, but not later”: At first I hated farm work, but now I love it.
  • Finally is for the last step. In the end means “after a long time or many problems”.
