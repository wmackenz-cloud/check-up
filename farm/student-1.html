What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • name the animals you see on a farm, and their young
  • describe a farm visit: what animals there were, how many and what they were doing
  • talk about farm animals in your country

You will know

  • bull, cow, calf; ram, ewe, lamb
  • herd, flock, litter
  • animal sounds: moo, baa, oink

You will understand

  • three sheep, two geese (not sheeps, gooses)
  • the cattle are (always plural)

Common mistakes to watch for

  • We saw many sheeps in the field. We saw many sheep in the field.
  • The cattle is in the field. The cattle are in the field.
  • The farmer has a big flock of cows. The farmer has a big herd of cows.
  • Two gooses chased me! Two geese chased me!
  • Look at that cow! He's huge. Look at that bull! He's huge. (a cow is female)
  • The farmer has twenty porks. The farmer has twenty pigs. (pork is the meat)

Warm-up

  • Which farm animals can you name in English? Make a quick list.
  • Did you grow up in a city or in the countryside? Have you ever touched a cow, a sheep or a horse?
  • Which farm animal do you think is the cleverest? Which is the funniest?

Exercise 5: Reading

A weekend on my cousin's farm

Last weekend I visited my cousin Marta on her family farm near Guelph, Ontario. I'm a city person, so everything was new to me.

The farm has a herd of about sixty dairy cows. They are milked twice a day, at six in the morning and five in the afternoon. Marta also keeps a small flock of sheep. The ewes had their lambs in March, so the lambs were already running and jumping in the field. My favourite animal was a new calf. She was only two days old, and she followed me everywhere.

Behind the barn there are about forty hens and one very loud rooster. He crowed at half past four on Sunday morning, so I didn't get much sleep! Marta's children collect the eggs every afternoon. There are also two pigs, a sow and a boar. The sow is going to have piglets next month.

Next time I want to stay for a whole week. Farm life is hard work, but I loved it.

Exercise 6: Speaking

Think about each question. We will talk about them in our lesson.

  1. Have you ever visited a farm? What animals did you see? If not, which farm animal would you most like to see up close?
  2. Which farm animals are common in your country? Is any animal special in your culture or in your cooking?
  3. What sounds do animals make in your language? Compare them with English: a cow says moo, a rooster says cock-a-doodle-doo.
  4. Would you like to keep a few hens or a goat at home? Why or why not?
