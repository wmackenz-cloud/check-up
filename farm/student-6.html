What this lesson is for

Why this matters: food is something we talk about every day: shopping, cooking, eating out and choosing what to buy. English has different words for the animal and its meat, and many food words are uncountable, so we need a loaf of, a carton of and much or many to talk about amounts.

You will know

  • meat words: beef, veal, pork, bacon, ham, lamb, mutton
  • food and shopping words: dairy, produce, free-range, organic, grass-fed, local, seasonal, farmers' market, butcher
  • amounts: a carton of milk, a dozen eggs, a loaf of bread, a kilo of potatoes, a jar of honey

You will understand

  • why we eat pork and beef, not pig and cow
  • the difference between countable and uncountable food: many eggs, much milk; a few apples, a little butter

You will be able to

  • shop at a market or farm shop and ask for the right amount
  • explain where your food comes from: The eggs are free-range and the beef is local.
  • talk about the food you buy and cook at home

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I bought two breads. I bought two loaves of bread.
  • Can I have some milks? Can I have some milk?
  • How much eggs do we need? How many eggs do we need?
  • We had pig for dinner. We had pork for dinner.
  • I'd like a dozen of eggs. I'd like a dozen eggs. (no of)
  • There is a few cheese left. There is a little cheese left.

Warm-up

  • Where do you usually buy food: a supermarket, a market, a small shop or a farm?
  • What did you eat yesterday? Which foods came from a farm?
  • Do you check where your food comes from? What labels do you look for?

Say it right: BUTCHer has the vowel of put, not but; dozen sounds like duzzen; loaf and loaves rhyme with go; PRODuce (food) has the stress at the start, but the verb is proDUCE.

Riverside Farmers' Market: a visitor's guide

The market is open every Saturday from 8 a.m. to 1 p.m., from May to October. Everything is grown or made within 100 kilometres of the city, so the food is local and seasonal.

Start at the Green Valley Dairy stall. They sell fresh milk in glass bottles, butter, yogurt and more than ten kinds of cheese. Ask for a free taste of the blue cheese!

Next to them, Hill Farm sells free-range eggs by the dozen or half dozen, and grass-fed beef and lamb. If you want pork sausages or bacon, visit the Martins' stall at the end of the row.

For fruit and vegetables, go to Orchard Lane. In June they have strawberries, and in September apples and pumpkins. Come early: the strawberries are usually gone by ten o'clock.

Don't forget the bakery van near the entrance. Their sourdough loaves are famous.

Bring your own bags. Most stallholders take cards, but a little cash is useful.

Part A: Countable or uncountable?

Watch out

  • countable: one egg, two eggs; many, a few, How many?
  • uncountable: no plural; much, a little, How much? To count it, use an amount: a loaf of bread, a carton of milk
