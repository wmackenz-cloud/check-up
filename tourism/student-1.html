What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • greet a visitor and make them feel welcome
  • ask closed and open questions with the right word order
  • give simple information: opening times, distances and suggestions

You will know

  • Welcome to ..., How was your journey?
  • closed and open questions
  • Yes, I am. No, we don't.

You will understand

  • Are you tired? (not Do you are tired?)
  • closed to check, open to chat

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Where you are from? Where are you from?
  • Do you are tired? Are you tired?
  • How long you stay? How long are you staying?
  • Did you enjoyed the tour? Did you enjoy the tour? (after did, use the base verb)
  • What time opens the museum? What time does the museum open?
  • Are you hungry? Yes, I do. Are you hungry? Yes, I am. (answer with the same verb as the question)

Warm-up

  • Have you ever worked with tourists, or helped a visitor in your town? What did they ask you?
  • When you travel, what is the first question people usually ask you?
  • Which questions are easy for you in English? Which ones do you often get wrong?

Short answers

Answer a closed question with the same helper verb. Just Yes or No can sound a little cold.

QuestionYesNo
Are you ...?Yes, I am. / Yes, we are.No, I'm not.
Is it ...?Yes, it is.No, it isn't.
Do you ...?Yes, I do.No, I don't.
Does it ...?Yes, it does.No, it doesn't.
Did you ...?Yes, I did.No, I didn't.
Have you ...?Yes, I have.No, I haven't.
Can I ...?Yes, of course.I'm sorry, you can't.

Turn a closed question into an open one

A closed question can stop the conversation: Yes. Use a question word to keep it going.

ClosedOpen
Did you enjoy the tour?What did you enjoy most about the tour?
Is this your first time in Canada?What made you choose Canada?
Are you here for work?What brings you to Toronto?
Do you like the hotel?How are you finding the hotel?
Did you sleep well?How did you sleep?

Say it right: In yes/no questions your voice usually goes up at the end: Is this your first visit? ↗ In open questions it usually goes down: Where are you from? ↘ In either/or questions it goes up, then down: Tea ↗ or coffee ↘?

Exercise 6: Speaking

Think about each task. We will practise them in our lesson.

  1. Role-play: a visitor arrives at your hotel, café or shop. Greet them and ask three closed questions (Is this ...? Do you ...? Would you like ... or ...?).
  2. Now ask the same visitor three open questions (Where ...? How long ...? What ...?). Which questions got longer answers?
  3. A tourist stops you in your town. They look lost. What do you say first? What questions do you ask to help them?
  4. Think of a place in your town or country that tourists love. What would you tell a visitor about it: when it's open, how far it is, and what to see?

Exercise 7: Closed or open, and mistakes

Part A: Closed or open?

Watch out

  • closed: the answer is yes or no, or one of the choices you give: Do you need a taxi? Tea or coffee?
  • open: starts with a question word, and the answer gives new information: Where are you staying?
