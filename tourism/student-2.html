What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • ask for directions, prices, times and tickets, and understand the answers
  • check that you understood: So I turn left at the lights?
  • ask a stranger a question politely, without sounding too direct

You will know

  • return ticket, platform, timetable, admission, souvenir
  • closed and open questions
  • Could you tell me ...? Do you know ...?

You will understand

  • What time does the museum open?
  • Could you tell me where the station is?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • What time the museum opens? What time does the museum open?
  • Could you tell me where is the station? Could you tell me where the station is?
  • How much costs it? How much is it? / How much does it cost?
  • Where I can buy tickets? Where can I buy tickets?
  • Do you know is there a bus? Do you know if there is a bus?
  • How long it takes? How long does it take?

Warm-up

  • Think of your last trip. What questions did you ask when you arrived?
  • Is it easy for you to ask a stranger for help in English? What do you say first?
  • What do you like to do in a new city: museums, food, walking, shopping?

Asking questions

KindHow it worksExample
closed: yes / nohelper verb (is, does, can) + subject + verb; the answer is yes or noDoes this bus go to the airport? Can I pay by card?
closed: either / ortwo choices with or; the answer is one of themIs it a single or a return? Inside or outside?
openquestion word + helper verb + subject + verb; the answer is informationHow do I get to the old town? What time does it close?
open with how / whichhow much (price), how long (time), how often, which + nounHow long does the tour take? Which platform is it?
polite indirectCould you tell me / Do you know + question word + subject + verb (normal order)Could you tell me where the station is?
polite indirect: yes / noDo you know if ...? (or whether)Do you know if there's a bank near here?

Talking to locals: starting, checking and thanking

Strangers are happy to help when you open politely and thank them at the end.

PhraseUse it for
Excuse me, could you help me?stopping someone in the street
Sorry to bother you, but ...a polite start when someone is busy
I'm not from here. Is this the way to ...?checking you are on the right road
Sorry, could you speak a bit more slowly?when people talk too fast
Is there anywhere good to eat around here?asking for local tips
What would you recommend?asking a local's opinion
Thanks so much, that's really helpful.thanking
Have a nice day!ending in a friendly way

Directions and checking

PhraseUse it forExample
Excuse me / Sorry to bother youstarting a question to a strangerExcuse me, could you help me?
go straight oncontinue in the same directionGo straight on for two blocks.
turn left / right at ...change direction at a placeTurn left at the lights.
it's on your left / rightwhere the place is when you arriveThe museum is on your right.
opposite / next tothe position of a placeIt's opposite the bank, next to a café.
Could you say that again?asking someone to repeatSorry, could you say that again, a bit more slowly?
So I ...?checking that you understoodSo I turn left at the lights, then it's on my right?

Say it right: Closed questions usually go up at the end (Can I pay by card? ↑); open questions usually go down (Where's the station? ↓). souvenir is soo-ve-NEER; queue sounds like the letter Q.

Exercise 5: Reading

At the tourist office in Quebec City

Isla: Excuse me, do you speak English?

Assistant: Yes, of course. How can I help?

Isla: We're here for two days. What should we see first?

Assistant: Start with the old town. There's a guided walking tour at ten o'clock every morning. It takes about two hours.

Isla: How much is it?

Assistant: Twenty-five dollars for adults. You can buy tickets here.

Isla: Great. Can I pay by card?

Assistant: Yes, no problem.

Isla: And could you tell me how we get to the Citadel?

Assistant: Sure. Go straight on up this street, then turn left at the big hotel. The Citadel is on your right. It's about fifteen minutes on foot.

Isla: So we turn left at the hotel?

Assistant: Exactly. And don't miss the ferry to Lévis. It leaves every thirty minutes, and the view of the city from the river is beautiful.

Isla: Thank you so much!

Exercise 6: Speaking

Think about each question. We will talk about them in our lesson.

  1. You are at a tourist office in a city you'd like to visit. Ask five questions to plan your day: two closed (Is there ...? Can I ...?) and three open (What ...? How much ...? How long ...?).
  2. Role-play: you are lost. Stop a local politely, ask the way to the station, and check that you understood (So I go ...?).
  3. Role-play: buy a train or bus ticket. Ask about the price, the time of the next one, the platform, and single or return.
  4. Tell me about a time you asked for help on holiday. What did you ask? Was it easy to understand the answer?

Exercise 7: Kinds of question and mistakes

Part A: Closed, open or polite indirect?

Watch out

  • closed: the answer is yes/no or one of two choices: Is this seat free? Single or return?
  • open: starts with a question word, and the answer is information: Where ...? How much ...?
  • polite indirect: starts with Could you tell me or Do you know, then normal word order
