Teacher Notes 6: Orders and Briefings

Open the student page

Purpose: help military and police students follow and give a simple briefing in English: the five-paragraph order (SMEAC), the warning order, the mission statement (who, what, when, where, why), intent, tasks and timings with the 24-hour clock, and the language for confirming orders and asking for clarification.

Lesson time: 60 minutes, plus homework.

Level: STANAG Level 1+ to 2, with a Level 3 extension (Speaking question 4 and the ideas below). Before the lesson: ask the student whether their service uses SMEAC or another format (many NATO forces use the same five headings under different names). Keep all examples everyday and non-sensitive: convoys of aid, visits, moves, sports days.

Lesson plan (60 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Note any time the student says a time; you will come back to it.
  2. Key vocabulary (12 min). The SMEAC headings first: ask the student to explain each one in their own words. Then the 24-hour clock: you say a time, the student writes it, then swap. Finish with the briefing language table.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Sort into SMEAC, then match the times. Exercise 2 is best done aloud first.
  4. Exercises 3 and 4 (8 min). Complete the sentences, then build the briefing phrases. Ask the student to say each built sentence aloud with good stress.
  5. Reading (9 min). “First Briefing in English.” Before the questions, ask: “What two pieces of advice did the commander give?”
  6. Speaking (12 min). Question 2: the student gives a mission statement and a one-minute briefing for an everyday task. You play a team leader who asks for clarification and confirms the timings. Then swap: you brief, the student confirms.
  7. Prepositions and articles (6 min). Part A together; Parts B and C at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • brief /briːf/: long /iː/. Spanish and Italian speakers sometimes say a short /ɪ/.
  • intent /ɪnˈtent/: stress on the second syllable, unlike INterest.
  • execution /ˌeksɪˈkjuːʃn/: stress on CU.
  • logistics /ləˈdʒɪstɪks/: stress on GIS; the g is soft /dʒ/.
  • clarify /ˈklærəfaɪ/: three syllables, stress on the first.
  • H-hour /ˈeɪtʃ aʊə/: the letter H is /eɪtʃ/ (aitch), and the h in hour is silent. Hence an hour.
  • hundred /ˈhʌndrəd/ in times: zero six HUNdred. French and Italian speakers often drop the /h/.

False friends and common mistakes

  • at the fourteen thirty (Spanish a las catorce treinta, Italian alle quattordici e trenta): no article and no and: at fourteen thirty.
  • explain me (Spanish explícame, Italian spiegami, French explique-moi): say explain to me or explain it.
  • actual / actually: Spanish actual, Italian attuale, French actuel and Polish aktualny mean current. The current situation, not the actual situation.
  • eventually: Spanish eventualmente, Italian eventualmente, French éventuellement mean possibly. In English eventually = in the end.
  • assist: Spanish asistir a and French assister à mean attend. I attended the briefing, not I assisted the briefing.
  • control: in Spanish, Italian, French and Polish it often means check. Check the vehicles, not control the vehicles (in English control means to direct or have power over).
  • Is there any questions? and missing articles: Polish has no articles, so Polish speakers need extra practice with Part B.
  • by / until: Spanish hasta, Italian entro / fino a, Polish do can cover both. By is a deadline; until is a period that continues and then stops.

Notes on the exercises

  • Exercise 1 (sort into five paragraphs, 10 chips): on a phone the columns stack. If the student's own format puts intent in a different place, accept it and explain that many NATO formats put it in Execution.
  • Exercise 2 (match, 6 times): after matching, read new times at random and let the student say them.
  • Exercise 3 (type, 8 items): warning order and WARNO are both accepted.
  • Exercise 4 (word order, 6 sentences): these are the phrases they will use in the speaking role-play.
  • Exercise 5 (reading): the key idea is checking understanding with a question that needs a real answer (Team 2, what is your task?), sometimes called a backbrief.
  • Exercise 7: Part A is time prepositions and brief on, in charge of, in order to; Part B is articles, including an hour; Part C mixes times and grammar.

Extension for Level 3

  • Speaking question 4: push for a structured comparison: On the one hand ..., on the other hand ..., The main risk of ... is that .... Introduce mission command as an idea: decentralized decisions based on intent.
  • Add backbrief, concept of operations, phase, contingency plan, be prepared to.
  • Reported orders: the student reports your briefing to a third person: The major told us to be ready by 0600 and not to use the main road. (Links to Grammar Traps 10 and 11.)
  • Give a short briefing with one deliberate gap or contradiction. The student must notice it and ask for clarification politely.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: Situation: the weather and the ground, other units in the area. Mission: who, what, when, where and why, one clear sentence: the task and its purpose. Execution: the commander's intent, tasks for each team. Admin and logistics: food, water and fuel, medical support. Command and signal: call signs and frequencies, where the commander will be.
  • Exercise 2: 0600 = zero six hundred; 1430 = fourteen thirty; 2100 = twenty-one hundred; 0915 = zero nine fifteen; 1200 = twelve hundred; 0045 = zero zero forty-five.
  • Exercise 3: 1 warning order, 2 mission, 3 task, 4 intent, 5 timings, 6 brief, 7 clarify, 8 confirm. (In 7, explain also works; in 8, check also works.)
  • Exercise 4: 1 I will now brief the mission. 2 Could you go over the timings again? 3 What do you mean by phase two? 4 Are there any questions? 5 Can I just confirm the start time? 6 We move at zero six hundred tomorrow.
  • Exercise 5: 1 supplies, 2 five, 3 times (timings), 4 twice, 5 confirm.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: 1 at, 2 on, 3 by, 4 on, 5 of, 6 until, 7 in, 8 at. Part B: 1 a, 2 – (no article), 3 an, 4 a, 5 The, 6 the, 7 – (no article), 8 an. Part C: 1 The briefing will start at fourteen thirty. 2 We have the briefing on 15 March. 3 Are there any questions? 4 We must arrive at the start point by 0545.
