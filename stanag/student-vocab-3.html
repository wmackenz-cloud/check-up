Key vocabulary

Word or phraseMeaningExample
field exercisetraining carried out outdoors, in realistic conditionsThe field exercise lasted five days in the mountains.
live firingtraining with real ammunitionLive firing is only allowed on an approved range.
range (firing range)an area designed for shooting practiceWe spent the morning on the range.
drilla set of actions practised many times until it becomes automaticWe practised the casualty evacuation drill until it was perfect.
simulationtraining that uses computers or models instead of a real situationThe simulation let us practise without using fuel or ammunition.
scenariothe imagined situation that an exercise is based onIn the scenario, a flood had cut off two villages.
objectivewhat a unit must achieveOur first objective was to secure the bridge.
to carry outto do or complete a task or planThe platoon carried out a night patrol.
safety briefinga short talk explaining the risks and rules before an activityNobody can fire before the safety briefing.
to assessto judge how well someone or something performsTwo observers assessed each team.
debriefa meeting after an activity to discuss what happenedThe debrief started immediately after the exercise.
after-action review (AAR)a structured discussion: what was planned, what happened, why, and what to changeThe AAR showed that our radio procedures were too slow.
lessons learnedknowledge gained from experience, written down to improve future workThe lessons learned were sent to the training school.

Exercise 2: Types of training

Tap a type of training, then tap its description.

  • field training exercise (FTX) = troops train outdoors with their vehicles and equipment
  • command post exercise (CPX) = headquarters staff practise planning and command, with few or no troops in the field
  • live-fire exercise = soldiers use real ammunition on a range
  • tabletop exercise = leaders talk through a scenario around a table or a map
  • simulation = computers create the situation and show the results of decisions
  • rehearsal = a unit practises a specific plan just before doing it for real

Exercise 5: Reading

Exercise Northern Bridge

Last spring, an engineer company took part in Exercise Northern Bridge, a five-day field exercise with units from three NATO countries. The scenario was a natural disaster: heavy rain had destroyed a bridge, and two villages were cut off. The company's objective was to build a temporary bridge and open a supply route within 48 hours.

First, the company commander gave a detailed briefing and the soldiers checked their equipment. Then they moved to the river at night. At the start, everything went well, but on the second day a crane broke down and the work stopped for six hours. Meanwhile, the radio links with the other national teams kept failing because each team was using different procedures.

In the end, the bridge opened four hours late. During the after-action review, the commander did not look for someone to blame. Instead, the group agreed on two lessons learned: carry spare parts for key equipment, and practise common radio procedures with allies before the next exercise.

Exercise 7: Prepositions and articles

Practise prepositions and articles with the vocabulary from this lesson.

Part A: Prepositions

Watch out

  • take part in an exercise: We took part in Exercise Northern Bridge.
  • on exercise, in the field, on the range: The battalion is on exercise this week.
  • during / by: during + noun (during the exercise); by + a time limit (by 06:00).
  • before / after + noun: before the safety briefing, after the exercise.

Tap the missing preposition in each sentence.

  5. The observers made notes the exercise and gave feedback at the end.
  6. Our objective was to secure the bridge 06:00.
  7. The debrief started immediately the exercise.

Part B: Articles

Watch out

  • a / an the first time you mention something: an exercise, a scenario.
  • the when the reader already knows which one: the exercise, the exercise director.
  • No article for general ideas: Training is important. Live firing is dangerous.
