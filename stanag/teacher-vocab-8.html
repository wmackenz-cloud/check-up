Teacher Notes 8: Police and Guardia Civil

Open the student page

Purpose: give police, gendarmerie and Guardia Civil students the everyday English of patrols, checkpoints, traffic and border work: the people (suspect, witness, victim), the procedures (ID check, breathalyser test, statement, report, fine), and above all the language for giving instructions to the public politely and firmly.

Lesson time: 60 minutes, plus homework.

Level: STANAG Level 1+ to 2, with a Level 3 extension (Speaking question 4 and the ideas below). Before the lesson: ask the student what their unit does (traffic, border, ports, rural patrol) and which situations with foreign members of the public they meet most often. Build the role-play around that. Keep everything at the level of public-facing, everyday police work.

Lesson plan (60 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). The three warm-up questions. Listen for agent, the police is and an evidence: they come up in Part C.
  2. Key vocabulary (12 min). The word table, then the “polite but firm” table. Practise the tone: say each instruction twice, once too soft and once right. The student chooses.
  3. Exercises 1 and 2 (8 min). Sorting, then matching the officer’s sentences. Ask the student to say each matched sentence aloud, politely.
  4. Exercises 3 and 4 (8 min). Complete the sentences, then build the questions. Questions are where word order breaks down under pressure, so drill them aloud.
  5. Reading (9 min). “Summer at the Port.” Ask the student to find the three passive verbs (was arrested, were kept, was hurt) after the questions.
  6. Speaking (12 min). Question 2 is the core: a checkpoint role-play. You are the driver: first polite and cooperative, then tired and a little annoyed, then a tourist with weak English. The student adapts.
  7. Prepositions and articles (6 min). Part A together; Parts B and C at home.

Key vocabulary: notes

Pronunciation

  • suspect: noun /ˈsʌspekt/, verb /səˈspekt/. The stress moves, as in record, present.
  • evidence /ˈevɪdəns/: stress on the first syllable. Spanish and Italian speakers often stress -DEN-.
  • breathalyser /ˈbreθəlaɪzə/: breath /breθ/, short /e/ and /θ/. Canadian spelling: breathalyzer.
  • vehicle /ˈviːəkl/: the h is silent: VEE-uh-kul.
  • licence /ˈlaɪsns/: two syllables. Spelling: licence (noun) in British and Canadian English; driver’s licence in Canada, driving licence in the UK.
  • police /pəˈliːs/: stress on the second syllable, not PO-lice.

False friends and common mistakes

  • agent: Spanish agente, Italian agente, French agent = police officer. In English an agent is a spy, a sales agent or a sports agent. Say officer or police officer; for the Guardia Civil, a Guardia Civil officer.
  • the police is: police is always plural: the police are. One person is a police officer.
  • an evidence / evidences: uncountable in English. Spanish una prueba = a piece of evidence.
  • denuncia (Spanish) = a report or a complaint to the police, not denunciation. She reported the theft / She made a complaint.
  • detener (Spanish), arrestare / fermare (Italian): English arrest is formal custody for a crime; detain is shorter or less formal; stop is for vehicles. We stopped the car and detained the driver.
  • mandat (Polish) = a fine or ticket, not a mandate. French PV (procès-verbal) and Italian multa / verbale = a fine, a ticket or a report.
  • control (Spanish control, Italian controllo, French contrôle, Polish kontrola) = check: a document check, a breath test, a routine check. Border control and passport control are fixed phrases that do use it.
  • Can you to step out ...? and You must to ...: no to after modal verbs.
  • Too direct: Spanish and Polish imperatives often sound rude in English without please or a modal. Teach the “polite but firm” ladder and when to move up it.

Notes on the exercises

  • Exercise 1 (sort, 12 chips): quick. Ask for one example sentence with each action.
  • Exercise 2 (match, 6 pairs): these are the six most useful sentences of the lesson. After matching, the student says them to you on camera, in a calm, firm voice.
  • Exercise 3 (type, 8 items): note the collocations stopped for speeding, take a statement from, call the emergency services.
  • Exercise 4 (word order, 6 questions and instructions): use them in the role-play.
  • Exercise 5 (reading): Algeciras is a real port with a busy summer crossing season; the officer and the events are fictional. Check the student is happy with the context: they may know it well.
  • Exercise 7: Part A is fined for, on duty, in custody, a witness to; Part B is the police, the emergency services and uncountable evidence; Part C is the four biggest mistakes for Spanish speakers.

Extension for Level 3

  • Speaking question 4: ask for a reasoned position with examples and a concession: Admittedly ..., but in most cases ....
  • Add de-escalate, comply, non-compliant, under the influence, offence, charge with, release, bail, custody officer, crime scene, cordon.
  • Reported speech for statements: the student turns a role-play into a written witness statement: The driver said that he had not seen the lorry. (Links to Grammar Trap 10.)
  • Register: rewrite the same incident twice, as a formal report and as a short, friendly explanation to a tourist.

Answers

  • Exercise 1: People: suspect, witness, victim, driver. Checks and controls: ID check, roadblock, breathalyser test, passport control. Actions: to arrest, to detain, to fine, to take a statement.
  • Exercise 2: May I see your = driving licence and insurance, please?; Please step out = of the vehicle.; Could you turn off = the engine, please?; I need you to = blow into this tube until I say stop.; Please wait here while = I check your documents.; Do you have anything = to declare?.
  • Exercise 3: 1 speeding, 2 fine, 3 witness, 4 statement, 5 smuggling, 6 arrested, 7 evidence, 8 emergency services. (In 2, ticket also works; in 6, detained also works.)
  • Exercise 4: 1 May I see your vehicle documents? 2 Please step out of the vehicle. 3 Where are you travelling to today? 4 Have you had anything to drink tonight? 5 Can you describe the man you saw? 6 What time did the accident happen?
  • Exercise 5: 1 Africa, 2 smuggling, 3 voice, 4 arrested, 5 witness.
  • Exercise 7: Part A: 1 for, 2 for, 3 on, 4 for, 5 in, 6 through, 7 to, 8 from. Part B: 1 an, 2 The, 3 the, 4 – (no article), 5 an, 6 – (no article), 7 a, 8 the. Part C: 1 Please, can you step out of the car? 2 The police are investigating the incident. 3 She gave evidence to the court. 4 I am an officer in the Guardia Civil.
