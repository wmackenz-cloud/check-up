Key vocabulary

The NATO phonetic alphabet

A Alfa · B Bravo · C Charlie · D Delta · E Echo · F Foxtrot · G Golf · H Hotel · I India · J Juliett · K Kilo · L Lima · M Mike · N November · O Oscar · P Papa · Q Quebec · R Romeo · S Sierra · T Tango · U Uniform · V Victor · W Whiskey · X X-ray · Y Yankee · Z Zulu

To spell a word, say I spell: Surname Novak, I spell: November Oscar Victor Alfa Kilo.

Numbers on the radio

0 zero · 1 wun · 2 too · 3 tree · 4 fower · 5 fife · 6 six · 7 seven · 8 ait · 9 niner

Say numbers one digit at a time: 359 = tree fife niner. A grid is said in two groups: 482 615 = fower ait too, six wun fife. Niner keeps 9 apart from German nein (no), and fife keeps 5 apart from fire.

Procedure words

Word or phraseMeaningExample
call signthe name a person or unit uses on the radioOur call sign is Bravo Two Zero.
this issays who is speaking: first the station you are calling, then your own call signHotel One, this is Bravo Two Zero.
rogerI have received your messageRoger, out.
wilcoI have received your message, I understand it and I will do it (never say roger wilco)Move to the gate. / Wilco, out.
overI have finished speaking and I am waiting for your replySend your location, over.
outI have finished, and no reply is needed (never say over and out)Thanks for the update, out.
say againplease repeat (on the radio, do not say repeat)Say again all after grid, over.
I say againI am repeating my message, or part of itI say again, fourteen thirty.
correctionI made a mistake; this is the right versionArrive at sixteen, correction, seventeen hundred.
wrongyour read back was not correct; here is the right versionWrong, I say again, tree fife niner.
wait one / wait outwait a few seconds / I need more time and will call you backWait one. ... Wait out.
read backrepeat my message back to me exactly as you heard itMeet at the north gate at zero niner hundred. Read back, over.
radio checka call to test the radio: how well can you hear me?Hotel One, this is Bravo Two, radio check, over.
loud and clearI can hear you very well (also: weak but readable, unreadable)Loud and clear, over.
breaka short pause between two parts of a long messageConvoy at checkpoint two, break, next report at fifteen hundred.
copy / copied(informal, used by many forces) I heard and understoodCopy that.

A short radio message

Four parts: 1 the call sign you are calling, 2 this is + your call sign, 3 the message, short and clear, 4 over (reply, please) or out (finished).

Hotel One, this is Bravo Two. Arrived at supply point, all vehicles okay, over.
Bravo Two, this is Hotel One. Roger, out.

Say it right: NIner, WILco, ROger, Ju-li-ETT, queBEC (keh-BECK), ALfa.

Exercise 5: Reading

A Clear Message

Captain Elena Marín is a Spanish signals officer on a NATO exercise in northern Italy. Units from six countries are taking part, and all radio traffic is in English. For many soldiers, this is the hardest part of the exercise. “On the radio you cannot see faces or use your hands,” she says. “You only have your voice, and the line is often weak or full of noise.”

On the second day, a Polish section was sent to a new supply point. The operator at headquarters gave the grid as tree fife niner, too wun ait and asked for a read back. The section commander read back tree fife fife, too wun ait. The operator heard the mistake at once. She said “Wrong, I say again,” and sent the grid a second time, slowly. This time the read back was correct, and the section arrived at the right place twenty minutes later.

“This is why we use read back,” Captain Marín explains. “A read back takes about ten seconds. A mistake can cost hours. My advice is simple: keep messages short, speak slowly, spell names with the phonetic alphabet, and never be afraid to say say again.”

Part A: Prepositions

Watch out

  • on the radio, on the phone, on channel 6, on this frequency: Stay on channel four.
  • in contact with, in touch with: We are in contact with the patrol.
  • by radio, by phone, by email (no article): Send the report by radio.
  • switch to a channel; hear someone over the noise; on duty.

Part B: Articles

Watch out

  • the with things that are unique or already known: the NATO alphabet, the antenna on the roof.
  • No article for general plural or uncountable nouns: Call signs are short. Communication is essential.
  • a / an the first time you mention something, and with jobs: a radio check, an error, She is a radio operator.

Tap a, an, the or – (nothing) for each gap.

  1. Hotel One, this is Bravo Two, request radio check, over.
  2. NATO phonetic alphabet is used by armies, airlines and police forces.
  3. On operations, clear communication is essential.
  4. I need new battery for my radio.
  5. He made error in the grid, so he said correction.
  6. Can you check antenna on the roof? It isn't working.
  7. She is radio operator in a signals unit.
  8. We use call signs, not real names, on the radio.
