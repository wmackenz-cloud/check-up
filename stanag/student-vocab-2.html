Key vocabulary

Word or phraseMeaningExample
routinethe usual things you do, in the usual orderOur routine starts with PT at 06:30.
duty rostera list that shows who does which duty, and whenCheck the duty roster before you ask for a day off.
shifta fixed period of work, for example a night shiftThis week I'm working the night shift.
to be on duty / off dutyto be working / not working at a given timeSergeant Ruiz is on duty until 18:00.
guard dutythe job of protecting a gate, building or areaWe do guard duty about twice a month.
sentrya soldier who stands guard at a gate or positionThe sentry checked our ID cards at the main gate.
duty officerthe officer responsible for the base outside normal working hoursReport any incident to the duty officer.
roll callcalling names to check that everyone is presentRoll call is at 07:45 every morning.
PT (physical training)organized exercise to keep personnel fitWe usually have PT three times a week.
inspectionan official check of people, rooms or equipmentThe commander is holding a kit inspection on Friday.
kitthe equipment and clothing a soldier usesMake sure your kit is clean and complete.
the messthe place where personnel eat and sometimes socializeLunch is served in the mess from 12:00.
leaveofficial time off from dutyShe's on leave until Monday.
to be on callto be ready to come to work at short noticeI'm on call this weekend, so I can't travel far.

Exercise 4: Reading

A Week to Remember

Sergeant Marco Galli works in the logistics company of a multinational brigade headquarters in southern Europe. On a normal day, his routine is simple. He arrives at 06:45, takes roll call for his section at 07:30 and then spends most of the morning checking stores and vehicles. He usually eats lunch in the sergeants' mess, and three afternoons a week he has PT with his section. Once or twice a month his name is on the duty roster as duty NCO, so he stays on base overnight and is on call for any problems.

This week, however, is different. A team from higher headquarters is visiting, and the whole company is preparing for a major inspection. Marco's soldiers are working longer shifts, and nobody is taking leave until Friday. “Everyone is a bit tired at the moment,” Marco admits. “But inspections are useful. They show us what we do well and what we need to fix before a real deployment.”

  1. What does Marco do at 07:30?
  2. How often does he have PT?
  3. What happens when he is duty NCO?
  4. Why is this week different?
  5. Marco says inspections are useful. Do you agree? Why or why not?
