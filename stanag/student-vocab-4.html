What this lesson is for

Why this matters: on a mission you report what happened, why it happened and what was done, often to people from other countries. Briefings, incident reports and the JFLT all expect clear cause-and-effect language and the passive.

You will know

  • mission words: deployment, mandate, ceasefire, patrol, checkpoint, buffer zone, rules of engagement
  • formal verbs used in reports: deploy, monitor, escort, de-escalate, establish

You will understand

  • how because, so, due to and as a result link a cause and its effect
  • why reports use the passive: A checkpoint was set up. (Who did it is less important.)

You will be able to

  • describe a mission, its mandate and the situation on the ground
  • report an incident: what happened, what was done and what the result was

Common mistakes to watch for

  • The patrol was send to the village. The patrol was sent to the village. (Passive = be + past participle.)
  • Two sections are in patrol tonight. Two sections are on patrol tonight. Also on a mission.
  • The vehicle stopped in the checkpoint. The vehicle stopped at the checkpoint. But in the buffer zone, and arrive in an area.
  • We were deployed in Lebanon. We were deployed to Lebanon. (deployed to a country or area)
  • With the mandate, the force protects civilians. Under the mandate, the force protects civilians. The agreement was signed by both sides.
  • The mandate is renewed by UN Security Council. It is renewed by the UN Security Council. (the with things that are unique or already known: the mandate, the buffer zone)
  • The civilians need protection. Civilians need protection. Peacekeeping is difficult. (No article for general plural or uncountable nouns.)
  • There was incident at the checkpoint. There was an incident at the checkpoint. (a / an the first time: an incident, a checkpoint)

Key vocabulary

Word or phraseMeaningExample
deploymentthe movement of troops or units to an area for a missionOur deployment lasted six months.
mandatethe official authority and tasks given to a mission, often by the UN Security CouncilThe mandate allows the force to protect civilians.
peacekeepinghelping to keep the peace after a conflict, usually with the agreement of both sidesCanada has a long history of peacekeeping.
ceasefirean agreement to stop fightingThe ceasefire was signed in March.
to monitorto watch and check a situation over timeObservers monitor the ceasefire line.
patrola group that moves around an area to observe and provide security; also the activityThe patrol left the camp at dawn.
checkpointa place where vehicles and people are stopped and checkedA new checkpoint was set up on the main road.
buffer zonean area between two sides that neither side may enter with armed forcesFarmers need permission to work in the buffer zone.
civilianspeople who are not members of the armed forcesProtecting civilians is a top priority.
NGO (non-governmental organization)an independent aid or humanitarian groupNGOs distributed food in the villages.
rules of engagement (ROE)the orders that state when and how force may be usedEvery soldier must know the rules of engagement.
to escalate / to de-escalateto become (or make something) more serious / less seriousThe officer stayed calm and de-escalated the situation.
rotationthe regular replacement of one unit by another on a missionOur battalion arrived with the third rotation.
local authoritiesthe officials who govern a town or regionThe patrol met the local authorities every week.

Exercise 2: Cause and effect

Tap a first half, then tap the second half that completes it. Notice the words that show cause and effect.

  • Because the two sides signed a ceasefire, = the fighting stopped in most areas.
  • The bridge was damaged by floods, = so the patrol had to use a longer route.
  • Due to rising tension near the border, = two extra checkpoints were set up.
  • The mandate was extended for another year. = As a result, a new rotation was planned.
  • The commander met village leaders every week. = Consequently, trust between the force and local people grew.
  • Several NGOs left the area = because of the poor security situation.

Exercise 3: Formal and everyday words

Reports and briefings often use formal verbs. Tap a formal verb, then tap its everyday meaning.

  • to deploy = to send troops to an area
  • to monitor = to watch closely over time
  • to escort = to go with someone to protect them
  • to de-escalate = to make a situation calmer
  • to negotiate = to talk in order to reach an agreement
  • to establish = to set up or create

Exercise 5: Reading

Small Problems Stay Small

In this fictional mission, a multinational force of about 4,000 personnel monitors a ceasefire between two former enemies. The force was deployed after a peace agreement was signed, and its mandate is renewed every year by the UN Security Council. Its main tasks are to patrol the buffer zone, operate checkpoints and support the local authorities.

Most days are quiet, but small incidents happen regularly. Last month, a farmer drove his tractor into the buffer zone because the boundary was not clearly marked. Soldiers from the other side moved forward, and the situation quickly became tense. A patrol arrived, spoke to both sides and called a liaison officer. As a result, the farmer was escorted back and the soldiers returned to their positions. Nobody was hurt.

“Incidents like this are rarely about weapons,” says one patrol commander. “They are about misunderstandings. Our job is to de-escalate early, communicate clearly and stay impartial. If people trust us, small problems stay small.”

Exercise 6: Speaking

Make a few notes for each question. We will talk about them in our lesson.

  1. Describe a mission you served on or know well (or an imaginary one): where it was, how long it lasted and what its mandate was. Do not share any sensitive details.
  2. What does a typical patrol or checkpoint shift involve? What can go wrong?
  3. Describe a situation where good communication solved a problem, on a mission, on duty or at work.
  4. Challenge: Some people argue that peacekeeping forces should have stronger mandates and be allowed to use more force to protect civilians. Others say this puts their impartiality at risk. What is your view? What might happen if a mission lost the trust of local people?
