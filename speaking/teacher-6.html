Teacher Notes 6: Telling a Story (STAR)

Open the student page

Purpose

Give the student a four-step map for telling a story or describing an experience, so they always know what comes next and do not lose the thread.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, then homework.

Why STAR works

  • Stories are where nervous speakers often get lost: too much background, then no ending. STAR keeps the background short and makes the ending part of the plan.
  • Everyday conversation is full of small stories: "How was your weekend?", "What happened at work?". Job interviews ask for them directly: "Tell me about a time when..."
  • STAR stories use mostly the past simple, so the student can focus on order and flow rather than new grammar.
  • A short STAR story fits inside A.R.E. and PREP. It is the strongest kind of Evidence or Example.

How to run it

TimeStageWhat to do
5 minPREP checkStudent gives their PREP homework answer. Praise the ending first.
8 minPresent STARGo through Part A, then tell your own STAR story. A real story from your life works better than the model in Part B, and it shows the student that ordinary events make good stories.
7 minPut it in orderStudent numbers the sentences in Part C, then tells the story aloud in their own words without reading.
12 minBuild a story togetherUse the problem-solving story from their Lesson 5 homework. Prompt each step: "Where and when was it?" "What did you need to do?" "What did you do first? Then?" "What happened in the end?" Then the student tells the whole story with no prompts.
10 minStory roundStudent chooses two prompts from Part D and tells each story. Track whether they hit S, T, A and R. No correction during this activity.
8 minConnect and wrap upAsk an opinion question. The student answers with PREP, using a short STAR story as the Example (Part E). Then feedback, homework, and a reminder to bring a real situation to practise in Lesson 7.

Answer key: Part C

Correct order from top to bottom: 3, 1, 4, 2.

Correction tips

  • The most common problem is a long Situation. If the background goes past about 20 seconds, say "And then?" to move them to the Task.
  • Do not correct past tense forms during the story. Write them down and review them at the end.
  • If the Result is just "and that's it", ask "What did you learn?" A lesson learned makes a strong ending.

If the student asks...

  • "My life is boring. I don't have any stories." Small stories are fine: a late train, a broken phone, a funny mistake. Listeners enjoy ordinary stories.
  • "Does my story have to be true?" In conversation practice and exams, a believable story is fine. In job interviews, use true ones.
  • "What is the difference between Task and Action?" The Task is what you needed to do, or the problem. The Action is what you actually did.

Exam link

IELTS Speaking Part 2 cue cards often begin "Describe a time when...". STAR gives students a plan that fills the full one to two minutes. It works the same way for experience questions in Cambridge and Aptis, and for behavioural questions in job interviews.

On the web page

The student page keeps all of the lesson (goal, Parts A to F, the check-yourself list and the homework) and adds three games. Wrong answers open a short "why?" explanation. There are no timers.

  1. Part C: Put it in order (tap a number 1 to 4 for each sentence). Top to bottom: 3, 1, 4, 2 (Situation = Last winter..., Task = couldn't find my keys, Action = called my neighbour, Result = In the end...). The word-order game builds single sentences, so the sentence order is done by numbering, as on the Doc.
  2. Part C practice: Match the steps (matching, 4 pairs) with a new story, "a time you helped someone": S = Last summer, I was waiting for a bus in the city centre. T = An old man was lost, and he needed to find the hospital. A = So I looked at the map on my phone and walked there with him. R = In the end, he arrived on time, and I learned that a little help matters.
  3. Part C practice 2: Task or Action? (tap, 8 items). Answers: Task, Action, Task, Action, Task, Action, Task, Action. It practises the "difference between Task and Action" question above.

The Part D checklist is not marked; it is sent to you with the student's answers.
