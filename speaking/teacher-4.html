Teacher Notes 4: Giving Your Opinion (A.R.E.)

Open the student page

Objective: The student will use the A.R.E. framework to state, explain, and support an opinion clearly in a conversation.

Level: B1 (Intermediate)

Lesson time: 25 minutes

Materials: Shared screen or digital whiteboard showing the A.R.E. acronym; the student version of this lesson.

Step 1: The Hook and the Problem (4 minutes)

What to do: Ask a quick, casual opinion question to see how the student naturally structures their thoughts.

Ask: “In your opinion, is it better to live in a big city or a small village?”

The goal: Let them answer freely. B1 students usually give a short answer such as “I like the city because it has restaurants.” Note what they say; you will come back to it.

Transition: Validate the answer, then introduce the tool. “Great point! Today I want to show you a simple trick called the A.R.E. method. It will make your opinions in English sound more complete and confident.”

Step 2: Introducing the A.R.E. Framework (6 minutes)

What to do: Display the acronym and explain the three parts in short fragments.

LetterPartQuestion it answersConnector
AAssertion (your opinion)What do you think?I believe... / I think... / In my opinion...
RReason (why)Why do you think that?because...
EEvidence (an example)How do you know?For example... / In my experience...

Teacher's model example

  • Assertion: “I believe living in a big city is better...”
  • Reason: “...because there are far more career and networking opportunities.”
  • Evidence: “For example, my friend moved to Toronto and found a marketing job within two weeks.”

Tip: Go back to the student's answer from Step 1 and show which letters they already used. Most will have A and part of R, but no E. This makes the gap concrete.

Step 3: Guided Practice, Build It Together (6 minutes)

What to do: Give a fresh topic and guide the student line by line.

Topic: “Should university education be free for everyone?”

  1. You: “Give me your Assertion. Yes or no?” Student responds.
  2. You: “Now add your Reason. Why? Start with because.” Student responds.
  3. You: “Excellent. Now give me some Evidence. It can be a personal story, something from the news, or a general example.” Student responds.
  4. Ask the student to say all three parts together as one answer.

Step 4: Independent Fluency Practice (6 minutes)

What to do: Let the student choose one of two topics and give a full A.R.E. answer without interruption.

  1. “Is working from home better than working in an office?”
  2. “Should fast food restaurants be banned?”

Your role: Listen for the structure. Do not correct small grammar mistakes right away. Just track whether they hit A, R, and E.

TopicARENotes for later
     

Feedback: Praise the structure first. “Your Reason was very clear! Your Evidence really proved the point.” Then, if time allows, share one or two grammar notes.

Step 5: Wrap-Up and Takeaway (3 minutes)

Ask: “The next time you are in a discussion or a meeting and someone asks your opinion, what are the three steps to remember?”

Expected answer: “Assertion, Reason, Evidence.”

Closing: “Perfect. Keep this framework in mind this week whenever you explain your thoughts.” Preview Lesson 5: PREP adds one more step, so your opinion ends as strongly as it starts.

Homework

The student records a one-minute A.R.E. answer to one topic from the back-pocket list (see the student sheet) and sends it before the next lesson.

Back-pocket topics

  • Is it better to rent or buy a home?
  • Should people retire at 60?
  • Are smartphones making us less social?
  • Is it important to learn about history?
  • Should tourists pay extra to visit popular cities?
  • Is it better to travel alone or with friends?

On the web page

The student page keeps Parts A to D, Remember and Homework, and adds four exercises. Part C (Build it together) stays as three boxes to fill in with you during Step 3; it is not marked. The “Check yourself” ticks are not marked either. Both arrive with “Send my answers to Bill”.

  • Exercise 1, Which part is this? (tap A, R or E, 8 lines): 1 A, 2 R, 3 E, 4 A, 5 E, 6 R, 7 E, 8 A. The connectors (I think, because, For example, In my experience) are the clue.
  • Exercise 2, Match the opinion and the reason (tap to match, 5 pairs from the back-pocket topics): rent = move easily for a new job; retire at 60 = still love their work; history = understand the world today; tourists pay extra = money to clean and repair the streets; travel with friends = share the costs and the memories.
  • Exercise 3, Build an A.R.E. answer (word order, 3 short answers): 1 I think breakfast is important because it gives me energy. For example, I work better. 2 I believe dogs are great pets because they are loyal. For example, my dog waits for me. 3 I think walking is better because it is healthy. In my experience, I sleep better.
  • Exercise 4 (Part D), Your turn (Claude checks the answer): the student writes a full A.R.E. answer to one of the two Step 4 questions. Claude checks for A, R and E first and names any missing part, then looks at grammar gently. It never judges the opinion. Use it after Step 4, or as extra homework.
