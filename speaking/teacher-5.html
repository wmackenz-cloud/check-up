Teacher Notes 5: Ending Strong (PREP)

Open the student page

Purpose

Build on A.R.E. by adding a final Point, so the student's opinions end as clearly as they start. Practise in work-meeting situations and exam-style opinion questions.

Lesson time: 50 minutes, then homework.

Why PREP works

  • Nervous speakers often trail off at the end of an answer ("...so, yeah, I don't know"). The final Point gives them a planned exit, so they know how the answer ends before they start.
  • The listener hears the opinion twice. Even if the middle is messy, the message lands.
  • PREP is A.R.E. plus one step. The student is not learning a new system, just finishing the one they already know.

Tell the student directly: repeating your point at the end is not a mistake or bad style. It is what confident speakers do in meetings.

How to run it

TimeStageWhat to do
5 minA.R.E. checkAsk a quick opinion question. The student answers with A.R.E. Notice how they end: most trail off or stop suddenly. Point this out kindly.
8 minPresent PREPGo through Part A and the example. Model your own answer too. Stress that the final Point uses new words, not an exact copy of the first one.
7 minWhat is missing?Student reads the three answers in Part B, names the missing step, then says the full answer aloud with the step added.
10 minBuild it togetherPart C topic. Prompt step by step, as in Lesson 4: "Your Point? Your Reason? An Example? Now say your Point again." Then the student says the whole answer twice. The second time is usually faster and smoother.
12 minMeeting simulationYou play a manager announcing the changes in Part D. The student responds to each with PREP. No correction during this activity.
8 minFeedback and wrap-upPraise the structure first, especially strong endings. Then give up to three language points. Set the homework. Preview Lesson 6: a formula for telling stories.

Answer key: Part B

  1. Missing: Reason (for example, "...because it reduces traffic").
  2. Missing: Example.
  3. Missing: the first Point. The answer starts with "Because..."

Adapting for level

ElementA2 to B1B1 to B2
Final Point phrasesOne phrase: "So that's why I think..."Varied: "That's why...", "So overall...", "For all these reasons..."
ReasonsOne reasonTwo reasons, linked with "Also..." or "On top of that..."
Meeting simulationEveryday changes, read slowlyReal work issues, plus one follow-up question from you
CorrectionOne error, the most usefulUp to three, student fixes them first

Correction tips

  • If the final Point is word for word the same as the first, praise it anyway, then show one way to rephrase it.
  • If the student skips the Example, use the open-palm signal from Lesson 2 instead of interrupting.
  • Keep a note of the strongest PREP answer they give. Read it back to them at the end. Hearing their own good answer is powerful.

If the student asks...

  • "Isn't repeating my point boring?" In conversation, no. It tells the listener you have finished and helps them remember your idea.
  • "What if I don't have an example?" Use a general one ("Many people...") or an imagined one ("Imagine a parent with two young children...").
  • "What if someone disagrees with me?" Use PREP again. Their idea is not a problem; it is a chance to give your Point a second time.

Exam link

IELTS Speaking Part 3, Cambridge B2 First Speaking Part 4 and Aptis Speaking Part 4 all ask for opinions with reasons. A PREP answer runs about 30 to 45 seconds, which is the right length, and it stops students from finishing too early.

On the web page

The student page keeps all of the lesson (goal, Parts A to E, the check-yourself list and the homework) and adds four games. Wrong answers open a short "why?" explanation. There are no timers.

  1. Part A practice: Sort the phrases (sorting game, 12 phrases into P / R / E / P again). P: I think..., In my opinion... R: ...because..., The main reason is..., On top of that... E: For example..., In my experience..., Imagine a parent with two young children... P again: So that's why I think..., So overall..., That's why..., For all these reasons...
  2. Part A practice 2: Build a PREP answer (word order, 4 sentences) to "Should shops open on Sundays?": In my opinion, shops should open on Sundays. / The main reason is that many people work all week. / For example, my brother can only shop on Sundays. / So that's why I think Sunday shopping is a good idea.
  3. Part B: What is missing? (tap the step). Items 1 to 3 are the Doc's: Reason, Example, first Point. Two extra items: 4 final Point (swimming), 5 Example (working from home).
  4. Part D practice: Write your reply (Claude checks the writing). The student writes a PREP reply to one of the three announcements; Claude marks it correct only when all four steps are there, and says kindly which step is missing.

Part C (the four boxes) and the Part D checklist are not marked; they are sent to you with the student's answers.
