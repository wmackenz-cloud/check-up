Teacher Notes 7: Real-World Rehearsal

Open the student page

Purpose

The student rehearses a real situation they fear, using every tool from the series, and leaves with a plan for keeping their confidence growing.

Lesson time: 50 minutes.

Why rehearsal works

  • Fear is strongest for situations the student has never practised. A rehearsal turns an unknown into something familiar.
  • Doing the same role-play twice lets the student feel the improvement in real time.
  • Comparing the freeze map ratings from Lesson 1 with today's ratings gives the student hard evidence of progress.

How to run it

TimeStageWhat to do
5 minWarm-upAsk the student to tell you the STAR story from their Lesson 6 homework. Celebrate the structure before anything else.
5 minChoose the scenarioLook back at their freeze map from Lesson 1. Student chooses one situation rated 3 or higher. Agree the details: who they are speaking to, where, and what they need.
5 minPrepareStudent fills in Part B: three things they will probably need to say and one rescue phrase they will probably need. Keep it short so it does not become a script.
10 minRole-play, round 1You play the other person. Be realistic but kind. Include one small surprise: a question they did not expect, or a word they may not know. No correction.
5 minDebriefAsk: "Where did you feel stuck? What did you do?" Point out every tool they used, including A.R.E., PREP or STAR when the situation called for an opinion or a story. Offer one or two phrases that would help.
10 minRole-play, round 2Same scenario, slightly harder: speak a little faster or add a second surprise. B1 to B2 students can try a second, harder scenario instead.
7 minLook backStudent fills in Part C, rating the situation and their confidence again. Compare with Lessons 1 and 3.
3 minKeep goingComplete Part D together.

Scenario ideas

LevelScenarios
A2 to B1Ordering food and asking about an ingredient; asking for directions; making a doctor's appointment by phone; returning an item to a shop
B1 to B2Introducing yourself in a work meeting; disagreeing politely with a colleague; complaining about a service; small talk with a neighbour or new colleague

Correction tips

  • No correction during either role-play. Save it all for the debrief.
  • Praise strategies first: "You used 'Could you say that again?' when I spoke fast, and the conversation kept going."
  • For the surprise, keep it realistic. The goal is a recoverable challenge, not a trap.

If the student asks...

  • "What if the real person is not as patient as you?" Some won't be. But most people are patient when you use a clear rescue phrase. Impatience usually comes from silence, not from a request to repeat.
  • "Should I memorise what I'll say?" Prepare key words and one or two sentences, not a full script. A script breaks the moment the other person says something unexpected.
  • "Is that the end of the course?" It is the end of this series. Confidence keeps growing with regular speaking practice, which is a natural next step.

After the series

The before-and-after ratings are the most persuasive evidence a nervous student will see. They also make an honest bridge into regular conversation lessons.

On the web page

The student page keeps all of the lesson (goal, toolkit, Parts B to D and the closing reminder) and adds two review games and an optional writing check. Wrong answers open a short "why?" explanation. There are no timers.

  1. Part A practice: Which tool fits? (tap, 8 moments; options: a rescue phrase / a filler / A.R.E. or PREP / STAR). Answers: 1 rescue phrase (waiter speaks fast), 2 A.R.E. or PREP (new office), 3 STAR (interview: solved a problem), 4 filler (need one or two seconds), 5 rescue phrase (can't find "receipt"), 6 STAR (funny holiday story), 7 rescue phrase (fixing "I go there yesterday"), 8 A.R.E. or PREP (team lunch on Fridays).
  2. Part A practice 2: Sort your toolkit (sorting game, 13 phrases). Rescue phrase: Sorry, could you say that again? / Could you speak more slowly, please? / What's the word? It's a thing for... / Sorry, I mean... Filler: Well... / Let me see... / So... Opinion (PREP): In my opinion... / The main reason is... / So overall... Story (STAR): A few months ago... / My job was to... / As a result...
  3. Part B: Prepare. The three "things I will probably need to say" have an optional Check button: Claude looks at the English and suggests a shorter, simpler version if needed, so it does not grow into a script. The situation and the rescue phrase are plain boxes.

Parts C (the before-and-after ratings) and D (the confidence plan) are plain boxes and tick boxes, not marked. They are sent to you with the student's answers, which gives you the Lesson 1 and Lesson 7 numbers side by side.
