Goal: Understand why you freeze when you speak English, and find out which situations are hardest for you.

Time: 50-minute lesson, then 5 minutes a day

You are not alone

Almost every adult learning a language freezes sometimes.

Your brain is trying to be perfect and fast at the same time. In this course, you will learn what to do when it happens.

Remember: the goal is not perfect English. The goal is to keep talking.

Why the freeze happens

A freeze is a normal brain reaction. It is not a sign that you are bad at English.

There are four common reasons:

  • Stress blocks the words. When you are nervous, the words are still in your head, but stress stops you from finding them.
  • Translating in your head. You build the sentence in your language, then change it into English. This is slow, and your brain gets too full. Short phrases you know by heart skip this step.
  • Being perfect. You are afraid of a mistake, of sounding silly, or of your accent, so you say nothing.
  • Knowing vs saying. On a worksheet, the word is in front of you. When you speak, you must find it yourself, quickly. So you can do well on a worksheet and still freeze.

Speaking time with Bill

Choose an easy situation from your freeze map (a 1 or a 2). Talk about it with Bill for a short time. Bill will not correct you. Then talk about the same topic again.

Which time felt easier?

Remember: native speakers pause, start again and forget words all the time. The difference is that they keep going.

Part C: Your homework

Every day, say three sentences aloud in English about your day. Record them on your phone if you can. Nobody needs to hear them.

Example:

  • This morning I had coffee at home.
  • I worked on my computer for three hours.
  • In the evening I watched a film.

You can also talk about things you do every day: I walk to work and home every day.

Then describe three everyday objects around you without using their names (for example, “It is a black object used for typing”).
