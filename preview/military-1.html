Key vocabulary

Word or phraseMeaningExample
ranka person's official level in the armed forcesWhat rank is she? She's a captain.
officera commissioned leader (lieutenant and above)The officers met before the exercise.
NCO (non-commissioned officer)a leader who rose from the ranks, such as a corporal or sergeantExperienced NCOs train new soldiers.
chain of commandthe order of authority, from the highest level to the lowestRequests must go through the chain of command.
commanding officer (CO)the officer in charge of a unit, often a battalionThe CO approved the plan.
second-in-commandthe person who takes over when the commander is absentThe second-in-command ran the briefing.
to report toto have someone as your direct superiorI report to the company commander.
to be in charge ofto be responsible forHe is in charge of logistics.
to be posted toto be sent to work in a unit or placeShe was posted to Brussels last year.
headquarters (HQ)the place or staff that commands a unitThe orders came from brigade headquarters.
barracksbuildings where soldiers liveThe barracks are near the training area.

Exercise 5: Reading

A Day in the Life of a Company Commander

Captain Elena Moretti commands Bravo Company, part of an infantry battalion based in northern Italy. The company has about 120 soldiers organized into three platoons and a small headquarters section. Each platoon is led by a lieutenant, supported by an experienced sergeant.

Elena's day usually starts at 07:00 with a meeting with her platoon commanders and her company sergeant major. They review the training plan, discuss any problems with equipment or personnel, and agree on priorities. At 09:00 she reports to the battalion's commanding officer, a lieutenant colonel, to update him on the company's readiness.

“The hardest part of the job is not the training,” she says. “It's making sure information moves in both directions. My soldiers need to understand why we're doing something, and my CO needs to know about problems before they become serious.”

Exercise 7: Prepositions and articles

Practise prepositions and articles with the vocabulary from this lesson.

Part A: Prepositions

Watch out

  • report to someone: I report to the company commander.
  • in charge of / in command of a unit: She is in charge of logistics. He is in command of the battalion.
  • posted to a unit or place: He was posted to Rome.
  • on a course, on leave; serve in the army: She is on a course this week. I serve in the army.

Tap the missing preposition in each sentence.

  1. Lieutenant Rossi reports the company commander.
  2. Captain Ferri is in charge Alpha Company.
  3. Last year she was posted a NATO headquarters in Brussels.
  4. Colonel Bruno is command of the brigade.
  5. Two of our sergeants are a course this month, so we are short of NCOs.
  6. I have served the army since 2012.
  7. The second-in-command takes over when the CO is leave.
  8. A platoon is made up three or four sections.

Part B: Articles

Watch out

  • a / an with a job or rank after be: She is a captain. He is an officer.
  • No article with a rank before a name (Captain Moretti, not “the Captain Moretti”) and for general ideas (Discipline is important).
  • the for a person or thing everyone in the unit knows: the CO, the chain of command.

Tap a, an, the or – (nothing) for each gap.

  2. Captain Moretti commands Bravo Company.
  3. Every morning I report to CO at 09:00.
  4. Elena is captain in an infantry battalion.
  5. In any army, discipline is important.
  6. When the CO is away, second-in-command takes over.
  7. Our battalion got new commanding officer last month.
  8. Requests must go through chain of command.
