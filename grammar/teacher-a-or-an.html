Teacher Notes: A or An? Vowels, Sounds and Countable Nouns

Open the student worksheet

Why students get this wrong

Two different problems. First, many students learn "vowel letter means an", which gives an university and a hour. The rule is about the first sound, so this lesson teaches vowel letters quickly and then moves straight to sounds. Second, speakers of languages with no articles (Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Polish, Turkish) often leave the article out altogether: I have phone, She is teacher. For these students, a dropped article is usually a bigger problem than choosing between a and an.

How to explain it

Say: "A and an mean one. Use them with one thing you can count. Choose an when the next word starts with a vowel sound, so the words flow."

The mouth test: have the student say "a apple" slowly, notice the stop between the two vowel sounds, then say "an apple". Adults remember the reason better than the rule.

Test questions: Can I count it? Is it one? What sound does the next word start with?

If the student asks...

  • "Why a university? U is a vowel." Because we say "you-niversity". The first sound is a y sound, which is a consonant sound. The same goes for a uniform, a European, a useful tip.
  • "Why an hour? H is a consonant." The h is silent, so the first sound is a vowel. The same goes for an honest person, an honour. With a spoken h, use a: a house, a hotel.
  • "What about letters and abbreviations?" Go by the name of the letter: an MBA, an X-ray, an FBI agent ("em", "ex", "eff"), but a UN report ("you").
  • "What do I use with uncountable nouns?" Nothing, or some: I need (some) advice. To count them, add a unit: a piece of advice, a glass of water.
  • "When do I use the?" That's the next step. A is for something new or any one; the is for something you both know. Grammar Trap 3 covers it.

Exercise notes and answers with reasons

Exercise 1: Vowel or consonant?

Vowel letter: apple, egg, idea, orange, umbrella. Consonant letter: book, table, car, house, lemon. These all follow the simple rule on purpose, so the student succeeds before the tricky cases.

Exercise 2: A or an?

a: university, European city, one-way ticket ("w" sound), uniform, house, car. an: hour, honest person (silent h), MBA ("em"), X-ray ("ex"), orange, idea.

Exercise 3: A, an or nothing?

  1. –: advice is uncountable (or some advice).
  2. an umbrella: vowel sound.
  3. –: books is plural.
  4. an hour: silent h.
  5. a glass: the glass is countable, even though water isn't.
  6. –: information is uncountable.
  7. an engineer: vowel sound.
  8. a new job: the article goes with new.
  9. a university: "you" sound.
  10. –: money is uncountable (any money also works).
  11. an honest mistake: silent h.
  12. a useful tip: "you" sound.

Exercise 4: Find and correct the mistake

  1. a phone: one countable thing needs an article.
  2. a teacher: consonant sound.
  3. some advice / advice: uncountable, never an advice.
  4. an hour: silent h.
  5. a European: "you" sound.
  6. some new furniture: uncountable, no plural and no a.

Exercise 5: Word order

  1. My brother is an honest man.
  2. I waited for an hour at the station.
  3. He studies at a university in London.
  4. There is an umbrella in my bag.
  5. Can I have some advice, please?
  6. I need some information about the course.
