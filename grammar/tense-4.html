What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • say how long you have been doing something: I've been learning English for two years.
  • explain a result you can see: I'm hot because I've been running.
  • ask How long have you been...?

You will know

  • have / has been + -ing
  • for, since, How long...?
  • state verbs: know, have, be, want

You will understand

  • activity (I've been painting) vs result (I've painted two rooms)
  • I've known her (not been knowing)

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I am learning English for three years. I have been learning English for three years.
  • I have been knowing him since school. I have known him since school.
  • She has been work here since May. She has been working here since May.
  • How long you have been waiting? How long have you been waiting?
  • I've been reading three books this month. I've read three books this month.

How we use it

  • how long an activity has continued up to now: We've been waiting for forty minutes.
  • a recent activity with a result you can see: The ground is wet. It's been raining.
  • repeated actions up to now: He's been calling me all morning.

How to make it

PositiveI've been working. She's been working. ('s = has)
NegativeI haven't been sleeping well. He hasn't been working long.
QuestionHave you been waiting long? How long has she been living here?
Short answersYes, I have. No, I haven't. Yes, she has. No, she hasn't.

Words that often go with it: for (two hours, ages, a long time), since (9 o'clock, Monday, 2019), How long...?, all day, all morning, lately, recently

Tip: Activity or result? Use the continuous for the activity and how long: I've been cooking all afternoon. Use the simple for the result or how many: I've cooked three dishes. State verbs always use the simple: I've had this car for years.
