What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • tell a story with two past times and make the order clear
  • explain a past situation: I was hungry because I hadn't eaten.
  • talk about first experiences: I had never seen snow before I came to Canada.

You will know

  • had + past participle: had left, had seen
  • by the time, already, before, after
  • gone, eaten, forgotten, taken, written

You will understand

  • she left vs she had left
  • no past perfect needed with before / after

Common mistakes to watch for

  • When I arrived, she already left. When I arrived, she had already left.
  • By the time we got to the station, the train left. By the time we got to the station, the train had left.
  • I had went there before. I had gone there before.
  • I was nervous because I never flew before. I was nervous because I had never flown before.
  • Did you had finished when he called? Had you finished when he called?

How we use it

  • the earlier of two past actions: When we got to the cinema, the film had already started.
  • with by the time: By the time the ambulance came, he had stopped breathing.
  • to explain a reason in the past: She was upset because she had lost her ring.
  • experience up to a past moment: I had never eaten sushi before that night.

How to make it

PositiveI / you / he / she / we / they had finished. Short form: I'd finished.
NegativeShe hadn't eaten. We had not seen it.
QuestionHad you met him before? Where had they gone?
Short answersYes, I had. No, they hadn't.

Words that often go with it: by the time, already, just, never ... before, before, after, when, until, because

Tip: Draw a time line. The past simple is your main story point; the past perfect goes one step further back. When I got home, my wife cooked dinner (after I came). When I got home, my wife had cooked dinner (it was ready).
