What this lesson is for

Why this matters: We use will every day to offer help, make promises and decide things on the spot. Getting it right makes you sound friendly and natural.

You will know

  • the form will + base verb, and the negative won't (will not)
  • words that often go with will: I think, I don't think, I'm sure, probably, I promise

You will understand

  • that will is often for a decision you make now, while you are speaking
  • why we never put to, -s or -ing after will

You will be able to

  • offer help and make promises
  • make a quick decision, for example when you order food
  • ask polite questions with Will you...?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I will to phone you tonight. I will phone you tonight.
  • Will you to join us for lunch? Will you join us for lunch?
  • He wills be here soon. He will be here soon.
  • I don't will forget your birthday. I won't forget your birthday.
  • A: This suitcase is so heavy! B: I'm going to help you. A: This suitcase is so heavy! B: I'll help you.

How we use it

  • Instant decisions, made while you speak: The phone's ringing. I'll get it.
  • Offers and promises: I'll carry that for you. I won't tell anyone.
  • Predictions from opinion, often with I think or probably: I think it'll be a good party.
  • Requests: Will you close the door, please?

How to make it

PositiveI / you / he / she / we / they will work (short form: I'll, she'll, we'll)
NegativeI won't work = I will not work
QuestionWill you help me? What will you do?
Short answersYes, I will. / No, I won't.

Words that often go with it: I think, I don't think, I'm sure, probably, maybe, I promise, I hope; reactions like Oh!, Wait, Don't worry

Tip: Decided now, while speaking? Use will: I'll have the soup. Decided before? Use going to. And say I don't think he'll come, not I think he won't come.
