What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • describe your daily routine and your job
  • ask and answer questions like Where do you live? and What does your partner do?
  • talk about timetables: The train leaves at 8:15.

You will know

  • works, watches, studies, has
  • do and does
  • always, usually, often, sometimes, never

You will understand

  • -s only after he, she, it
  • Does she work? (not works)

Common mistakes to watch for

  • He work in a bank. He works in a bank.
  • Does she works on Saturdays? Does she work on Saturdays?
  • She don't like coffee. She doesn't like coffee.
  • Where you live? Where do you live?
  • I go always to bed late. I always go to bed late.

How we use it

  • habits and routines: I walk the dog every morning.
  • facts that are always true: Water freezes at 0 degrees.
  • jobs and long-term situations: My sister works for the city.
  • timetables: The bus leaves at 7:40.

How to make it

PositiveI / you / we / they work. He / she / it works.
NegativeI don't work. She doesn't work. (not doesn't works)
QuestionDo you work? Does he work? Where does she work?
Short answersYes, I do. No, I don't. Yes, she does. No, she doesn't.
Spellingwatch → watches, go → goes, study → studies, play → plays, have → has

Words that often go with it: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day, on Mondays, once a week, twice a year, in the morning

Tip: Only one -s in a sentence: She works, but Does she work? and She doesn't work. Frequency words go before the main verb but after be: I often cook. I'm often late.
