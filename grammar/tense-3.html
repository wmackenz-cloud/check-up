What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • talk about your life experience: I've never tried skiing.
  • give news: I've just found a new job.
  • say how long: I've known her for ten years.

You will know

  • have / has + past participle: gone, seen, written
  • ever, never, just, already, yet, for, since

You will understand

  • yesterday, in 2019 + past simple
  • for (length) vs since (start)

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I have seen him yesterday. I saw him yesterday.
  • I'm living here since 2020. I've lived here since 2020.
  • She has went home. She has gone home.
  • Are you ever been to Mexico? Have you ever been to Mexico?
  • I have finished already my work. I have already finished my work.

How we use it

  • life experience, with no time given: I've travelled to Peru twice.
  • news with a result now: I've lost my wallet, so I can't pay.
  • a situation from the past to now: We've lived in Mississauga for six years.
  • a time that isn't finished: I've had three coffees today.

How to make it

PositiveI've finished. She's finished. ('s = has)
NegativeI haven't finished. He hasn't called.
QuestionHave you finished? Has she called? How long have you lived here?
Short answersYes, I have. No, I haven't. Yes, he has. No, he hasn't.

Words that often go with it: ever, never, just, already, yet, so far, recently, today, this week, for + a length of time, since + a starting point. Past simple instead: yesterday, last year, ago, in 2018, When...?

Tip: No finished time with the present perfect. If you say when (yesterday, last week, two years ago), use the past simple: I've been to Halifax, but I went to Halifax in 2019.
