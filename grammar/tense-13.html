What this lesson is for

Why this matters: Use it to say that something will be finished before a time in the future. It helps you talk about deadlines, goals and plans at work and at home.

You will know

  • the form will have + past participle: I'll have finished
  • the time words by, by the time, before and by then

You will understand

  • that we stand at a point in the future and look back at something already complete
  • why we use the present simple after by the time: by the time you arrive, not will arrive

You will be able to

  • talk about deadlines: I'll have sent it by Friday.
  • say what you will have done by the end of the year

Common mistakes to watch for

  • By the time you will arrive, we will have eaten. By the time you arrive, we will have eaten.
  • I will have finish the report by Friday. I will have finished the report by Friday.
  • She will has left by 6. She will have left by 6.
  • By next year I have saved enough money. By next year I will have saved enough money.
  • They will had moved by June. They will have moved by June.

How we use it

  • Something finished before a future time: By Friday, I'll have sent all the invoices.
  • Something finished before another future event: By the time you get here, the movie will have started.
  • Goals and totals up to a future point: By December, we'll have paid off the car.

How to make it

PositiveI will have finished. / She'll have left.
NegativeWe won't have finished by 5. / He will not have arrived.
QuestionWill you have finished by Monday?
Short answersYes, I will. / No, I won't.

Words that often go with it: by Friday, by 2030, by then, by the time + present simple, before, by the end of, already, not ... yet

Tip: Future continuous is in the middle: At 8, I'll be eating. Future perfect is finished: By 8, I'll have eaten. After by the time, use the present simple, not will.
