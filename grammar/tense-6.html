What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • describe the background to a story: the weather, what people were doing
  • tell what you were doing when something happened
  • ask What were you doing? correctly

You will know

  • was / were + verb-ing
  • when and while
  • state verbs: know, want, believe, own

You will understand

  • I was cooking when the phone rang
  • What were you doing at 8? vs What did you do at 8?

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I was knowing the answer. I knew the answer.
  • We was watching TV when you called. We were watching TV when you called.
  • When the phone rang, I cooked dinner. When the phone rang, I was cooking dinner.
  • While I was walking home, I was seeing an accident. While I was walking home, I saw an accident.
  • What you were doing at nine o'clock? What were you doing at nine o'clock?

How we use it

  • an action in progress at a past time: At 7 p.m. I was driving home.
  • a longer action interrupted by a short one (past simple): I was cooking when the power went out.
  • the background to a story: It was raining and people were hurrying to work.
  • two actions happening at the same time: While I was working, the kids were playing.

How to make it

PositiveI / he / she / it was working. You / we / they were working.
NegativeI wasn't sleeping. They weren't listening.
QuestionWere you sleeping? What was she doing?
Short answersYes, I was. No, they weren't.

Words that often go with it: when, while, as, at 8 o'clock last night, at that moment, all day yesterday

Tip: Think of a film: the past continuous is the long scene in the background (I was walking), and the past simple is the short event that happens in it (I saw a fox). Use while with the long action and when usually with the short one.
