What this lesson is for

Why this matters: Use it to say what you will be in the middle of at a time in the future, and to ask polite questions about people's plans. It sounds natural and friendly in everyday English.

You will know

  • the form will be + verb-ing: I'll be working
  • time phrases like at 8 pm tomorrow, this time next week and when you arrive

You will understand

  • the difference between an action in progress at a future time and a single future action
  • why Will you be using the car? sounds softer and more polite than Will you use the car?

You will be able to

  • say what you will be doing at a certain time tomorrow or next week
  • ask politely about someone's plans before you ask a favour

Common mistakes to watch for

  • This time tomorrow I will working. This time tomorrow I will be working.
  • Will you using the car tonight? Will you be using the car tonight?
  • At 9 pm I will be watch the game. At 9 pm I will be watching the game.
  • Next week I will to be travelling. Next week I will be travelling.
  • Soon I will be knowing the answer. Soon I will know the answer.

How we use it

  • An action in progress at a time in the future: At 10 am tomorrow, I'll be sitting in a meeting.
  • An action that will be in progress when something else happens: When you land, I'll be waiting at the gate.
  • Polite questions about plans: Will you be using the printer this afternoon?
  • Things that will happen anyway, as part of a plan or routine: I'll be passing the bank, so I can drop it off.

How to make it

PositiveI will be working. / She'll be travelling.
NegativeWe won't be staying long. / He will not be coming.
QuestionWill you be using the car? / What will you be doing at 6?
Short answersYes, I will. / No, I won't.

Words that often go with it: at 8 pm tomorrow, this time next week, at noon on Friday, when you arrive, all day tomorrow, in two hours, still

Tip: Think of a photo taken in the future: When you arrive, I'll be eating (I'm in the middle of it). Compare When you arrive, I'll eat (I start then). State verbs like know, want and own don't take -ing.
