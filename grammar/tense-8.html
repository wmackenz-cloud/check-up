What this lesson is for

You will be able to

  • say how long something had been happening before another past moment
  • give the reason for a past situation you could see or feel
  • ask How long had you been ... ? correctly

You will know

  • had been + verb-ing: had been waiting
  • for, since, all day, how long
  • state verbs: had known, had had

You will understand

  • had been painting (activity) vs had painted (result)
  • Her eyes were red because she had been crying.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I had been knowing him for years. I had known him for years.
  • When I met her, she was living in Toronto for five years. When I met her, she had been living in Toronto for five years.
  • She was tired because she has been working all day. She was tired because she had been working all day.
  • I had been write emails all morning. I had been writing emails all morning.
  • How long you had been living there? How long had you been living there?

How we use it

  • how long an activity went on before a past moment: I had been driving for six hours when I stopped.
  • the reason for a past result: The grass was wet because it had been raining.
  • with for and since in a past story: They had been dating since 2019 when they got engaged.

How to make it

PositiveI / she / they had been working. Short form: I'd been working.
NegativeHe hadn't been sleeping well. We had not been listening.
QuestionHad you been waiting long? How long had she been living there?
Short answersYes, I had. No, she hadn't.

Words that often go with it: for two hours, since Monday, all day, all morning, how long, when, before, by the time, because

Tip: Ask yourself: how long or how many? How long is about the activity, so use had been + -ing: I had been reading for an hour. How many is about the result, so use had + past participle: I had read fifty pages.
