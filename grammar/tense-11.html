What this lesson is for

Why this matters: When something is already arranged, like an appointment, a booking or a meeting with friends, English speakers usually use the present continuous. It is the most natural way to talk about your calendar.

You will know

  • the form am / is / are + -ing with a future time word
  • the difference between an arrangement, a plan and a quick decision

You will understand

  • why I'm meeting Sam at six talks about the future even though it looks like the present
  • that timetables (trains, films, classes) use the present simple: The train leaves at 8.

You will be able to

  • talk about your appointments and weekend plans
  • ask What are you doing on Saturday? and answer naturally
  • choose between present continuous, going to and will

Common mistakes to watch for

  • I see the doctor tomorrow at 10. I'm seeing the doctor tomorrow at 10.
  • What do you do on Saturday evening? What are you doing on Saturday evening?
  • We flying to Calgary next Tuesday. We're flying to Calgary next Tuesday.
  • I will have lunch with a client at one. It's booked. I'm having lunch with a client at one. It's booked.
  • A: Can you come tomorrow? B: Sorry, I work late. A: Can you come tomorrow? B: Sorry, I'm working late.

How we use it

  • Arrangements: plans with a time, a place or other people, often booked: I'm having lunch with Sara on Thursday.
  • Always with a future time, so the listener knows it is not now: We're flying to Montreal next Friday.
  • Timetables use the present simple: The bus leaves at 8:05. The film starts at 7.
  • Compare: I'm seeing the dentist at 3. (arranged) / I'm going to get fit. (intention) / I'll call you back. (decided now)

How to make it

PositiveI'm meeting Tom at six. She's flying home on Sunday. They're coming tonight.
NegativeWe aren't coming to the party on Saturday. I'm not working tomorrow.
QuestionWhat are you doing tonight? Are you working on Sunday?
Short answersYes, I am. / No, we aren't.

Words that often go with it: tonight, tomorrow, at 3 o'clock, on Friday, next week, this weekend; words like booked, appointment, tickets, in my calendar

Tip: If it is in your calendar (a time, a place, other people), use the present continuous. If it is just an idea in your head, use going to. If you decide now, use will.
