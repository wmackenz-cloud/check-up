What this lesson is for

Why this matters: Use it to say how long something will have been going on when we reach a future time. It is perfect for anniversaries, careers, long trips and long waits.

You will know

  • the form will have been + verb-ing: I'll have been working
  • the time words for + a length of time and by + a date or time

You will understand

  • that we stand at a future point and look back at how long an action will have continued
  • the difference between how long (future perfect continuous) and how many (future perfect)

You will be able to

  • say how long you will have been living, working or studying somewhere by a future date
  • talk about long waits and long trips: We'll have been driving for ten hours.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • By June I will have been work here for ten years. By June I will have been working here for ten years.
  • By next month I will be living here for a year. By next month I will have been living here for a year.
  • By May I will have been knowing her for ten years. By May I will have known her for ten years.
  • By the time she will retire, she will have been nursing for 40 years. By the time she retires, she will have been nursing for 40 years.
  • By 5 pm I will have been writing three reports. By 5 pm I will have written three reports.

How we use it

  • How long an action will have continued up to a future time: By June, I'll have been working here for ten years.
  • How long, up to a future event: When she retires, she'll have been teaching for 35 years.
  • To explain a future result: He'll be tired when he arrives. He'll have been driving all day.

How to make it

PositiveI will have been working. / They'll have been waiting.
NegativeShe won't have been living here long.
QuestionHow long will you have been studying by June?
Short answersYes, I will. / No, I won't.

Words that often go with it: for + a length of time (for ten years, for six hours), by + a date (by next June, by 2030), by the time + present simple, when, all day, how long

Tip: How long? Use will have been + -ing: I'll have been writing for three hours. How many? Use the future perfect: I'll have written three reports. State verbs like know use the future perfect too.
