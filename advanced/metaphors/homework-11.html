Quick reminder

People are often described as animals, and each animal carries a fixed character: foxes are cunning, wolves are dangerous or solitary, sheep follow the crowd, lions are powerful. Most of these images come from fables, farming and the Bible, so they are old and stable.

  • the elephant in the room: an obvious, important problem that everyone avoids mentioning
  • a red herring: something that distracts attention from the real issue, often deliberately
  • a dark horse: a little-known competitor who unexpectedly succeeds, or someone with surprising hidden talents
  • let the cat out of the bag: reveal a secret, usually by accident
  • the lion's share: the largest part of something
  • a sacred cow: a belief, custom or institution treated as beyond criticism
  • crocodile tears: fake sadness or sympathy
  • a wolf in sheep's clothing: a dangerous person who appears harmless

Watch out for

  • She let the dog out of the bag. She let the cat out of the bag. The animal is fixed.
  • He cried tears of crocodile. He shed crocodile tears. Crocodile works as an adjective, before the noun.
  • The boss took the share of the lion. The boss took the lion's share. Use the possessive, not of the.
