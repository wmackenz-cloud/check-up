Quick reminder

Idioms are fixed, but not frozen. Natives bend them for effect: headline writers love puns, such as grate expectations for a story about a cheese shop, and comedians extend a familiar image until it becomes absurd. The trick works only because everyone knows the original.

  • for all intents and purposes: in every practical sense; virtually
  • nip it in the bud: stop something at an early stage
  • a moot point: a question that no longer matters, or one still open to debate
  • I couldn't care less: I don't care at all
  • case in point: an example that proves what you have just said
  • toe the line: obey the rules or official policy, often reluctantly
  • home in on: move steadily towards a target; focus closely on something
  • free rein: complete freedom to act as you choose

Watch out for

  • For all intensive purposes, the deal is done. For all intents and purposes, the deal is done. A classic mishearing, known as an eggcorn.
  • We'll burn that bridge when we come to it. We'll cross that bridge when we come to it. Mixing in burn your bridges is only funny on purpose.
  • Ironically, it rained on the day of our picnic. Unfortunately, it rained on the day of our picnic. Bad luck isn't irony; irony needs a reversal of expectation.
