Quick reminder

English treats time like money: we have a limited amount, we can spend it well or waste it, save it, invest it, borrow it, or run out of it. A meeting can cost us an afternoon; a shortcut can save us an hour.

  • buy time: do something to get more time before a decision or problem arrives
  • on borrowed time: continuing to exist or live longer than expected, but likely to end soon
  • pressed for time: having very little time
  • worth your while: worth the time or effort it takes
  • make up for lost time: do a lot of something quickly because you couldn't do it earlier
  • kill time: do something unimportant while waiting
  • against the clock: trying to finish before a deadline
  • time is money: time is valuable, so don't waste it

Watch out for

  • I passed two hours in the airport to kill the time. I killed time at the airport for two hours. Kill time has no article, and spent fits better than passed.
  • I'm pressing for time today. I'm pressed for time today. It is passive: the time pressure is on you.
  • It's worth of your while to visit. It's worth your while to visit. Worth takes no of.
