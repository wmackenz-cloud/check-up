Quick reminder

In English, a risky decision is often pictured as a bet at a card table. There is money on the table (the stakes), a chance of winning (the odds), cards you can see and cards you hide, and other players who may be bluffing.

  • up the ante: increase the demands, risks or stakes in a situation
  • play your cards right: act cleverly so that you get an advantage
  • keep your cards close to your chest: keep your plans or intentions secret
  • call someone's bluff: challenge someone to prove a threat or claim you think is empty
  • hedge your bets: reduce risk by supporting more than one option
  • an ace up your sleeve: a secret advantage you can use later
  • the odds are stacked against: success is very unlikely because conditions are unfair or difficult
  • hit the jackpot: have a sudden big success or piece of luck

Watch out for

  • They decided to rise the ante. They decided to raise (or up) the ante. Rise has no object; raise and up take one.
  • He kept his cards close to his heart. He kept his cards close to his chest. The heart idiom is 'close to my heart' (dear to me), a different meaning.
  • We caught his bluff and he backed down. We called his bluff. In poker you 'call' a bet; 'catch' sounds like a translation.
