Quick reminder

Ideas and organisations are often plants. They start as seeds, take root, grow, branch out, flourish, bear fruit or wither. This image emphasises time, care and natural development.

  • lay the foundations: create the basic conditions that later success depends on
  • take root: become established and accepted
  • bear fruit: produce good results, usually after time and effort
  • nip it in the bud: stop a problem at an early stage before it grows
  • the cracks are starting to show: problems in something that looked strong are becoming visible
  • a house of cards: a plan or organisation so weak it could collapse at any moment
  • an ivory tower: a privileged place cut off from practical everyday life, especially academia
  • build bridges: create better relations between groups who disagree or distrust each other

Watch out for

  • We must cut the problem in the bud. We must nip the problem in the bud. The verb is fixed; a bud is a young flower.
  • The project finally gave fruit. The project finally bore fruit. English uses bear, not give.
  • She lives in a tower of ivory. She lives in an ivory tower. Fixed word order, and it's a criticism, not a compliment.
