Quick reminder

In English, the mind has eyes. We see what someone means, an explanation is clear or murky, and a clever person is brilliant while a slow one is dim. Lakoff and Johnson listed this pattern as UNDERSTANDING IS SEEING.

  • shed light on: help to explain something that was unclear
  • in the dark: not informed about something
  • a blind spot: an area where someone consistently fails to notice or understand something
  • tunnel vision: focusing on one thing so much that you ignore everything else
  • not see eye to eye: disagree
  • see where you're coming from: understand the reasons for someone's opinion
  • in my mind's eye: in my imagination or memory
  • turn a blind eye: deliberately ignore something you know is wrong

Watch out for

  • We see eye in eye on most things. We see eye to eye on most things. The preposition is to, and the phrase is far more common in the negative.
  • Can you throw light to the problem? Can you shed light on the problem? Shed or throw light on, never to.
  • The managers closed a blind eye to the bullying. The managers turned a blind eye to the bullying. The verb is fixed: turn.
