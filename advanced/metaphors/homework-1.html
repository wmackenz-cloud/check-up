Quick reminder

In Metaphors We Live By (1980), linguist George Lakoff and philosopher Mark Johnson used ARGUMENT IS WAR as their first example of a conceptual metaphor: a whole way of thinking that shows up in ordinary words, not just in poetry.

  • stick to your guns: refuse to change your opinion or plan despite pressure
  • bite the bullet: force yourself to do something unpleasant that can't be avoided
  • dodge a bullet: narrowly escape something bad
  • close ranks: (of a group) unite to protect one another from criticism
  • a double-edged sword: something that has both a clear benefit and a related drawback
  • shoot down (an idea): reject an idea or proposal firmly and quickly
  • bury the hatchet: end a quarrel and become friendly again
  • fight fire with fire: respond to an attack by using the same methods as the attacker

Watch out for

  • We need to bite the bullet of the price increase. We need to bite the bullet and accept the price increase. Bite the bullet takes no object.
  • She stuck to her weapons in the meeting. She stuck to her guns. The noun is fixed: always guns.
  • Let's bury the axe and move on. Let's bury the hatchet. Don't swap in a synonym.
