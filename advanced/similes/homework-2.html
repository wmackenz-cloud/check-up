Quick reminder

With things and situations, as ... as similes mostly work as strong intensifiers. As easy as pie simply means very easy; as old as the hills means extremely old. The image is memorable, but nobody thinks about pies or hills.

  • as light as a feather: extremely light in weight
  • as cold as ice: very cold; also emotionally cold and unfriendly
  • as old as the hills: extremely old; very well known for a long time
  • as clear as day: completely obvious or easy to see
  • as easy as pie: very easy
  • as dead as a doornail: completely dead; also completely finished or failed
  • as right as rain: perfectly healthy or fine again, especially after illness
  • as safe as houses: very safe or secure, with no risk

Watch out for

  • Her hands were as cold as snow. As cold as ice. Snow is understandable but it isn't the fixed simile.
  • Don't worry, the recipe is as easy as cake. As easy as pie, or a piece of cake. Don't mix the two.
  • After a week in bed I feel right like rain. I feel as right as rain (or right as rain). Never like in this simile.
