Which One Did I Say?

Goal: Hear and say the difference between English sounds that are close together. If two words sound the same when you say them, the listener has to guess which one you mean.

Time: 10 minutes a day

Loading…

What this lesson is for

Why this matters: many English words are different by only one sound, like three and tree. If you say both words the same way, the listener has to guess what you mean.

You will know

  • how to make th, s, sh and z with your tongue, lips and throat
  • the difference between long ee (sheep) and short i (ship)

You will understand

  • why a small change in your mouth makes a different word
  • why hearing the difference helps you say it

You will be able to

  • hear which word of a pair someone says
  • say each word so your teacher knows which one you mean
  • read sentences with these sounds clearly

Common mistakes to watch for

  • tree three, tank thank: put your tongue between your teeth. Vietnamese speakers often say t.
  • sink think: tongue out, not behind your teeth. Chinese speakers often say s.
  • day or zay they: tongue between your teeth, and let your throat buzz
  • see she: push your lips forward. Northern Vietnamese speakers often mix s and sh.
  • price prize: buzz on the z at the end
  • leave live, fool full: keep the short sound short and relaxed

Part B: You say, the teacher guesses

Choose one word from each pair and say it. Your teacher will guess which one. If the guess is wrong, try again.

Part C: How to make the sounds

th (three, think, they): put the tip of your tongue lightly between your teeth and blow air out. Use a mirror or your camera to check your tongue is visible.

Long ee (sheep, leave): smile a little and make the sound longer. Short i (ship, live): relax your mouth and make it short.

sh (she, sure): push your lips forward, like you are telling someone to be quiet. s (see, sore): smile and keep your lips flat.

z (prize): the same as s, but your throat buzzes. Put your hand on your throat to feel it.

Part D: Sentences

Read each sentence aloud. Make the bold sounds clear.

  • I think there are three trees in the garden.
  • She can see the ship from here.
  • I want to live here. I don't want to leave.
  • The price of the prize was very high.
  • Thank you for the third time.

Homework

Record yourself reading Part D. Listen to your recording. Which sentence was the most difficult? Say it again slowly, then at normal speed.
