Rhythm and Stress

Goal: Put the stress on the right syllable, make important words strong, and keep small words short.

Time: about 15 minutes. Do it once soon after our lesson, and do Parts 4 and 5 again the day before the next one. Goes with: Lesson 4: Tap the Rhythm and Drill 5: Rhythm and Stress.

What this homework is for

Why this matters: stress helps English listeners know which word you are saying. If the stress is in the wrong place, people may not understand the word, even when every sound is right.

You will know

  • the stress in common words: BUSI-ness, ho-TEL, pho-TO-gra-pher
  • the weak small words: of, to, and, can, are, for

You will understand

  • why the stress can move in a word family: PHO-to-graph, pho-TO-gra-pher
  • why can is weak but can't is strong

You will be able to

  • hear and clap the strong syllable
  • catch the small weak words in a sentence
  • talk about your plans with a natural beat

Common mistakes to watch for

  • busi-NESS BUSI-ness, de-ve-LOP de-VE-lop: the stress is often not on the last syllable
  • PHO-to-gra-pher pho-TO-gra-pher: the stress moves when the word gets longer
  • a cup OF tea a cuppa tea: make of weak
  • She CAN swim. She kn SWIM. Can is short in a positive sentence.
  • ba-na-na (every syllable the same) ba-NA-na: make the strong syllable longer and louder. Chinese speakers often just make it higher.

Part 2: Which stress pattern?
