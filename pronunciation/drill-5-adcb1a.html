Rhythm and Stress

Goal: Make important words strong and small words short, so your English has a natural beat.

Time: 5 to 10 minutes a day. Goes with: Lesson 4: Tap the Rhythm.

Loading…

What this drill is for

Why this matters: English listeners find the meaning in the strong syllables. If every syllable is the same, or the stress is in the wrong place, a word can be hard to understand, even when every sound is right.

You will know

  • the weak small words: to = "tuh", of = "uh", and = "n"
  • the stress in common words: BUSiness, hoTEL, imPORtant

You will understand

  • why a line with more small words still takes about the same time
  • why stress means longer and louder, not only higher

You will be able to

  • keep a steady beat and fit the small words in between
  • stress the right syllable in common long words
  • stress the word that corrects a mistake

Common mistakes to watch for

  • busi-NESS BUSI-ness: many students stress the last syllable. Check the stress of each new word.
  • I WANT TOO GO I WANT-tuh GO, a CUP OF TEA a CUP-uh TEA: make small words short
  • com-pu-ter (every syllable the same) comPUter: make the strong syllable longer and louder. Chinese speakers often just make it higher.
  • No, it's a blue CAR. No, it's a BLUE car. Stress the word that corrects the mistake.

Drill A: Same beat, more words

Tap the table on each word in CAPITALS. Keep the same speed of tapping for every line, so the small words get squeezed in between.

CATS   EAT   FISH.

The CATS   EAT the   FISH.

The CATS will EAT the FISH.

The CATS have been EATing the FISH.

Each line should take almost the same time.

Drill D: Correct me

Stress the word that corrects the mistake.

  • Is it a red car? No, it's a BLUE car.
  • Did you go on Monday? No, I went on TUESday.
  • Is she a teacher? No, she's a DOCtor.
  • Do you live in Hanoi? No, I live in HO CHI MINH City.
  • Was it two hundred? No, it was THREE hundred.
