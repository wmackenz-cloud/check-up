Join the Words

Goal: Connect words smoothly, the way English speakers do. Joining words also helps you say the end sounds you practised in Assignment 2.

Time: 10 minutes a day

Loading…

What this lesson is for

Why this matters: English speakers join words, so a sentence sounds like one smooth line. Joining also makes your end sounds easier to say and easier for the listener to hear.

You will know

  • the main rule: a consonant at the end + a vowel at the start = move the consonant
  • what to do when the same sound comes twice: bus stop

You will understand

  • why joining brings back the endings: worked in sounds like work-tin
  • why a stop after every word sounds choppy

You will be able to

  • join words in phrases and full sentences
  • find joined phrases in your own speaking

Common mistakes to watch for

  • worked · in (a stop between the words) work-tin: move the t to the next word
  • work in worked in: Vietnamese speakers often drop the -ed. Join it to the next word and it comes back.
  • lived-uh in liv-din: no extra vowel. Chinese speakers often add one; the d moves to the next word instead.
  • an · apple a-napple: the n joins the next word
  • bus · stop (two s sounds) bu-sstop: say the s once, a little longer

The main rule

When a word ends with a consonant and the next word starts with a vowel, move the consonant to the next word.

worked in sounds like work-tin

pick it up sounds like pi-ki-tup

This makes the end sound easier, because it becomes the start of the next sound.

Part B: Same sound twice

When one word ends with the same sound the next word starts with, say it once but a little longer.

  • bus stop: bu-sstop
  • big game: bi-ggame
  • good day: goo-dday
  • this sentence: thi-ssentence

Part C: Sentences

First read each sentence word by word. Then read it again, joining the words.

  • I worked in an office for a year.
  • Can I have an orange, please?
  • She lived in a small town.
  • Let's meet at eight o'clock.
  • First of all, I'd like to talk about it.
