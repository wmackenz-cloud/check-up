Joining Words

Goal: Join words smoothly, the way English speakers do, so your endings come back and your English flows.

Time: about 15 minutes. Do it once soon after our lesson, and do Parts 4 and 5 again the day before the next one. Goes with: Lesson 5: Join the Words and Drill 4: Joining Words.

What this homework is for

Why this matters: when you join words, your English sounds smooth and your endings come back, so people hear what you mean more easily.

You will know

  • three kinds of join: move the consonant, a tiny w, a tiny y
  • a tiny w comes after o and oo; a tiny y after ee and I

You will understand

  • why joining helps you say -ed: worked in sounds like work-tin
  • why there should be no gap between joined words

You will be able to

  • hear how two words join
  • catch joined phrases in a sentence and write them
  • talk about your morning with the words joined

Common mistakes to watch for

  • pick · it · up (a stop between each word) pi-ki-tup: move each last sound to the next word
  • ask about it as-ka-bou-tit: keep the t of asked and join it to about
  • go · out go-wout, she · asked she-yasked: a tiny w or y, not a stop
  • come-uh in co-min: no extra vowel. Chinese speakers often add one.
  • thuh end thee-yend: before a vowel, the sounds like "thee"
