s, sh, z, v and w

Goal: Hear and say s, sh and z clearly, and keep v and w apart.

Time: about 15 minutes. Do it once soon after our lesson, and do Parts 4 and 5 again the day before the next one. Goes with: Lesson 3: Which One Did I Say? and Drill 3: s, sh and z.

What this homework is for

Why this matters: see and she, vest and west are different words. When these sounds are clear, your listener does not have to guess which word you mean.

You will know

  • the lip shapes for s, sh, v and w
  • spelling tricks: the s in sure sounds like sh, the c in city like s, the s in music like z

You will understand

  • why spelling does not always show the sound
  • why v uses your teeth and w does not

You will be able to

  • hear the difference between s and sh, s and z, v and w
  • say sentences with all five sounds
  • describe a shop or market you like

Common mistakes to watch for

  • see she, sort short: round your lips for sh. Northern Vietnamese speakers often mix s and sh.
  • bus buzz, ice eyes: let your throat buzz for z. Chinese speakers often say s.
  • wery very, west vest: top teeth on your bottom lip for v. Chinese speakers often say w.
  • vine wine, vet wet: round lips and no teeth for w
