The rule

Ask yourself: is the important thing the person who did the action, or the thing the action happened to?

Active: the subject does the action. Someone stole my bike.

Passive: the action happens to the subject. My bike was stolen.

Use the passive when we don't know who did it, it isn't important who did it, or it's obvious.

Form: be + past participle. Only be changes for the tense. The past participle never changes.

To say who did it, add by: The book was written by Agatha Christie.

The tense is in "be"

Tense be Example
Present simple am / is / are English is spoken here.
Present continuous am / is / are being The road is being repaired.
Past simple was / were The house was built in 1900.
Present perfect has / have been My flight has been cancelled.
Future will be The results will be sent by email.
Modal can / must / should be This form must be signed.

Have something done

When someone else does a job for you: have + thing + past participle.

I had my hair cut yesterday. (The hairdresser cut it.) We're having our kitchen painted.

Watch out

  • The letter was send yesterday. The letter was sent yesterday.
  • The house built in 1990. The house was built in 1990.
  • English is speaking here. English is spoken here.
  • I cut my hair at the hairdresser's. I had my hair cut at the hairdresser's.
