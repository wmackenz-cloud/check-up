Teacher Notes 3: Articles (a, an, the, or nothing)

Open the student worksheet

Why students get this wrong

Two different problems. Speakers of languages with no articles (Russian, Polish, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Arabic in part) drop them: She is teacher. Speakers of languages that use the definite article for general ideas (French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, German, Greek) add the where English has none: The life is short, I love the music. Know which problem your student has, because the fix is different.

How to explain it

Say: "Articles answer one question for the listener: do you know which one I mean? If you know which one, I say the. If you don't know yet, and it's one countable thing, I say a or an. If I'm talking about things in general, I say nothing."

Test questions (in this order): 1. Does my listener know exactly which one? 2. If not, is it one countable thing? 3. Is it general, plural or uncountable?

Demonstrate: "I bought a book yesterday." (You don't know which.) "The book is about Egypt." (Now you know which: the one I just mentioned.)

For the general vs specific problem: "Dogs are loyal means all dogs, everywhere. The dogs next door means those particular dogs. English uses no article for 'everything of this kind'."

If the student asks...

  • "Why a university, not an university?" The rule is about sound, not spelling. University starts with a "you" sound, which is a consonant sound. Hour starts with a vowel sound because the h is silent.
  • "Why the UK but not the Canada?" Country names that are a plural or include a word like Kingdom, States or Republic take the: the UK, the USA, the Netherlands, the Czech Republic. Ordinary single names don't.
  • "Why go to bed, not go to the bed?" With bed, school, work, hospital, prison and church, no article means you're there for the place's main purpose: sleeping, studying, working, being a patient. The means the physical place: I sat on the bed. I went to the school to meet the teacher.
  • "Why the piano but play football?" It's a convention: instruments take the, sports don't. There's no deeper logic; it has to be memorised.

Exercise notes and answers with reasons

Exercise 1: a or an

Have the student say each word aloud before choosing. The ear decides, not the eye.

  1. an apple: vowel sound.
  2. a university: "you" sound.
  3. an hour: silent h.
  4. an honest: silent h.
  5. a uniform: "you" sound.
  6. an interesting: vowel sound.
  7. a one-way: "won" sound, a w consonant.
  8. an MP3: the letter M is said "em", a vowel sound.

Exercise 2: a, an, the, or nothing

  1. an architect: jobs take a / an.
  2. the salt: both people know which salt (the one on the table).
  3. X cold weather: general.
  4. a hotel / The hotel: first mention, then known.
  5. the violin: instruments.
  6. X golf: sports.
  7. the highest / the world: superlative; unique.
  8. X lunch: meals.
  9. the Netherlands: plural name.
  10. X Italy: ordinary country name.
  11. X Money: general and uncountable.
  12. a bank: any bank, not a specific one.
  13. the Amazon: rivers.
  14. X bed: for its purpose (sleeping).

Exercise 3: General or specific

The pairs are deliberate: 1 and 2, 3 and 4. Ask "All children everywhere, or particular ones?"

  1. Children: all children.
  2. The children: specific ones, in my class.
  3. music: all music.
  4. The music: specific, in this restaurant.
  5. Life: in general.
  6. The water: specific, in this lake.

Exercise 4: Find and correct the mistake

  1. a very good teacher: jobs and descriptions of one person need a / an.
  2. to work: for its purpose.
  3. Correct: unique.
  4. Correct: superlative.
  5. some information: uncountable, so no a (links to Trap 4).
  6. had dinner: meals.
  7. Dogs ... cats: general.
  8. Correct: instruments.

Exercise 5: The story

  1. a trip: first mention.
  2. X Portugal: country.
  3. an old house: first mention, vowel sound.
  4. the sea: unique in context.
  5. The house: second mention.
  6. a beautiful garden: first mention.
  7. X breakfast: meal.
  8. a good cook: describing a person.
  9. X restaurants: plural, not specific.
  10. The best: superlative.
  11. a small village: first mention.
  12. The people: specific, there.
  13. a wonderful holiday: describing one thing.

Exercises 6 and 7: About you and speaking

Have the student write first, then underline every noun and check it against the three questions. Articles improve fastest through self-checking written work, not through speaking correction alone.
