Part 1: Vowels and consonants

English has 26 letters. Five of them are vowels: A, E, I, O, U. All the other letters are consonants.

Part 2: A or an? Listen, don't look

Use an before a vowel sound. Use a before a consonant sound.

an apple, an egg, an idea · a book, a car, a house

Try this: say "a apple" out loud. Your voice stops between the two vowel sounds. Now say "an apple". The n joins the words, so they flow. That is why an exists.

The important part: the rule is about the sound at the start of the word, not the letter. Most of the time they match. Sometimes they don't:

WordFirst letterFirst soundArticle
universityu (vowel)"you" (consonant sound)a university
Europeane (vowel)"you" (consonant sound)a European city
oneo (vowel)"w" (consonant sound)a one-way ticket
hourh (consonant)"ow" (vowel sound, the h is silent)an hour
honesth (consonant)"o" (vowel sound, the h is silent)an honest person
MBAM (consonant)"em" (vowel sound)an MBA

Tip: the article goes with the next word, even if it's an adjective: an apple but a red apple; a car but an old car.

Part 3: Review: countable and uncountable nouns

A and an mean "one". So you can only use them with one thing you can count.

Ask three questions:

  1. Can you count it? water, advice, information, money, furniture, homework, news are uncountable. No a or an. I need advice. or I need some advice.
  2. Is it one? Plurals take no a or an. He bought books. or He bought some books.
  3. Does it start with a vowel sound? Yes: an. No: a. an umbrella, a glass of water

Don't drop it! With one countable thing, English needs a word in front: I have phone. I have a phone.

Exercise 6: Speaking: say it fast

Drill 1: A or an, quickly. Your teacher says a word. You say it with a or an, as fast as you can. apple · hour · uniform · egg · house · honest man · umbrella · useful app · email · one-euro coin

Drill 2: Add an adjective. Your teacher says a phrase and an adjective. Change the article if you need to. an apple + red → a red apple · a car + old → an old car · an idea + good → a good idea

Drill 3: What's in the room? Look around you. Name six things in one minute: "There's a lamp, an old chair, some books…"
