Present Perfect or Past Simple?

The rule

Ask yourself: is the time finished, or does it continue to now?

Past simple (worked, went, saw): the time is finished. We know when, or we can say when.

I visited Rome in 2019. She called me yesterday.

Present perfect (have/has + past participle): the time connects to now. We don't say exactly when.

I have visited Rome three times. She has just called me.

Never use the present perfect with a finished time: I have seen him yesterday.

Key words

Past simple Present perfect
yesterday, last night, last week, in 2015, two days ago, when I was a child, What time...?, When...? ever, never, already, yet, just, so far, recently, since..., for... (to now), this week (not finished)

For or since?

for + a period of time: for two years, for a long time, for ten minutes

since + a starting point: since 2020, since Monday, since I was young

Watch out

  • I live here since 2019. I have lived here since 2019.
  • When have you arrived? When did you arrive?
  • I have seen her two days ago. I saw her two days ago.
  • Did you ever eat sushi? Have you ever eaten sushi?

Already, yet, still, ago or before?

already = sooner than expected. It usually goes before the past participle: I've already eaten.

yet = up to now. Use it in questions and negatives, at the end: Have you finished yet? I haven't finished yet.

still = it hasn't stopped. It goes before the main verb or after be: She still lives here. I'm still waiting.

ago = back from now, with the past simple: We met three years ago.   before = at any time before then, at the end: I've never been here before. I had never been to Canada before.
