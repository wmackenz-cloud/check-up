Teacher Notes 5: Gerund (-ing) or Infinitive (to + verb)?

Open the student worksheet

Why students get this wrong

Most languages use the infinitive after almost every verb (French j'aime nager, Spanish disfruto nadar), so learners produce I enjoy to swim. The look forward to + -ing pattern feels wrong to learners because they've been taught that to is always followed by the base verb.

How to explain it

Start with the one rule that has no exceptions. Say: "After a preposition, the verb always takes -ing. Always. In, on, at, for, about, without, before, after. Thanks for coming. I'm good at cooking."

Then the lists. Say: "For other verbs, English doesn't follow logic perfectly, so we learn two lists. But here's a helpful pattern: to often points forward, like an arrow, to something not done yet. I want to go. I plan to study. I hope to see you. The -ing form is often about the activity itself, as a thing: I enjoy swimming. I finished cooking. I avoid driving."

Test questions: Is there a preposition before it? Then -ing. If not, which list is the verb on?

For stop / remember / forget / try, use the arrow idea: with to, the action comes after (remember, then do it). With -ing, the action came before (you did it, then you remember it).

If the student asks...

  • "Why look forward to hearing? To + verb is the infinitive!" Here to isn't part of the infinitive. It's a preposition, like at or in. Test: can you put a noun after it? I look forward to the holiday. Yes, so to is a preposition, and prepositions take -ing. Same with be used to, object to, get used to.
  • "Can I say I like swimming and I like to swim?" Yes. Like, love, hate, start, begin and prefer take both with almost no change in meaning. But would like always takes to: I'd like to swim.
  • "Why suggested going and not suggested to go?" Suggest is on the -ing list. You can also say She suggested that we go. But never suggested to go or suggested me to go.
  • "Is there a trick to memorise the -ing verbs?" Many -ing verbs are about feelings toward an activity (enjoy, mind, can't stand, miss) or about stopping or avoiding it (finish, avoid, give up, quit). It's not perfect, but it helps.

Exercise notes and answers with reasons

Exercise 1: Sort the verbs

+ -ing: enjoy, finish, avoid, mind, keep, practise, suggest, miss.

+ to: want, decide, hope, promise, refuse, manage, afford, agree.

Ask the student which ones point forward to a future action: decide, hope, promise, agree, want. That's the arrow pattern in action.

Exercise 2: -ing or to + verb

  1. reading: enjoy + -ing.
  2. to learn: want + to.
  3. cleaning: finish + -ing.
  4. to take: decide + to (a future choice).
  5. forgetting: keep + -ing (keep = continue, repeat).
  6. to go: afford + to.
  7. closing: mind + -ing. Note: Would you mind closing...? is a very polite request.
  8. to be: promise + to (future).
  9. driving: avoid + -ing.
  10. to help: refuse + to.
  11. moving: consider + -ing.
  12. to finish: manage + to (succeed in doing something difficult).

Exercise 3: After a preposition

Every answer is -ing. Ask the student to circle the preposition in each sentence first.

  1. cooking: at.
  2. saying: without.
  3. inviting: for.
  4. waiting: of.
  5. finishing: after.
  6. buying: about.
  7. seeing: to (a preposition here; use the noun test).
  8. flying: of.

Exercise 4: Same verb, different meaning

For each item, ask: "Which came first, the remembering or the action?"

  1. to buy: remember first, then buy (a task).
  2. visiting: visited first, remember now (a memory).
  3. eating: he doesn't eat meat any more.
  4. to have: we stopped (the car) in order to have coffee.
  5. drinking: an experiment; a suggestion to solve a problem.
  6. to call: an effort that didn't succeed.
  7. to send: the task wasn't done.
  8. seeing: a memory.

Exercise 5: Find and correct the mistake

  1. to meeting: look forward to + -ing.
  2. suggested taking: suggest + -ing.
  3. Correct: hope + to.
  4. gave up smoking: give up + -ing (up is a particle; the phrasal verb means stop).
  5. Correct: don't mind + -ing.
  6. agreed to help: agree + to.
  7. interested in studying: preposition + -ing.
  8. Correct: want + to.

Exercises 6 and 7: About you and speaking

Correct every gerund or infinitive error immediately in this section. These patterns are learned by ear, so the student needs to hear the correct version many times. Have them repeat the corrected sentence aloud.
