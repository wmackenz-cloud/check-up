The rule

Ask yourself: can I put a number in front of it?

Countable nouns have a singular and plural: one chair, three chairs.

Use: many, a few, few, fewer, a / an, How many?

Uncountable nouns have no plural and no a / an: water, money, advice. They take a singular verb.

Use: much, a little, little, less, How much?

Both: some, any, a lot of, lots of, no, enough.

a few / a little = some, enough (positive). few / little = almost none, not enough (negative).

Tricky uncountable nouns

These are countable in many languages, but uncountable in English. Never add -s, never use a / an.

Uncountable To count it, say...
advice a piece of advice, some advice
information a piece of information
news a piece of news (The news is good.)
furniture a piece of furniture, a chair, a table
luggage / baggage a suitcase, a bag
homework, work, research an exercise, a job, a task, a study
equipment a piece of equipment, a tool
accommodation a room, a flat, a place to stay
bread, toast a loaf of bread, a slice of toast

Watch out

  • Can you give me an advice? Can you give me some advice?
  • I have many homeworks. I have a lot of homework.
  • The news are bad. The news is bad.
  • There were less people this year. There were fewer people this year.
  • How much people came? How many people came?
