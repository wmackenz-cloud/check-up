Teacher Notes 6: Conditionals and Wish

Open the student worksheet

This worksheet has nine exercises. For most students, split it over two lessons: first and second conditionals in lesson one (Exercises 1 to 3 and 5), third conditional and wish in lesson two (Exercises 4, 6 and 7). Exercises 8 and 9 work at the end of either.

Why students get this wrong

Many languages use a future after if (If it will rain) or a conditional in both halves (If I would have money, I would buy it, common from German and Dutch speakers). Learners also don't connect the tense with how real the situation is, so they choose forms by memorised labels (first, second, third) and mix them up.

How to explain it

Say: "Conditionals are about how real something is. The less real, the further back the tense goes. Real and possible? Present. Imaginary now? Go back one step, to the past. Imaginary in the past? Go back again, to the past perfect. Going back in time is how English says 'this isn't real'."

Draw it: three steps going down. Top step: "real" (present). Middle step: "imaginary now" (past). Bottom step: "imaginary past" (past perfect).

Test question: Is this real, imaginary now, or imaginary in the past?

The golden rule. Say: "Will and would never go in the if half. They live in the result half."

Contrast pair: "If I see him, I'll tell him" (I might see him). "If I saw him, I'd tell him" (I probably won't). Ask: "Which person thinks they'll see him?"

If the student asks...

  • "Why If I were, not If I was?" Were for all persons is the traditional form for imaginary situations. Was is very common in speech and is accepted, but If I were you is a fixed phrase for advice. Use were in exam writing.
  • "Why is it past tense if it's about now?" The past tense here doesn't mean past time. It means distance from reality. That's the key idea; repeat it.
  • "What's a mixed conditional?" (B2) A past cause with a present result: If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now. Mix the halves when the time of the condition and the time of the result are different.
  • "Can I say If I'd known, I'd have come? What does 'd mean?" In the if half, 'd = had. In the result half, 'd = would. Context tells you which.

Exercise notes and answers with reasons

Exercise 1: First conditional (real, possible)

  1. is / will go
  2. will call / hear: the if half can come second; still no will in it.
  3. don't hurry / will miss
  4. passes / will go: third person -s in the if half is often forgotten.
  5. won't go / rains
  6. see / will tell

Exercise 2: Second conditional (imaginary now)

  1. had / would learn: I don't have time.
  2. lived / would swim: she doesn't live near the sea.
  3. would you do / found: imaginary situation.
  4. were / would take: advice phrase.
  5. would be / didn't smoke: he does smoke.
  6. didn't have / would travel: we do have children.

Exercise 3: First or second

Ask: "Is this likely or unlikely?" The result half gives the clue (will or would).

  1. see: result has will, so it's first conditional.
  2. were: imaginary; the result has would.
  3. heat: zero conditional, a scientific fact.
  4. won: unlikely; the result has would.
  5. snows: real possibility; will.
  6. could: dogs can't talk, so imaginary.

Exercise 4: Third conditional (imaginary past)

For each item, ask "What really happened?" to make the meaning clear first. Item 1: I didn't know, so I didn't come.

  1. had known / would have come
  2. hadn't missed / wouldn't have been
  3. would have won / had played
  4. had told / would have helped
  5. had left / wouldn't have got (US: gotten)
  6. wouldn't have met / hadn't gone: a nice personal example to model with your own story.

Exercise 5: Unless

Say: "Unless means if not. So after unless, the verb is positive." The most common error is a double negative: unless you don't come.

  1. I won't go unless you come with me.
  2. You'll be late unless you leave now.
  3. She won't get the job unless she improves her English.
  4. Unless you study, you won't pass.

Exercise 6: Wish

Say: "Wish works like the second and third conditionals. It's about things that aren't real, so the tense goes back."

  1. I wish I had a garden: now.
  2. I wish I could drive: ability now.
  3. I wish I lived closer to my family: now.
  4. I wish I had gone to the party: past regret.
  5. I wish I hadn't eaten so much: past regret.
  6. I wish I spoke or could speak Italian: now.

Exercise 7: Find and correct the mistake

  1. If it is cold: no will after if.
  2. Correct: second conditional.
  3. If I knew the answer: no would after if.
  4. would have passed: last week is past, so third conditional.
  5. I wish I could stay: wish + past.
  6. Correct: unless + positive verb.

Exercises 8 and 9: About you and speaking

The third speaking question (an important moment in your life) often produces the best language and real engagement. Model your own example first, for instance: "If I hadn't become a teacher, I would never have..."
