The explain-it method

Use this for any grammar question a student asks, including ones not in these lessons.

  1. Meaning first, form second. Ask what the speaker wants to say before naming the tense or structure. Most grammar choices are meaning choices.
  2. One test question. Give the student a question they can ask themselves, such as Is the time finished? A test question travels into the exam; a rule list doesn't.
  3. Contrast pair. Show two sentences that differ by one word and ask how the meaning changes.
  4. Rehearse aloud. Drill until the correct version sounds right to the student's ear.

If a student asks something you can't explain on the spot, it's fine to say: "Good question. Let me give you a clear answer next lesson." Then bring a contrast pair and a test question.
