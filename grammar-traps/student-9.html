The rule

Relative clauses give more information about a noun. They join two sentences into one.

I have a friend. She speaks five languages. → I have a friend who speaks five languages.

Step 1: choose the word by looking at the noun.

person → who · thing or animal → which · place → where · possession (his, her, its, their) → whose · time → when

Step 2: is the information necessary, or extra?

Necessary (tells us which one): no commas. You can use that instead of who or which. The man who / that lives next door is a doctor.

Extra (a bonus fact): use commas, and never use that. My father, who is 80, still plays tennis.

Leaving out the relative word

In necessary clauses, you can leave out who / which / that when it is the object (when another subject follows it).

The book (that) I'm reading is great. (I = subject, so you can leave out that.)

The book that won the prize is great. (No other subject, so you must keep that.)

Watch out

  • The man which I met... The man who I met...
  • Toronto, that is in Canada, is a big city. Toronto, which is in Canada, is a big city.
  • This is the hotel where we stayed in it. This is the hotel where we stayed. (Don't repeat it.)
  • That's the woman who her son is a pilot. That's the woman whose son is a pilot.

Exercise 5: Find and correct the mistake

Some sentences are correct. Tick them.

  4. I have a friend which lives in Australia.
  5. This is the shop where I bought my coat.
  6. He's the man who his house burned down.
