Must, Might, Can't: How Sure Are You?

Name: Date:

Type your answers in the boxes. They stay on this device.

The rule

Ask yourself: how sure am I?

must = I'm almost sure it's true. The lights are on. They must be home.

might / may / could = it's possible. He might be at the gym. I'm not sure.

can't = I'm almost sure it's NOT true. She can't be 50. She looks 30!

Important: the opposite of must here is can't, not mustn't. Mustn't means "it's not allowed".

For the past: modal + have + past participle. She must have left. He can't have seen us. They might have forgotten.

The certainty scale

How sure? Now Past
Almost sure: YES must be must have been / must have done
Possible might / may / could be might / may / could have done
Almost sure: NO can't be can't have been / can't have done

Watch out

  • He mustn't be at home; his car isn't here. He can't be at home.
  • She must left early. She must have left early.
  • They can't have went. They can't have gone.
  • It may rained last night. It may have rained last night.
