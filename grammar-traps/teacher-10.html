Teacher Notes 10: Reported Speech

Open the student worksheet

Why students get this wrong

Many languages don't move the tense back when reporting (Russian, Chinese, Japanese), so learners say She said she is tired, which is sometimes fine but often marked wrong in exams. The biggest error, though, is word order in reported questions: learners keep the question form (He asked me where did I work), because in their language the question word order stays. Say vs tell is also a constant slip.

How to explain it

Say: "When you report what someone said, their words belong to the past now. So the tense moves one step back into the past. 'I am tired' becomes she said she was tired. Then you change the people and the time words, because you're telling it from a new place and time."

On questions, say: "A reported question isn't a real question any more. It's a statement about a question. So it uses normal statement order: subject, then verb. And no question mark."

Demonstrate: write Where do you live? Cross out do. Move you before the verb. Change to past: She asked where I lived.

Test questions: Did I move the tense back? Did I change the people and time words? If it was a question, is it in normal order?

Say vs tell. Say: "Tell always needs a person after it: tell me, tell him. Say doesn't: she said that... If you want a person after say, you need to: she said to me."

If the student asks...

  • "Do I always have to change the tense?" No. If the statement is still true now, you can keep the present: He said he lives in Toronto (he still does). But in exams, backshift is the safe, expected choice, especially in key word transformations.
  • "What if the reporting verb is present?" If you use says (present), no backshift: She says she's tired. Backshift only happens after a past reporting verb.
  • "Why if, not that, for yes/no questions?" That introduces a fact. If or whether introduces an unknown, a yes-or-no. He asked if I was ready means he didn't know.
  • "What about could, would, should, might?" These don't change. "I could help" → She said she could help.

Exercise notes and answers with reasons

Exercise 1: say or tell

  1. told: a person (me) follows.
  2. said: no person.
  3. tell: tell someone the time is a fixed phrase.
  4. said: say goodbye is fixed.
  5. told: tell the truth is fixed, and a person follows.
  6. say: say to you; the person comes after to.

Fixed tell phrases worth teaching: tell the truth, tell a lie, tell a story, tell a joke, tell the time.

Exercise 2: Statements

  1. (that) she was hungry: am → was.
  2. (that) they lived in Toronto: live → lived (or live, if still true).
  3. (that) he was watching TV: is watching → was watching.
  4. (that) she had bought a new car: bought → had bought.
  5. (that) he had lost his keys: have lost → had lost; my → his.
  6. (that) he would call me the next day: will → would; you → me; tomorrow → the next day.
  7. (that) she couldn't come that night: can't → couldn't; tonight → that night.
  8. (that) he didn't like fish: don't → didn't; I → he.

Exercise 3: Questions

For each item, ask: "Is it a wh- question or a yes/no question?" Yes/no questions need if or whether.

  1. where I worked: wh- question, no did.
  2. what time it was: subject before verb.
  3. if / whether I liked jazz: yes/no.
  4. if / whether I was married: yes/no.
  5. why I was late: you → I.
  6. if / whether we had been to Canada: yes/no, present perfect → past perfect.
  7. how much it cost: no does.
  8. if / whether he could help her: yes/no, can → could, me → her.

Exercise 4: Orders and requests

Say: "For orders and requests, don't use that. Use the verb + person + to + verb. For negative orders, not to."

  1. She told me to close the window.
  2. Mum told the children not to touch that.
  3. The nurse asked us to wait there: here → there.
  4. The doctor advised him to rest.
  5. He reminded me not to forget my passport. (Also natural: to take my passport.)

Exercise 5: Find and correct the mistake

  1. She told me or She said: say doesn't take a person directly.
  2. Correct
  3. what time the film started: statement order, past tense.
  4. Correct
  5. if I was ready: statement order.
  6. Correct

Exercises 6 and 7: Real conversation and speaking

The five-question game in Exercise 7 is the fastest way to fix question word order. Ask quickly, have the student report immediately, and correct every inversion. Repeat with new questions until it's automatic. Reverse roles: the student asks, you report, and they check you.

B2 extension: reporting verbs

For B2 Cambridge students, add these reporting verbs: admit, deny, suggest, apologise for, offer, refuse. They appear often in key word transformations. The full drill is in your Trap 10 lesson in Google Drive (Drill B).
