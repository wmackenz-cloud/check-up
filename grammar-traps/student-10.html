What this lesson is for

Why this matters: you often need to tell someone what another person said or asked. Reported speech changes the tense, the people and the word order, so it helps to practise the pattern.

You will know

  • the tense moves one step back: am becomes was
  • time words change: tomorrow becomes the next day
  • tell needs a person, say doesn't

You will understand

  • why reported questions use normal word order and no question mark
  • when to use if or whether

You will be able to

  • report statements, questions, orders and advice
  • tell someone about a conversation, a call or a message

Common mistakes to watch for

  • She said me she was tired. She told me she was tired. / She said she was tired.
  • He asked me where did I work. He asked me where I worked.
  • She asked me do I like coffee. She asked me if I liked coffee.
  • He told me don't worry. He told me not to worry.

The rule

When you report what someone said, follow three steps:

1. Move the tense one step back. "I am tired." → She said she was tired.

2. Change the people and the time words to match the new situation. "I'll see you tomorrow." → He said he would see me the next day.

3. Questions: use normal sentence word order and no question mark. "Where do you live?" → She asked where I lived.

For yes/no questions, use if or whether. "Are you ready?" → He asked if I was ready.

say or tell? tell needs a person: She told me that... say doesn't: She said that...

Tense changes

Direct speech Reported speech
present simple: "I like it." past simple: She said she liked it.
present continuous: "I'm working." past continuous: He said he was working.
past simple: "I saw him." past perfect: She said she had seen him.
present perfect: "I've finished." past perfect: He said he had finished.
will: "I'll help." would: She said she would help.
can: "I can swim." could: He said he could swim.

Word changes

Direct Reported
today / tonight that day / that night
tomorrow the next day / the following day
yesterday the day before / the previous day
next week the following week
here / this there / that

Reporting orders, requests and advice

tell / ask / advise + person + to + verb: "Sit down." → She told me to sit down. "Don't be late." → He told us not to be late.
