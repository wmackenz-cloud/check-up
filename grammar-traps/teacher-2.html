Teacher Notes 2: Present Simple or Present Continuous?

Open the student worksheet

Why students get this wrong

Most languages use one present tense for both meanings (French je travaille, Swedish jag arbetar, Spanish trabajo). Learners then overuse the continuous once they learn it (I am working in a bank), or apply it to state verbs (I am knowing). Speakers of some Asian and Indian varieties of English also use stative verbs in the continuous (I am having two brothers), which exams mark as wrong.

How to explain it

Say: "The simple is for what's always or usually true: your life, your habits, facts. The continuous is for what's happening now, or just for a while. It's like a photo of this moment, compared with a description of your normal life."

On state verbs, say: "The continuous means you're in the middle of an action. Some verbs aren't actions, they're states: knowing, liking, owning. You can't be in the middle of knowing something. You either know it or you don't."

Test question: Is it always or usually true, or is it happening now or for a short time? And is the verb an action or a state?

Contrast pair: "I work in a bank" (my job). "I'm working from home this week" (temporary). Ask: "Which one will change soon?"

If the student asks...

  • "But McDonald's says I'm lovin' it!" Great question. Advertisers break the rule on purpose to sound energetic and informal. It means "I'm enjoying it right now". In an exam, use I love it.
  • "Why is We're having dinner correct?" Have is only a state verb when it means own or possess. Have dinner, have a shower, have a good time are actions, so the continuous is fine.
  • "Why He's always forgetting his keys?" Always + continuous expresses annoyance about a repeated habit. It's correct but emotional. Keep it for B2 students.
  • "Can I say I'm thinking?" Yes, when think means the activity of using your mind (I'm thinking about my holiday). No, when it means opinion (I think you're right).

Exercise notes and answers with reasons

Exercise 1: Habit or now

  1. N: happening at this moment.
  2. H: her permanent job.
  3. N: temporary, "until our house is ready". Point out that temporary counts as "now" even if it lasts months.
  4. H: never describes a habit.
  5. N: happening now.
  6. H: every morning.

Exercise 2: Choose the correct form

  1. walk: every day = habit.
  2. is sleeping: right now (Be quiet!).
  3. doesn't like: like is a state verb.
  4. is living: temporary, until he finds a job.
  5. rises: a fact.
  6. do you do: "What do you do?" asks about your job.
  7. are you doing: "What are you doing?" asks about this moment. Items 6 and 7 are a useful pair to say aloud.
  8. need: state verb.
  9. belongs: possession, a state.
  10. are going up: a changing situation, this year.
  11. have: usually = habit.
  12. don't remember: remember is a mental state.

Exercise 3: Complete with the verb

  1. is snowing: "Look!" signals now.
  2. works: his permanent job.
  3. don't understand / does ... mean: both are states.
  4. are you wearing: now, and surprising.
  5. do you go: how often asks about habits.
  6. am driving: at this moment.
  7. wants: state verb.
  8. are building: in progress this year.
  9. tastes: taste as a quality is a state.
  10. am reading: in progress, at the moment (even if not this second).

Exercise 4: Find and correct the mistake

  1. I know the answer: state verb.
  2. Correct: temporary.
  3. He owns two houses: possession is a state.
  4. Correct: present continuous for a future arrangement. Mention this use briefly if the student asks.
  5. Are you listening to me?: happening now.
  6. I prefer tea to coffee: state verb.
  7. Correct: a changing trend.
  8. She always goes to bed at ten: a neutral habit. (She's always going... is possible only as a complaint.)

Exercise 5: State or action

  1. think: opinion.
  2. am thinking: mental activity.
  3. are having: have a good time is an experience, an action.
  4. have: possession.
  5. smells: a quality.
  6. is tasting: the chef is doing the action of tasting.

Exercises 6 and 7: About you and speaking

The first speaking task (normal day, then this week) is the most useful. It forces the student to switch tenses on the same topic. For the photo task, insist on the continuous: examiners want "The man is holding...", not "The man holds...".
