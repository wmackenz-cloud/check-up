The rule

Rule 1 (always true): after a preposition, use -ing.

Prepositions: in, on, at, of, for, about, without, before, after, by.

I'm interested in learning Spanish. Thanks for helping me. Before leaving, lock the door.

Rule 2: some verbs take -ing, some take to + verb. You need to learn the lists below.

A helpful tip: to often looks forward to something not done yet (want to, plan to, hope to). -ing often talks about the activity itself (enjoy, finish, avoid).

Rule 3: a few verbs take both, but the meaning changes: stop, remember, forget, try, regret.

Learn these lists

Verb + -ing Verb + to + verb
enjoy, finish, avoid, mind, suggest, keep, consider, practise, miss, imagine, admit, deny, can't stand, don't mind, give up, look forward to want, need, decide, hope, plan, promise, refuse, agree, manage, afford, offer, learn, seem, expect, would like, would love

Same verb, different meaning

Verb + -ing + to + verb
stop I stopped smoking. (I don't smoke now.) I stopped to buy water. (I stopped walking so I could buy water.)
remember I remember meeting him. (a memory of the past) Remember to call Mum. (don't forget, in the future)
forget I'll never forget seeing the sea for the first time. (a memory) I forgot to lock the door. (I didn't do it.)
try Try restarting the computer. (an experiment to fix a problem) I tried to open the jar. (I made an effort; it was difficult.)

Watch out

  • I enjoy to swim. I enjoy swimming.
  • I'm interested in learn English. I'm interested in learning English.
  • She suggested to go out. She suggested going out.
  • I look forward to hear from you. I look forward to hearing from you. (Here to is a preposition.)
  • I want going home. I want to go home.
