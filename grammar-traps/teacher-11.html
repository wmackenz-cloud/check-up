Teacher Notes 11: Negative Infinitives (not to + verb)

Open the student worksheet

Why students get this wrong

Students build the negative the way they build every other negative in English: with don't. That gives to don't go and advised me don't eat. Spanish, Portuguese and Arabic speakers also carry over no before the verb, which gives no to go or to no go. Speaking fluency is the second problem: even students who know the rule pause to build the phrase, so the drills here focus on saying not to as one fast chunk.

How to explain it

Say: "An infinitive is to + verb. It has no helper verb, so there is no place for don't. To make it negative, put not in front of the whole thing: not to go."

Test question: Where is not? Did I add don't by mistake?

Contrast pair: "Don't eat!" (an order) and "The doctor told me not to eat it." (a report of the order). This is the bridge from imperatives the student already knows.

If the student asks...

  • "I hear native speakers say to not go. Is it wrong?" No. It's called a split infinitive and it's common in speech. Accept it in conversation. For writing and exams, not to go is the safer form.
  • "Can I say I want not to go?" It's grammatical but sounds unnatural. With want, need, like and have to, English makes the main verb negative: I don't want to go. Teach it as a fixed exception.
  • "What's the difference between not to and so as not to?" So as not to (or in order not to) gives a purpose: I whispered so as not to wake the baby. Plain not to follows a verb or adjective: I decided not to go.

Running the speaking drills

The goal is speed, not perfection. Keep cues short and the rhythm steady. Fix errors with a quick recast (you say it correctly, the student repeats) rather than an explanation.

Exercise notes and answers with reasons

Exercise 1: Right or wrong?

Correct: not to go, not to worry, so as not to miss, not to be late, not to forget.

Wrong: to don't go (no don't in an infinitive), no to eat (no should be not), to no speak (wrong word and wrong order), don't to call (don't again), not forget to (to in the wrong place).

Exercise 2: Make it negative

  1. advised me not to eat fast food.
  2. promised my teacher not to forget my homework again.
  3. chose not to attend: the reason (it wasn't mandatory) now makes sense.
  4. warns visitors not to feed the animals.
  5. important not to text while driving.
  6. reminded me not to be late.

Exercise 3: Fill in the blanks

  1. not to spill: try + not to.
  2. not to worry: tell someone + not to.
  3. not to be: "not to be late" is the natural phrase.
  4. not to wake: so as not to shows purpose.
  5. not to laugh: it was hard + not to.

Exercise 4: Find and correct the mistake

  1. decided not to buy: no don't in an infinitive.
  2. crucial not to touch: no becomes not.
  3. asked the students not to speak: to no becomes not to.
  4. try not to eat: to was missing.
  5. agreed not to tell: accept to not tell in conversation; not to tell is the exam form.

Exercise 5: About you

Answers will vary. Samples: My dad always told me not to give up too quickly. This year I've decided not to check my phone in bed. Employees are asked not to eat at their desks. I'd warn visitors not to drive downtown at rush hour.
